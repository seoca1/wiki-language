# English Modal Verbs — 조동사 (can/could/may/might/will/would/shall/should/must)

**Type:** grammar
**Level:** B1-B2 / CEFR
**Date Added:** 2026-08-11

## Overview

English modal auxiliaries are a closed class of nine core verbs (can/could/may/might/will/would/shall/should/must) that combine with a bare infinitive to express modality — ability, permission, possibility, intention, obligation, advice, and deduction. They are **defective verbs**: no -s ending, no past participle, no infinitive form, and no do-support in negation/question. Modals operate in a present/past pair system (can/could, will/would) and pair with **function** (permission/ability/obligation/advice) rather than strict tense.

## Key Points

- **Form rule**: modal + **bare infinitive** ("He can swim" — NOT "He can to swim"). Negation = modal + not + V ("cannot / can't", "must not / mustn't").
- **can / could**: ability or permission. "I can swim." / "Could I borrow your pen?" (polite request).
- **may / might**: formal permission or epistemic possibility. "May I leave early?" / "It might rain."
- **will / would**: future prediction or volition. "It will rain tomorrow." / "Would you like tea?" (polite offer).
- **shall**: formal future or suggestion (mostly BrE). "Shall we dance?" / legal contracts: "The buyer shall pay..."
- **should / must**: advice vs. strong obligation. "You should see a doctor." / "You must wear a seatbelt."
- **Past form = past or hypothetical**: "could" = past ability ("I could swim at age 5") OR present hypothetical ("I could help if you asked"). Same for "would".
- **No -s on 3rd person singular**: "She can drive" (NOT "cans"). "He will arrive" (NOT "wills").
- **No do-support in questions**: "Can you help?" (NOT "Do you can help?"). "Should I stay?" (NOT "Do I should stay?").

## Cross-language Connections

- **스페인어**: 스페인어는 동사 시제 + **접속법 (subjuntivo)** 으로 양태 표현 — *puedo, podría, debo, debería* 가 can/could/should/must 에 대응. 영어 조동사 1 어절 vs 스페인어 동사 굴절.
- **일본어**: 일본어 양태는 **문장 끝 조사 + 助動詞** — "〜できる/〜られる" (can), "〜だろう/〜かもしれない" (will/might), "〜べきだ/〜なければならない" (should/must). 영어와 기능 매핑은 유사하지만 형태가 다르다.
- **한국어**: 한국어 양태는 **어미** — "-(으)ㄹ 수 있다" (can), "-(으)ㄹ 것이다" (will), "-(으)면 되다" (could), "-아/어 하다/해야 하다" (should/must). 한국어 어미 1 종 vs 영어 조동사 9 종.
- **중국어**: 중국어는 양태 부사 + 能/会/可以/要/得/应该/可能 — 能/会 ≈ can, 要/得 ≈ must, 应该 ≈ should, 可能 ≈ may/might. 영어 조동사보다 분산.

## Examples

«I can speak three languages.» — 능력 (현재) — 3 개 국어를 할 수 있다.

«Could you open the window, please?» — 공손한 요청 — 창문 좀 열어 주실래요?

«It might snow tonight.» — 추측 (불확실) — 오늘 밤 눈이 올 수도 있다.

«You should see a doctor.» — 조언 — 의사를 봐야 한다.

«Students must wear uniforms.» — 강한 의무 — 학생들은 교복을 입어야 한다.

«Would you like some coffee?» — 공손한 제안 / 의지 — 커피 한 잔 드시겠어요?

«May I have your name, please?» — 정중한 허가 요청 — 성함을 알려 주시겠어요?
