# pretty

**Part of Speech:** adjective
**Level:** A1

**Definition:** Pleasant to look at; attractive (often for women, things, or situations).

**Pronunciation:**
- IPA: /ˈprɪti/
- Syllables: pret-ty (2 syllables)
- Stress: 1st syllable (PRET-ty)

**Memory Tip:** "**Pret**ty" sounds like "pretty" — both are pleasant! Or: pretty = pre + tty → a "pre-tty" (good-looking) person.

**Common Mistakes:**
- ❌ "pretty girl" to a stranger → can feel like catcalling
- ❌ "prettier" / "prettiest" — both correct (comparative/superlative)
- ❌ "more pretty" → "prettier" (one-syllable → -er)
- ✅ "That's pretty." (general positive — not just appearance)
- ✅ "Pretty good" / "Pretty nice" (intensifier, common in casual English)
- Note: For men use [[handsome]], for universal "attractive" use "good-looking"

**Register:** universal but informal. Use sparingly for strangers.
**Frequency:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (top 500 English words)

## Examples

- "What a pretty dress!" — 정말 예쁜 드레스다!
- "She's so pretty." — 그녀는 너무 예뻐.
- "Pretty good, thanks." — 꽤 좋아, 고마워.
- "The view is pretty." — 경치가 꽤 좋다.
- "Not pretty ugly" → avoid this double negative; use "not pretty" or "kind of ugly"

## Mini-Dialogue

```
A: Wow, your hair looks pretty today!   ← 와, 오늘 머리 진짜 예쁘다!
B: Thanks! I got it cut yesterday.   ← 고마워! 어제 잘랐어.
A: It suits you.   ← 어울린다.
B: Thank you, that's sweet.   ← 고마워, 그거 좋다.
```

## Cultural Notes

- **Compliment culture**: Western culture values specific compliments ("That dress really brings out your eyes!") over generic ("You're pretty").
- **Avoid**: Don't use for strangers — can feel like harassment.
- **Self-deprecation**: When complimented, many Westerners respond with "Oh, this old thing?" — modest deflection.
- **In Spanish**: "linda/bonita" (women), "guapo" (men). In Korean: "예쁜" (women), "잘생긴" (men). In Japanese: "可愛い (kawaii)" or "綺麗 (kirei)" (women), "ハンサム/かっこいい" (men).
