# family

**Part of Speech:** noun
**Level:** A1

**Definition:** A group of people related by blood, marriage, or adoption; one's immediate relatives.

**Pronunciation:**
- IPA: /ˈfæm(ə)li/
- Syllables: fam-i-ly (3 syllables)
- Stress: 1st syllable (FAM-i-ly)

**Memory Tip:** "FAM-i-ly" — your "fam" (people close to you). Or: family = "familiar faces" = familiar people = family!

**Common Mistakes:**
- ❌ "a family" for one person → "a family member" (family = group)
- ❌ "familys" → "**families**" (plural)
- ❌ "Family's car" → "**The family's** car" or "Family car"
- ❌ "My family are nice" (US OK) → "My family **is** nice" (US formal)
- ✅ "I love my family." — 가족 사랑해
- ✅ "Family time" — 가족 시간
- ✅ "Family dinner" — 가족 식사
- Note: In Korean 가족 (gajok), Japanese 家族 (kazoku), Spanish familia, all related to Latin "familia".

**Register:** universal.
**Frequency:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (top 300 English words)

## Examples

- "I love my family." — 나는 내 가족을 사랑해.
- "How big is your family?" — 가족이 몇 명이에요?
- "Family comes first." — 가족이 먼저야.
- "We had a family reunion." — 가족 모임을 가졌다.
- "Extended family" — 대가족 / 친척

## Mini-Dialogue

```
A: Tell me about your family.   ← 가족에 대해 말해줘.
B: I have a mom, dad, and a sister.   ← 엄마, 아빠, 여동생이 있어.
A: Are you close?   ← 친해?
B: Yes, very. We have dinner together every Sunday.   ← 응, 매우. 매주 일요일마다 함께 저녁 먹어.
```

## Cultural Notes

- **Western concept**: Nuclear family (parents + children) is common. Extended family varies.
- **Korean 가족 (gajok)**: Often includes extended family (할머니, 할아버지, 삼촌, 이모, etc.).
- **Japanese 家族 (kazoku)**: Historically multi-generational (三代同居); modern = nuclear + nearby grandparents.
- **Spanish familia**: Very close-knit, frequent gatherings, includes godparents (padrinos).
- **Family values**: All 4 cultures emphasize respect for elders and family duty (효 in Korean, 孝 in Japanese, respeto in Spanish).
