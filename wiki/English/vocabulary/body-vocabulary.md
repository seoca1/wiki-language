## Head & Face

### head

**Part of Speech:** noun

**Definition:** the upper part of the body, containing the brain, eyes, ears, nose, and mouth

**Pronunciation / IPA:** /hɛd/

**Etymology:** Old English "heafod", from Proto-Germanic *haubudą. Cognate with German "Haupt".

#### Examples

- My head hurts. — 머리가 아파요.
- She nodded her head. — 그녀가 고개를 끄덕였다.

#### Related Terms

- ''brain''
- ''skull''
- ''forehead''

#### Cultural Notes

In English, "head" is metonymically used for "mind" or "intelligence" (e.g., "use your head", "a head for heights"). In Korean translations, this is often rendered as "머리" but the English idiom may map better to "머리를 써" (use your brain).

### face

**Part of Speech:** noun

**Definition:** the front part of a person's head, from forehead to chin

**Pronunciation / IPA:** /feɪs/

**Etymology:** From Old French "face", from Latin "facies" (form, appearance, face).

#### Examples

- She has a round face. — 그녀는 둥근 얼굴을 가졌다.
- Wash your face. — 얼굴을 씻어.

#### Related Terms

- ''forehead''
- ''cheek''
- ''chin''
- ''jaw''

#### Cultural Notes

In English idioms, "face" carries strong social meaning: "lose face" (체면 잃다), "save face" (체면을 살리다), "face-to-face" (직접, 대면으로). These are loan translations of East Asian concepts into English.

## Torso

### chest

**Part of Speech:** noun

**Definition:** the front surface of the upper body, from the neck to the abdomen

**Pronunciation / IPA:** /tʃɛst/

**Etymology:** Old English "cest" (box, coffin), from Proto-Germanic *kistō. Original meaning was "container"; modern anatomical sense from 16th c. Distinguish from "breast" (가슴, mammary).

#### Examples

- He has a broad chest. — 그는 가슴이 넓다.
- She has a pain in her chest. — 그녀는 가슴이 아프다.

#### Related Terms

- ''breast''
- ''rib''
- ''heart''

#### Cultural Notes

Korean "가슴" can mean both "chest" and "breast" — English distinguishes. For health/anatomy, prefer "chest". For mammary context, "breast". For emotional context (e.g., "가슴이 아프다"), "heart" or "chest" both work.
