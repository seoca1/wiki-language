### Christmas

**Part of Speech:** noun

**Definition:** 크리스마스

**Pronunciation / IPA:** /ˈkrɪsməs/

**Etymology:** From Old English *Crīstes mæsse* "Christ's mass."

#### Examples

- Merry Christmas!
- We spend Christmas with family.

#### Cultural Notes

Christmas in the US/UK: December 25, family gathering, gift exchange, Christmas tree, Santa Claus. Less religious for many families today.

### Halloween

**Part of Speech:** noun

**Definition:** 할로윈

**Pronunciation / IPA:** /ˌhæl.oʊˈiːn/

**Etymology:** From *All Hallows' Eve*.

#### Examples

- What are you wearing for Halloween?
- Halloween is October 31.

#### Cultural Notes

October 31. Trick-or-treat, costumes, jack-o-lanterns, haunted houses. Especially popular in US, Canada, UK, Ireland.

### Thanksgiving

**Part of Speech:** noun

**Definition:** 추수감사절

**Pronunciation / IPA:** /ˌθæŋksˈɡɪvɪŋ/

**Etymology:** From *thanks* + *giving*.

#### Examples

- We eat turkey on Thanksgiving.
- Thanksgiving is in November.

#### Cultural Notes

4th Thursday of November in the US, 2nd Monday of October in Canada. Traditional foods: turkey, stuffing, cranberry sauce, pumpkin pie.

### Easter

**Part of Speech:** noun

**Definition:** 부활절

**Pronunciation / IPA:** /ˈiːstər/

**Etymology:** From Old English *ēastre*, named after the spring goddess.

#### Examples

- We hide Easter eggs.
- Easter Sunday is in spring.

#### Cultural Notes

Christian celebration of Jesus's resurrection. Easter eggs (chocolate, decorated), Easter bunny, egg hunt. Sunday in March/April.

- [[holidays-and-celebrations]]
