# American Frontier History

**Overview:** The American frontier refers to the moving western edge of European-American settlement during the 17th-19th centuries. The concept of Manifest Destiny, the Louisiana Purchase (1803), the Lewis and Clark Expedition (1804-1806), the Oregon Trail, the California Gold Rush (1849), and the closing of the frontier in 1890 form the central narrative of westward expansion.

## Key Points

- **Manifest Destiny (1845)**: Coined by journalist John L. O'Sullivan in 1845, the ideology held that the United States was destined by God to expand across North America. Used to justify territorial acquisition from Mexico, native displacement, and war.
- **Louisiana Purchase 1803**: President Thomas Jefferson bought 828,000 square miles from France for $15 million, doubling U.S. size. Lewis and Clark (1804-1806) explored the new territory.
- **Oregon Trail (1840s-1860s)**: 2,170-mile route from Missouri to Oregon. ~400,000 settlers traveled it. ~10% died along the way. Replaced by the transcontinental railroad (1869) as the main migration route.
- **California Gold Rush (1849)**: Gold discovery at Sutter's Mill in January 1848 set off the 1849 Gold Rush. ~300,000 people traveled to California. San Francisco grew from a village to a major city.
- **Homestead Act 1862**: Granted 160 acres of public land to settlers who farmed it for 5 years. ~1.6 million homestead claims; ~270 million acres distributed. Concentrated in the Great Plains.
- **Frederick Jackson Turner "Frontier Thesis" (1893)**: Historian Turner argued that the closing of the frontier (declared in the 1890 Census) ended a key formative phase of American character — democracy, individualism, and self-reliance attributed to frontier experience. Influential but contested.
- **Native American Displacement**: Westward expansion came at enormous cost to Native peoples — Trail of Tears (1830s), Long Walk of the Navajo (1864), Wounded Knee Massacre (1890), boarding schools, reservation system. Many U.S. historians now center indigenous perspectives on the frontier.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국에는 미국식 "서부개척" 명절 없음. 한국 역사에서 남쪽 변방 (영남, 호남) 으로의 이주 (영남대사건) 가 비슷한 구조. 한국 영토는 작아서 Manifest Destiny 와 같은 광대함의 이주 서사가 약함.
- **Japonés**: 일본에는 "북방개척" (北海道開拓) 이 비슷한 구조. 明治 시대 (1869+) 도호쿠/홋카이도로의 이주 의식. 미국 西部 開拓 과 비슷하지만 시간차가 있고 식민주의 비판 시각이 다름.
- **Chino**: 중국 闯关东 (Chuang Guandong) — 19-20세기 화북 농민의 동북 (만주) 으로의 대규모 이주. 미국 서부개척과 비슷한 "변방 + 식민 + 개발" 의 패턴이지만 중국 내부 이주임.

## Examples

«Manifest Destiny, the belief that the United States was destined to expand across North America, was used to justify westward expansion in the 19th century.»

북미 대륙 전체로 확장하는 것이 미국의 운명이라는 신념이었던 Manifest Destiny 는 19세기 서부 확장을 정당화하는 데 사용되었습니다.

«Roughly 400,000 settlers traveled the Oregon Trail between the 1840s and 1860s, seeking farmland and new opportunities in the Pacific Northwest.»

1840년대에서 1860년대 사이에 약 40만명의 정착민들이 오리건 트레일을 따라 태평양 북서부의 농경지와 새로운 기회를 찾아 여행했습니다.

«Historian Frederick Jackson Turner argued in 1893 that the closing of the American frontier marked the end of a key formative era in American democracy and individualism.»

역사가 Frederick Jackson Turner 는 1893년에 미국 frontier 의 종식이 미국 민주주의와 개인주의의 핵심 형성 시대의 끝을 알렸다고 주장했습니다.
