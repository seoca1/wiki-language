# American Food Culture: Thanksgiving and Regional Cuisines

**Overview:** American cuisine is a melting pot of immigrant traditions, with regional specialties reflecting historical settlement patterns and local ingredients.

## Key Points

- **Thanksgiving Dinner (4th Thursday of November)**:
  - **Turkey (七面鳥)**: Centerpiece — often 12-20 lbs, stuffed with bread stuffing
  - **Mashed Potatoes (マッシュポテト)**: With gravy
  - **Stuffing (スタッフィング)**: Bread-based, with herbs (sage, thyme)
  - **Cranberry Sauce (クランベリーソース)**: Sweet-tart condiment
  - **Sweet Potato Casserole (スイートポテト)**: Often with marshmallows on top
  - **Pumpkin Pie (パンプキンパイ)**: Traditional dessert — Thanksgiving-specific
  - **Gravy (グレイビー)**: From turkey drippings
  - **Mac and Cheese (マックアンドチーズ)**: Especially Southern
- **Regional Cuisines**:
  - **Southern (BBQ, Cajun/Creole, Low Country)**: BBQ sauces (vinegar-based NC, mustard SC, tomato-based KC, sweet Memphis), gumbo, jambalaya, shrimp & grits
  - **Tex-Mex (Texas, Southwest)**: Chili con carne, fajitas, breakfast tacos
  - **New England (Lobster, Clam Chowder)**: Boston cream pie, lobster rolls
  - **Midwest (Chicago, Detroit)**: Deep-dish pizza, coney dogs
  - **California Cuisine**: Fusion, farm-to-table
  - **Pacific Northwest**: Salmon, coffee culture (Seattle/Portland)
- **Fast Food Culture**:
  - McDonald's, Burger King, Wendy's, Taco Bell
  - Drive-thru (order without leaving car)
- **Casseroles and Comfort Food**: Mac and cheese, pot pie, meatloaf
- **Regional BBQ Differences**:
  - **Texas**: Beef brisket, dry rub
  - **Memphis**: Pork ribs, dry rub OR wet sauce
  - **Kansas City**: Burnt ends, sweet thick sauce
  - **North Carolina**: Whole hog, vinegar-based sauce
  - **South Carolina**: Mustard-based yellow sauce
