# American Workplace Culture: Open Door and Small Talk

**Overview:** American workplace culture values direct communication, individualism, and casual relationship-building through open-door policies and informal small talk.

## Key Points

- **Open Door Policy**: Managers keep office doors open — signaling approachability. "My door is always open" = invite for questions/discussion.
- **First Names**: American workplaces use first names quickly, even for bosses. "Hi, Sarah" vs. "Director Smith." Titles used sparingly.
- **Small Talk**: Essential to American workplace culture. Topics:
  - Weather ("Beautiful day, isn't it?")
  - Weekend plans ("How was your weekend?")
  - Sports (American football, baseball — "Did you see the game?")
  - Family/kids (carefully — avoid controversial topics)
- **Topics to Avoid**: Religion, politics, money/salary, controversial social issues.
- **Networking (business cards era → LinkedIn)**:
  - **Small talk** before business
  - **Follow-up email** within 24 hours
  - **LinkedIn** — primary professional network
- **Feedback Culture**:
  - "Feedback is a gift" — common HR phrase
  - **360-degree reviews**: Feedback from peers, subordinates, supervisors
  - **Performance Improvement Plans (PIP)**: Formal warning for underperformers
- **Work Hours**:
  - 9-5 standard (often 9-6)
  - "Working from home" (WFH) common since 2020
  - **Vacation/PTO** (Paid Time Off): 10-20 days/year standard
  - **At-will employment**: Can be fired without cause (most states)
- **Office Hierarchies**:
  - **C-Suite**: CEO, CFO, COO, CTO, CMO
  - **VP (Vice President)**
  - **Director**
  - **Manager**
  - **Individual Contributor (IC)**
