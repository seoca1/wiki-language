# American Communication: Direct Style and Small Talk

**Overview:** American communication is generally direct, low-context, and emphasizes clarity, with small talk as a key social lubricant.

## Key Points

- **Low-Context Communication**: Direct and explicit. "Say what you mean" — Edward T. Hall's cultural framework.
- **Politeness Strategies**:
  - **Direct with softeners**: "Could you possibly..." / "Would you mind..."
  - **"I think" / "I feel"** — Personal opinion framing
  - **"Let's"** — Inclusive team language
- **Small Talk Topics**:
  - **Weather**: Universal ice-breaker
  - **Sports**: NFL, NBA, MLB — "How about those [team]?"
  - **Weekend plans**: "Any big plans for the weekend?"
  - **Family/children**: Safe if positive
- **Topics to Avoid**:
  - Politics (especially election season)
  - Religion
  - Salary/money specifics
  - Age, weight
  - Politics of foreign countries
- **Conflict Resolution**: Direct — "Let's address the elephant in the room." Avoid avoiding conflict.
- **Greeting Styles**:
  - "Hi!" / "Hey!" (informal)
  - "Hello" (formal, on phone)
  - Handshakes (business)
  - Hugs (close friends, sometimes)
- **Email/Slack Culture**:
  - "Thanks!" (closing)
  - "Looping in" (cc'ing)
  - "Per my last email" (slightly passive-aggressive)
  - "Friendly reminder" (often considered aggressive)
  - Emojis in work messages (acceptable in some teams)
- **"Have a Nice Day" Culture**: Politeness norms — service industry, customer service, retail.
