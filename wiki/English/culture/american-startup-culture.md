# American Startup Culture

**Overview:** American startup culture, centered on Silicon Valley in the San Francisco Bay Area, has spread globally through technology entrepreneurship. Defined by venture capital, equity compensation, "move fast and break things" philosophy, and the pursuit of billion-dollar "unicorn" valuations, it shapes how many Americans think about work, identity, and success.

## Key Points

- **Silicon Valley Origins**: The term "Silicon Valley" was coined in 1971 (Don Hoefler) for the semiconductor cluster south of San Francisco. Hewlett-Packard (founded 1939 in a Palo Alto garage) is the iconic predecessor. Stanford University ties (Dean of Engineering Frederick Terman) seeded the regional ecosystem.
- **"Move Fast and Break Things"**: Facebook's internal motto (Mark Zuckerberg, 2009-era). The phrase captured the broader tech philosophy that rapid iteration trumps caution. Critics have pushed back ("move fast with stable infra") and Facebook moved on from the slogan in 2014.
- **Unicorn Culture**: A "unicorn" is a privately-held startup valued at over $1 billion. The term was coined by Aileen Lee in 2013 (TechCrunch). By 2024, 1,200+ unicorns exist globally, ~half in the U.S. Examples: SpaceX, Stripe, Canva, ByteDance (China).
- **Hustle Culture**: Long working hours (60-80 hour weeks), "rise and grind," sleep deprivation as a badge, weekend work, founder martyrdom. The 2010s saw pushback — "quiet quitting" (2022 TikTok trend), four-day work week trials, and burnout awareness.
- **Venture Capital System**: U.S. venture capital raised $200+ billion annually in 2021-2022. Major firms: Sequoia Capital, Andreessen Horowitz, Kleiner Perkins, Accel. Term sheets, dilution, board seats, and "exit" (IPO or acquisition) define the VC-startup relationship.
- **Equity Compensation**: Stock options and Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) make tech employees potentially wealthy if the company succeeds. Many compensation packages are 50%+ equity. This is unusual outside the U.S.
- **Failure Tolerance**: Bankruptcy is acceptable — failed founders often raise again ("unfair advantage"). "Fail fast" and "pivot" are accepted. Bankruptcy is not publicly stigmatized the way it is in some cultures.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국 스타트업 문화 — 2010년대 이후 강남/판교 중심. 카카오 (Kakao), 토스 (Toss), 당근마켓 (Daangn) 등. 미국보다 보수적이고 VC 시장 작음. 한국만의 "삼성 같은 대기업 취업 선호" 가 강한 저항.
- **Japonés**: 일본 スタートアップ — 2015년 이후 政府 지원 강화. SmartHR, freee 등. 미국보다 보수적, "오래 다니는 대기업" 문화가 여전히 강함. 최근 "Japan startup ecosystem" 으로 글로벌 진출 시도.
- **Chino**: 중국 스타트업 — BAT (Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent) 의 996 근무 (朝9晚9周六倍, 오전 9시-오후 9시 주 6일) 문화는 미국보다 훨씬 강함. 미국 startup culture 의 수입이지만 더 가혹한 버전. 2021년 이후 정부 규제로 변화.

## Examples

«Silicon Valley in northern California is home to most of the world's largest technology companies, including Apple, Google, Meta, and Tesla.»

캘리포니아 북부의 실리콘밸리는 Apple, Google, Meta, Tesla 를 포함하여 세계에서 가장 큰 기술 회사들의 본거지입니다.

«The phrase "move fast and break things" became Facebook's internal motto during its early years, encouraging engineers to iterate quickly.»

"move fast and break things" 라는 문구는 초기 몇 년 동안 Facebook 의 내부 모토가 되어, 엔지니어들이 빠르게 반복 실험하도록 장려했습니다.

«A unicorn is a privately-held startup company valued at over one billion dollars, a term coined in 2013 that reflects the rapid growth of tech valuations.»

Unicorn 은 2013년에 만들어진 용어로, 사적으로 보유한 스타트업 회사로 10억 달러 이상의 가치를 가진 회사이며, 이는 기술 가치의 급속한 성장을 반영합니다.
