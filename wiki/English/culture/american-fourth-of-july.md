# American Fourth of July

**Overview:** American Independence Day (July 4) commemorates the adoption of the Declaration of Independence on July 4, 1776, when the thirteen colonies declared independence from Great Britain. It is a federal holiday celebrated with fireworks, parades, barbecues, and patriotic displays across the country.

## Key Points

- **1776 Declaration of Independence**: Drafted primarily by Thomas Jefferson, the document was adopted by the Second Continental Congress on July 4, 1776 (actually signed by most signers in August 1776). Independence was formally declared on July 2 — but July 4 stuck.
- **First Fireworks (1777)**: Philadelphia held the first organized fireworks display on July 4, 1777, just one year after independence. By the 1830s, fireworks were widespread. Today, fireworks are responsible for about 18,000 fires and 9,000 ER visits each year.
- **Patriotic Symbols**:
  - **American Flag (Stars and Stripes)**: 13 stripes (original colonies) + 50 stars (states), last updated July 4, 1960 after Hawaii became a state.
  - **Bald Eagle**: National bird since 1782.
  - **Uncle Sam**: Personification of U.S. government, originated in War of 1812.
  - **Liberty Bell**: Symbol of independence, located in Philadelphia.
- **Barbecue Tradition**: July 4 is one of the top grilling days in America alongside Memorial Day and Labor Day. Hot dogs are iconic (consumed by the millions), along with hamburgers, ribs, corn on the cob, and watermelon.
- **Parades and Concerts**: Small-town parades (with veterans, marching bands, beauty queens) are a hallmark. Boston Pops holds a famous outdoor concert on the Esplanade with fireworks finale. Many cities stage large public fireworks displays.
- **"Nathan's Hot Dog Eating Contest"** (Coney Island, NYC, since 1916): Annual contest on July 4 with competitive eaters.
- **Weather**: July 4 lands in peak summer — typical activities include pool parties, beach trips, and outdoor concerts. Heat and humidity are common in most regions.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국의 광복절 (光復節, 8월 15일) — 일제 강점기에서 해방 1945. 미국 7월 4일 과 같은 "독립/해방" 기념일이지만 군사 퍼레이드 위주. 한국의 여름 명절은 더 다양 (단오, 추석).
- **Japonés**: 일본 建国記念の日 (2월 11일) — 신화적 건국일. 미국 7월 4일 과 다른 "건국신화" 기반 명절이지만 같은 국가 기원일. 일본은 fireworks 를 여름 (花火大会) 에 주로 함.
- **Chino**: 중국에는 미국식 독립기념일 없음. 国庆节 (10월 1일) — 1949 년 중화인민공화국 건국. 미국 7월 4일 과 같은 "국가 수립" 명절이지만 BBQ/폭죽 문화는 다름. 중국 청도 (靑島) 등지에서 폭죽 허용.

## Examples

«On July 4, 1776, the Second Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence, announcing that the thirteen American colonies were free from British rule.»

1776년 7월 4일, 제2차 대륙회의는 독립선언을 채택하여 13개 아메리카 식민지가 영국의 통치에서 벗어났음을 선언했습니다.

«Philadelphia held the first organized fireworks display to celebrate Independence Day in 1777, just one year after the Declaration of Independence.»

필라델피아는 독립선언 채택 이듬해인 1777년에 독립기념일을 기념하는 최초의 조직화된 폭죽 공연을 개최했습니다.

«Many families celebrate the Fourth of July with a backyard barbecue, eating hot dogs and hamburgers while watching fireworks in the evening.»

많은 가족들이 뒷마당 바비큐로 7월 4일을 기념하며, 저녁에 폭죽을 보면서 핫도그와 햄버거를 먹습니다.
