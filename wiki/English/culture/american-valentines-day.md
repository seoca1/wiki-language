# American Valentine's Day

**Overview:** American Valentine's Day (February 14) is a holiday centered on romantic love, expressed through greeting cards, flowers (especially red roses), chocolates, jewelry, and candlelit dinners. Originally a Christian saint's feast day, it became a major commercial holiday in the 19th and 20th centuries.

## Key Points

- **Saint Valentine Origin**: February 14 was the feast day of Saint Valentine, a Roman Christian martyr (3rd century). Multiple early Christian Valentines existed; details are historically unclear. The romantic association grew gradually through medieval and Renaissance literature.
- **1840s-1860s Commercial Rise**: Esther A. Howland of Worcester, Massachusetts began selling mass-produced "valentines" (greeting cards) in the 1840s, creating one of America's first major greeting card industries. By the 1860s, valentines were widely exchanged.
- **Greeting Card Dominance**: Americans exchange ~145 million Valentine's cards annually, second only to Christmas cards. Hallmark (founded 1910) and other card companies dominate the market. "Valentine's" without an "s" is the more common American spelling.
- **Chocolate and Candy Tradition**: The "conversation heart" candy (NECCO, introduced 1901) and chocolate boxes (Whitman's Sampler, since 1912) are iconic. Americans spend ~$2 billion on Valentine's candy each year.
- **Red Roses**: Red roses symbolize love. ~250 million roses are produced for Valentine's Day, with most imported from Colombia and Ecuador in winter months.
- **School Classroom Parties**: Children exchange valentine cards (often with cartoon characters, animals, or generic "be mine" messages) with classmates. Teachers often organize card-making crafts and a small party.
- **Friendship vs Romantic Variation**: "Galentine's Day" (Feb 13, popularized by Parks and Recreation TV show in 2010) celebrates female friendships. Many schools have expanded the card exchange to include all classmates, not just romantic interests.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국에는 2월 14일 발렌타인데이가 있음 (남자가 여자에게 초콜릿/꽃 선물) + 3월 14일 화이트데이 (여자가 남자에게 사탕 선물) + 4월 14일 블랙데이 (싱글자 파스타 먹기). 미국 Valentine's Day 의 비대칭화된 버전. 일본 영향.
- **Japonés**: 일본 Valentine's Day 도 2월 14일 (여자가 남자에게) + 3월 14일 화이트데이. 한국과 같은 비대칭 명절 구조. 일본 발렌타인 시장 규모는 전 세계 1-2위. 미국 Valentine's Day 의 본고장 보다는 수입/재창조.
- **Chino**: 중국에는 발렌타인데이가 명절이 아님. 七夕节 (음력 7월 7일, "중국 발렌타인") 이 가깝지만 중국 고유의 견우직녀 전승 기반. 2월 14일은 도시 젊은층의 소비 이벤트로만 존재.
