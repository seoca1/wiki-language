# American Easter Traditions

**Overview:** American Easter (a Sunday in March or April) is a religious Christian holiday celebrating the resurrection of Jesus, layered with secular springtime traditions — egg hunts, the Easter Bunny, candy, and family meals. Date varies each year; roughly 85% of American adults identify as Christian historically, though active observance varies.

## Key Points

- **Religious Foundation**: Christians attend sunrise services, called "Easter sunrise services," and traditional church services. Many churches hold special music (Handel's Messiah selections), baptism ceremonies, and Easter lilies decorate sanctuaries.
- **Easter Eggs (Pagan Roots)**: Decorated eggs predate Christianity. The egg symbolizes spring fertility, rebirth, and new life across many ancient cultures (Egyptian, Persian, Greek). Christians adopted the egg as a Jesus-tomb-resurrection symbol.
- **Easter Bunny (German Origin)**: The "Osterhase" (Easter hare) was a 17th-century German tradition brought to Pennsylvania by German immigrants in the 1700s. The hare was believed to lay eggs for good children. It spread nationwide in the 19th century.
- **Easter Egg Hunt**: Parents hide plastic eggs filled with candy or coins; children find them in yards, parks, and church grounds. The White House Easter Egg Roll (since 1878) is a famous annual event on the Monday after Easter.
- **Candy and Chocolate**: Americans spend over $3 billion on Easter candy. Cadbury Creme Eggs, Reese's Peanut Butter Eggs, Peeps (marshmallow chicks, made by Just Born since 1953) are iconic. Chocolate bunnies and jelly beans dominate.
- **Easter Sunday Attire**: Traditionally, churchgoers wear new clothing, often a new hat ("Easter bonnet"). Spring pastels (yellow, pink, lavender) are common.
- **Holy Week**: The week before Easter (Palm Sunday, Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, Holy Saturday) is observed by many churches. Good Friday is not a federal holiday but is observed by some businesses closing early.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국은 기독교 인구의 상당수가 부활절 (復活節) 을 예배로 지키지만, 일반 명절은 아님. 추석, 설날, 추석이 가족 명절. 미국 Easter 와 같은 부활절이지만 큰 명절이 아님.
- **Japonés**: 일본에는 기독교가 소수이므로 부활절이 명절이 아님. 봄 명절은 3-4월의 花見 (Ohanami, 벚꽃) 와 5월 5일 어린이날. 부활절 토끼는 페르시안과 독일 Osterhase 에서 유래한 비-기독교 풍습.
- **Chino**: 중국은 공식 부활절 명절 없음. 춘절 (春節), 청명절 (清明節, 4월 4-6일), 단오절 (端午節) 이 봄 명절. 부활절 토끼와 달토끼 (玉兔) 의 모티프는 비슷하지만 기원 다름.

«Children wake up on Easter morning to find that the Easter Bunny has left them a basket filled with chocolate eggs, jelly beans, and candy.»

아이들은 부활절 아침에 일어나 Easter Bunny 가 초콜릿 달걀, 젤리빈, 사탕으로 채운 바구니를 남겨둔 것을 발견합니다.

«The tradition of the Easter Bunny originated in 17th-century Germany and was brought to the United States by German immigrants in Pennsylvania.»

Easter Bunny 의 전통은 17세기 독일에서 시작되어 펜실베이니아로 이민 온 독일인들에 의해 미국으로 전해졌습니다.
