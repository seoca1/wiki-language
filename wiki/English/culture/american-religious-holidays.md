# American Religious & Federal Holidays

**Overview:** American holidays blend Christian heritage with secular federal observances. Thanksgiving and the Fourth of July are uniquely American; Christmas and Easter are religious holidays that have become mostly secular family events; Halloween has shifted almost entirely into a costume-and-candy tradition. Federal holidays like Memorial Day and MLK Day create three-day weekends that shape the whole calendar.

## Key Points

- **Thanksgiving (4th Thursday of November)**: Federal holiday since 1863 (Lincoln). Family dinner centered on turkey, stuffing, and pumpkin pie. Traces to a 1621 Pilgrim harvest meal shared with the Wampanoag. Black Friday shopping kicks off the next day.
- **Christmas (December 25)**: Religious origin (birth of Jesus) but widely celebrated as a secular family holiday. Santa Claus, gifts, Christmas tree, dinner. "Merry Christmas" vs "Happy Holidays" reflects ongoing cultural tension between religious and inclusive language.
- **Easter (March/April, movable)**: Sunday determined by the first full moon after the spring equinox. Religious (resurrection) but also secular: Easter Bunny, egg hunts, candy. Ham or lamb is traditional.
- **Halloween (October 31)**: Originally "All Hallows' Eve," now almost entirely secular. Costumes, trick-or-treat, jack-o'-lanterns, haunted houses. Americans spend billions of dollars yearly, the highest per-capita in the world.
- **Memorial Day (last Monday of May)**: Federal holiday since 1971 (originally 1868). Honors military members who died in service. Unofficial start of summer; pools open, beaches fill.
- **MLK Day (3rd Monday of January)**: Federal holiday since 1986 honoring Martin Luther King Jr., born January 15, 1929. A day of service; some states combine it with civil rights observances.
- **Independence Day / 4th of July**: Federal holiday since 1870. Fireworks, BBQ, parades, patriotic displays. Most purely "American" holiday, dating from the 1776 Declaration of Independence.
- **Religious vs secular split**: Pew data shows ~65% of Americans identify as Christian, but participation in religious services has dropped sharply. Most public-holiday rituals are now family- and food-centered rather than church-centered.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국은 추석 (Chuseok) 과 설날 (Seollal) 이 명절, 가족 + 음식 + 조상 제례 중심. 미국 추수감사절 (Thanksgiving) 과 가장 유사한 구조. 한국에는 4th of July 같은 국가 창설 기념일은 없음.
- **Japonés**: 일본 お正月 (New Year) 이 1월 1-3일 가족 명절, 오세치 료리 (お節料理) 먹는 풍습은 미국 추수감사절 과 구조가 비슷. 일본 建国記念日 (2월 11일) 은 미국 7월 4일 의 일본 버전이지만 화려하지 않음.
- **Chino**: 중국 春节 (춘절, Lunar New Year) 가 가장 큰 명절, 가족 + 음식 + 세뱃돈. 미국 추수감사절 보다 규모가 크고 음력 기반. 미국 독립기념일 같은 solar calendar 국가 기념일은 중국国庆节 (10월 1일) 이 가장 가까움.
- **Español**: 스페인 Todos los Santos (11월 1일) 과 Día de los Muertos (멕시코 11월 2일) 가 미국 할로윈과 시기적으로 인접하지만 의미는 다름 (영령 추모 vs 코스튬/사탕). 멕시코 독립기념일 (9월 16일) 는 미국 7월 4일 과 비슷한 국가 정체성 행사.

## Examples

«On Thanksgiving, most families gather around a table with a big turkey, stuffing, and pumpkin pie.» (추수감사절에 대부분의 가족은 큰 칠면조, stuffing, 호박 파이로 식탁에 모인다.)

«"Merry Christmas" and "Happy Holidays" are both used in December, and people sometimes argue about which is more appropriate.» (12월에는 "Merry Christmas" 와 "Happy Holidays" 둘 다 쓰이며, 어느 쪽이 더 적절한지에 대해 사람들이争论하곤 한다.)

«Memorial Day weekend is the unofficial start of summer; the pools open and everyone heads to the beach.» (메모리얼 데이 연휴는 사실상 여름의 시작이다. 수영장이 문을 열고 사람들이 해변으로 향한다.)
