# American 1960s Counterculture

**Overview:** The American counterculture of the 1960s was a youth-driven social movement rejecting mainstream Cold War values of conformity, militarism, and consumerism. Centered on anti-Vietnam War protests, civil rights, sexual liberation, psychedelic drugs, and back-to-the-land communalism, the counterculture peaked in 1967 (Summer of Love) and crashed by 1970 amid political backlash and the Manson murders.

## Key Points

- **Vietnam War Protests**: The U.S. troop deployment to Vietnam grew from 16,000 advisors (1963) to over 500,000 by 1968. Anti-war protests grew on campuses. The Tet Offensive (January 1968) shifted public opinion. Draft resistance surged — over 200,000 men dodged or refused the draft.
- **Free Speech Movement (1964-1965)**: UC Berkeley students protested restrictions on political organizing on campus, founding modern student activism. Mario Savio's "Bodies Upon the Gears" speech became iconic.
- **Civil Rights Backlash and Beyond**: Many white youth radicalized through civil rights activism. The Students for a Democratic Society (SDS, founded 1960) and later the Black Panther Party (1966) channeled this energy. The 1968 DNC Chicago protests were violently suppressed by police.
- **Summer of Love 1967**: ~100,000 young people, especially from San Francisco's Haight-Ashbury neighborhood, gathered for "flower power," psychedelic rock, free love, and anti-war activism. The "human be-in" at Golden Gate Park drew 20,000+.
- **Psychedelic Drugs**: LSD, popularized by Timothy Leary ("Turn on, tune in, drop out," 1966), was central to the experience. Leary's dismissal from Harvard (1963) made him a counterculture icon. Federal LSD ban in 1966.
- **Music as Organizing Force**: Folk (Bob Dylan, Joan Baez, Phil Ochs), psychedelic rock (Grateful Dead, Jefferson Airplane, Jimi Hendrix), and protest music reached mass audiences via the Monterey Pop Festival (1967) and Woodstock (1969, ~400,000 attendees).
- **Woodstock (August 15-18, 1969)**: Music festival on a dairy farm in Bethel, New York. ~400,000 attendees. Defined the generation. Drug use, mud, rain, and "three days of peace and music" became mythology. Altamont Free Concert (December 1969) ended the era after a Hells Angels killing during the Rolling Stones' set.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국 1980년대 대학생 민주화 운동 (학생 운동) — 6월 항쟁 (1987) 등. 미국 1960년대 학생운동과 같은 "대학 + 저항" 패턴이지만 한국은 군사독재와의 투쟁, 미국은 베트남 전쟁 저항.
- **Japonés**: 일본 1960년대 安保闘争 (Anpo tōsō) — 1960년 미일안보条约 갱신 반대. 베트남 파병 반대 (1970 Okinawan protest). 미국 1960년대 counterculture 와 비슷한 "전쟁 저항 + 학생 운동" 구조이지만 hippie 문화는 약함.
- **Chino**: 중국 文化大革命 (1966-1976) — Mao Zedong 주도 청년 운동. 미국 1960년대 counterculture 와 같은 "청년 + 반체제" 의 외형이지만 중국은 Mao 의 권력 투쟁으로 변질. 미국 hippie 문화는 中공정권 비판 대상.

## Examples

«The Summer of Love in 1967 drew roughly 100,000 young people to San Francisco's Haight-Ashbury neighborhood to celebrate peace, music, and psychedelic drugs.»

1967년의 Summer of Love 는 약 10만명의 젊은이들을 평화, 음악, 그리고 환각 물질을 축하하기 위해 샌프란시스코의 헤이트-애시버리 동네로 끌어모았습니다.

«Woodstock, held in August 1969 on a New York dairy farm, attracted approximately 400,000 people and became the defining music festival of the counterculture generation.»

1969년 8월 뉴욕의 낙농장에서 열린 Woodstock 는 약 40만명이 참가했으며, counterculture 세대의 결정적 음악 축제가 되었습니다.

«Opposition to the Vietnam War intensified in the late 1960s, with hundreds of thousands of young Americans dodging the draft or participating in anti-war protests.»

베트남 전쟁에 대한 반대는 1960년대 후반에 격화되어, 수십만명의 미국 젊은이들이 draft 를 회피하거나 반전 시위에 참여했습니다.
