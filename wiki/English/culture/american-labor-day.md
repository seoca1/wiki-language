# American Labor Day

**Overview:** American Labor Day is a federal holiday on the first Monday of September, honoring the American labor movement and the contributions of workers. It also unofficially marks the end of summer, with back-to-school events, last beach trips, and a "no more white clothes" fashion rule historically.

## Key Points

- **1894 Federal Holiday**: President Grover Cleveland signed Labor Day into federal law on June 28, 1894, shortly after the Pullman Strike (May-July 1894) — a railroad workers' strike violently suppressed by federal troops. The law was partly a reconciliation gesture.
- **Workers' Rights Origin**: The holiday was pushed by labor unions to recognize the social and economic achievements of American workers. The first Labor Day celebration was in New York City on September 5, 1882, organized by the Central Labor Union.
- **Date Significance (May vs. September)**: Unlike May Day (International Workers' Day on May 1, tied to the 1886 Haymarket affair in Chicago), the U.S. chose September to avoid association with the more radical labor movements of Europe and to distance itself from the Haymarket memory.
- **End of Summer Marker**: Labor Day weekend is the last long weekend before fall. Schools traditionally start right after Labor Day. Many pools close. "End of summer BBQ" is a popular ritual.
- **No White After Labor Day**: Old (now mostly abandoned) fashion rule historically held that white clothing should not be worn after Labor Day. Originated with 19th-century upper-class etiquette.
- **Last Major Sporting Event**: NFL season opens the week after Labor Day. College football kicks off. The Southern 500 NASCAR race (Darlington) is held on Labor Day weekend.
- **Retail Sales**: Once a major sale event (pre-Internet), Labor Day has been eclipsed by Memorial Day and July 4 sales in the modern retail calendar.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국의 노동절 (5월 1일) — 국제노동자협정일. 미국 Labor Day 와 같은 "노동자 + 5월 1일" 명절이지만 한국은 봄에, 미국은 가을에 지킴. 한국의 경우 임시공휴일 지정으로 연휴 발생.
- **Japonés**: 일본 11월 23일 勤労感謝の日 (근로감사의 날) — 가을 노동 의례. 미국 Labor Day 와 비슷한 "근로자 + 가을" 구조. 일본에는 5월 1일 메이데이 (May Day) 와 9월 칠석 (Tanabata 관련) 등 다양한 노동/감사 명절.
- **Chino**: 중국 5월 1일 劳动节 (노동절) — 국제노동자협회 기념 + 봄 황금위크 (五一黄金周) 의 시발점. 미국 Labor Day 와 같은 "노동" 명절이지만 5월 (봄) 명절이고 가족 여행이 핵심.

## Examples

«Labor Day is observed on the first Monday of September and honors the social and economic achievements of American workers.»

Labor Day 는 9월 첫 번째 주 월요일에 기념되며, 미국 노동자들의 사회적, 경제적 성취를 기립니다.

«President Grover Cleveland made Labor Day a federal holiday in 1894, partly as a way to recognize the labor movement after the violent Pullman Strike.»

Grover Cleveland 대통령은 1894년에 폭력적인 Pullman 파업 이후 노동운동을 인정하는 의미로 Labor Day 를 연방 공휴일로 지정했습니다.

«Many Americans consider Labor Day weekend the unofficial end of summer, with the last beach trips, final barbecues, and the start of a new school year.»

많은 미국인들은 Labor Day 주말을 여름의 비공식적인 끝으로 여기며, 마지막 해변 여행과 바비큐, 새 학기 시작과 함께 보냅니다.
