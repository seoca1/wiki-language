# American Food History

**Overview:** American cuisine developed through layers of immigration, regional adaptation, and indigenous influence. European settlers, enslaved Africans, Latin American migrants, and Asian immigrants each contributed dishes and techniques, producing a national food identity that is regional, hybrid, and constantly reinventing itself.

## Key Points

- **Indigenous roots**: Pre-Columbian staples include corn (maize), beans, squash, turkey, and chili peppers. Many "American" foods (cornbread, succotash, pemmican, maple syrup) descend directly from Native American cooking.
- **Thanksgiving origins**: A 1621 harvest meal in Plymouth Colony shared between English Pilgrims and the Wampanoag people. Mythologized as the "first Thanksgiving," though similar harvest feasts had occurred earlier in English and Native traditions.
- **Colonial era (1600s-1700s)**: English, Dutch, German, and Scots-Irish settlers brought stews, puddings, pies, sausages, and bread baking. Regional ingredients (New England cod, Southern pork) shaped local dishes.
- **Enslaved African contributions**: Soul food in the American South — collard greens, black-eyed peas, fried chicken, mac and cheese, cornbread, gumbo — draws directly on West and Central African cooking, adapted to Southern ingredients.
- **Immigration waves (1800s-1900s)**: Italian (pizza, pasta), Jewish (bagels, deli sandwiches), Chinese (chop suey, then Cantonese restaurant food), Irish (corned beef), German (sausage, beer) all became mainstream American food.
- **Regional cuisines**:
  - **Tex-Mex**: Texas-Mexican border cooking. Chili con carne, fajitas, breakfast tacos, queso. Distinct from interior Mexican food.
  - **Cajun / Creole**: Louisiana. French and Spanish influence with African and Caribbean layers. Gumbo, jambalaya, étouffée, beignets.
  - **New England**: Clam chowder (especially Boston's cream-based version), lobster rolls, baked beans, Boston cream pie.
  - **California**: Fusion, fresh produce, farm-to-table movement (1970s+).
- **Fast food as global export**: McDonald's, KFC, and Burger King, all founded in the US (1940s-1950s), reshaped global eating habits and became symbols of American culture worldwide.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국 음식은 미국보다 강한 국가 정체성 (김치, 된장찌개, 불고기) 을 가지지만, 1980년대 이후 미국식 빵, 피자, 햄버거가 일상화. 한국에는 강한 regional food (전주 한정판, 부산 어묵) 가 있고, 미국 BBQ 와 비슷한 패턴.
- **Japonés**: 일본 洋食 (yōshoku, Western-style food) 는 메이지 시대 이후 미국/유럽 요리를 일본식으로 변형 — 카레라이스, 돈까스, 햄버거. 미국이 세계로 수출한 햄버거는 일본에서 완전히 재창조됨. 일본 ラーメン 도 중국에서 수입 후 일본화.
- **Chino**: 중국 음식은 미국에서 "Chinese food" 라는 범주로 뭉뚱그려지지만, 실제로 사천, 광둥, 상하이는 완전히 다름. 중국 요리사는 19세기 미국에 이민해 철도 노동자 식사를 준비했고, chop suey 가 미국에서 발명된 것으로 알려져 있음.
- **Español**: 스페인 요리 (paella, tapas, jamón) 는 강한 국가 정체성을 유지. 멕시코 요리는 스페인 식민지 영향 + 토착 원주민 영향 (corn, beans, chili), 미국 Tex-Mex 의 원형. 푸에르토리코 요리 (mofongo, arroz con habichuelas) 도 미국 내에서 중요한 지역 음식.
