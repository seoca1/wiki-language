# American Urban Renewal

**Overview:** Urban renewal refers to mid-20th-century U.S. government programs that demolished low-income and minority neighborhoods to build highways, public housing, commercial developments, and civic projects. Often justified as "slum clearance," these programs frequently displaced poor and Black residents while failing to deliver promised benefits. The legacy continues to shape debates about gentrification, displacement, and housing policy.

## Key Points

- **1949 Housing Act**: Federal legislation funding "slum clearance" and urban renewal. Worked alongside FHA mortgage redlining (refusing loans in Black neighborhoods) to reshape American cities. Title I funded local authorities to acquire and clear land.
- **Robert Moses in NYC**: From the 1930s-1960s, Moses reshaped New York City with highways (Cross Bronx Expressway), bridges, public housing (Towers in the Park), and parks (Jones Beach). His projects demolished neighborhoods and displaced ~500,000 people. Robert Caro wrote the influential biography "The Power Broker" (1974).
- **Cross-Bronx Expressway (1948-1964)**: Built through the South Bronx, the expressway displaced thousands of residents. Combined with 1970s arson and disinvestment, it contributed to the South Bronx's decline.
- **Detroit Decline**: Detroit peaked at 1.85 million residents in 1950. The city's population fell to under 640,000 by 2020. White flight, automotive industry decline, 1967 riots, and failed urban renewal all contributed. Major urban renewal failure.
- **Public Housing (Towers in the Park)**: Pruitt-Igoe (St. Louis, 1954) became the most famous public housing failure. Designed by modernist Yamasaki Leinweber, the 33-building complex was demolished in 1972 after becoming a symbol of concentrated poverty and crime.
- **Gentrification Debates**: From the 1990s-2020s, gentrification (wealthier residents moving into previously disinvested neighborhoods, often displacing poorer residents) became a major debate. Examples: Brooklyn's Williamsburg, San Francisco's Mission District, DC's U Street.
- **Modern Reforms**: New Urbanism, transit-oriented development, inclusionary zoning, and community land trusts emerged as partial responses. Programs like NYC's "City of Yes" zoning reform (2024) attempt to address housing shortages.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국 도시정비 (都市整備) / 재개발 — 1960-80년대 서울/부산 빈민굴 (달동네) 헐기 + 아파트 신축. 일본의 도심 재개발과 비슷하지만 한국은 강제 이주 + 보상 부족 비판. 미국 urban renewal 의 동아시아 버전.
- **Japonés**: 일본 도심 재개발 — 1970-2000년대 마루노우치 (丸の内) 등 도심 재생. 일본은 이주보다 재건축 중심. 미국 urban renewal 처럼 대규모 헐기보다 단계적 재생. 일본 제2차 세계대전 후 도시계획.
- **Chino**: 중국 도시화 — 1990-2010년대 농촌 → 도시 대규모 이주 + 도심 재개발. 北京 旧城区 (구성도) 헐기 + 胡同 (후퉁) 보호 충돌. 중국 urban renewal 은 스케일이 미국보다 훨씬 크고 강제적.

## Examples

«Robert Moses reshaped New York City from the 1930s through the 1960s, building highways, bridges, and housing projects that displaced hundreds of thousands of residents.»

Robert Moses 는 1930년대부터 1960년대까지 뉴욕시를 재편성하여, 수십만명의 주민을 이주시킨 고속도로, 다리, 주택 프로젝트를 건설했습니다.

«Detroit's population fell from a peak of 1.85 million in 1950 to under 640,000 in 2020, partly due to white flight, deindustrialization, and failed urban renewal programs.»

디트로이트의 인구는 1950년 최고치 185만명에서 2020년 64만명 미만으로 감소했는데, 이는 백인 이탈, 탈산업화, 그리고 실패한 도시 재개발 프로그램에 부분적으로 기인합니다.

«Gentrification in cities like San Francisco, Brooklyn, and Washington D.C. has displaced long-time residents as wealthier newcomers moved into previously disinvested neighborhoods.»

샌프란시스코, 브루클린, 워싱턴 D.C. 같은 도시의 젠트리피케이션은 이전에 투자가 부족했던 동네에 더 부유한 신참자들이 이사하면서 장기 거주자들을 이주시켰습니다.
