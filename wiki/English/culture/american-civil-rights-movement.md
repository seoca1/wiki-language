# American Civil Rights Movement

**Overview:** The American Civil Rights Movement (roughly 1954-1968) was a mass mobilization of Black Americans and allies challenging racial segregation, disenfranchisement, and violence in the Jim Crow South. The movement produced landmark legislation (Civil Rights Act of 1964, Voting Rights Act of 1965) and reshaped American law, politics, and culture.

## Key Points

- **Background: Jim Crow South**: From the 1870s through the 1960s, Southern states enforced racial segregation in schools, public facilities, transportation, and voting (poll taxes, literacy tests). Black Americans faced systemic violence (lynching) and economic exclusion.
- **Brown v. Board of Education (1954)**: Supreme Court unanimously ruled that racial segregation in public schools was unconstitutional. The case, argued by Thurgood Marshall, overturned the "separate but equal" doctrine from Plessy v. Ferguson (1896).
- **Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955-1956)**: Rosa Parks's arrest (December 1, 1955) for refusing to give up her bus seat sparked a 381-day boycott. Martin Luther King Jr. emerged as a national leader. The Supreme Court ruled bus segregation unconstitutional.
- **Greensboro Sit-Ins (1960)**: Four Black college students sat at a segregated Woolworth's lunch counter in North Carolina, sparking a wave of nonviolent sit-ins. The Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC) formed from these efforts.
- **March on Washington (August 28, 1963)**: 250,000 people gathered at the Lincoln Memorial. King's "I Have a Dream" speech defined the movement. The march helped pass the Civil Rights Act of 1964.
- **Civil Rights Act of 1964**: Banned segregation in public accommodations and employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin. Endorsed by President Lyndon B. Johnson.
- **Voting Rights Act of 1965**: Outlawed discriminatory voting practices. Within five years, Black voter registration in the Deep South rose from 23% to 61%. Later weakened by Shelby County v. Holder (2013).
- **Assassinations**: Martin Luther King Jr. (April 4, 1968) and Robert F. Kennedy (June 5, 1968) were assassinated. Riots erupted in over 100 U.S. cities after King's death.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국 5·18 민주화 운동 (1980, 광주) — 군부 독재에 대한 시민 저항. 미국 시민권운동과 같은 비폭력 시민 저항이지만 미국은 "인종 평등", 한국은 "민주주의" 가 핵심. 한국 진보운동의 중요한 레퍼런스.
- **Japonés**: 일본에는 미국식 시민권운동 없음. 部落問題 (Buraku mondai) — 차별받는 소수민족 문제는 비슷하지만 광범위한 시민 운동으로는 전개되지 않음. 미국 시민권운동은 1960년대 일본 지식인에게 큰 영향.
- **Chino**: 중국에는 미국식 시민권운동 없음. 중화민국 (대만) 시절 228 사건 (1947) 이 비슷하지만 국제적 주목을 받지 못함. 중국 본토의 反右 (1957) 운동은 비판 박살.

## Examples

«Rosa Parks's arrest in Montgomery, Alabama in 1955 sparked a 381-day bus boycott that brought Martin Luther King Jr. to national prominence.»

1955년 앨라배마 몽고메리에서 Rosa Parks 의 체포는 381일 동안 계속된 버스 보이콧을 촉발시켰고, 이는 Martin Luther King Jr. 를 전국적 저명 인물로 이끌었습니다.

«The Voting Rights Act of 1965 outlawed discriminatory voting practices such as literacy tests, dramatically increasing Black voter registration in the Southern United States.»

1965년의 Voting Rights Act 는 문해력 시험과 같은 차별적 투표 관행을 금지하여, 미국 남부에서 흑인 유권자 등록률을 크게 증가시켰습니다.

«The March on Washington in August 1963 drew 250,000 people to the Lincoln Memorial, where Martin Luther King Jr. delivered his "I Have a Dream" speech.»

1963년 8월 워싱턴 행진은 25만명을 링컨 기념관으로 끌어모았고, Martin Luther King Jr. 는 그곳에서 "I Have a Dream" 연설을 했습니다.
