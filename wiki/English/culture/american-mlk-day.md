# American Martin Luther King Jr. Day

**Overview:** Martin Luther King Jr. Day (third Monday of January) is a federal holiday honoring the birthday of civil rights leader Martin Luther King Jr. (January 15, 1929 — assassinated April 4, 1968). Signed into federal law in 1983 and first observed as a federal holiday in 1986, it is the only U.S. federal holiday commemorating a person who was not a president.

## Key Points

- **1983 Federal Holiday**: President Ronald Reagan signed the King Holiday Bill on November 2, 1983. The first federal observance was January 20, 1986. The holiday is observed on the third Monday of January, near King's January 15 birthday.
- **Civil Rights Legacy**: King led the Montgomery bus boycott (1955-1956), helped found the Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC, 1957), led the March on Washington (1963), and won the Nobel Peace Prize (1964). His "I Have a Dream" speech and "Letter from Birmingham Jail" are foundational American texts.
- **"Day of Service, Not a Day Off"**: The King Center and Corporation for National and Community Service promote MLK Day as a day of community service. AmeriCorps holds annual service projects. Many schools and companies organize volunteer activities.
- **Slow State Adoption**: Despite federal recognition, some states resisted. South Carolina was the last state to recognize the holiday (in 2000) as a paid holiday for state employees, following a 1999 NASCAR boycott controversy.
- **Coined with "MLK Day"**: Combines the civil rights leader's initials with the day abbreviation, distinct from generic federal holidays.
- **"I Have a Dream" Speech Anniversary**: The 1963 March on Washington, where King delivered the speech at the Lincoln Memorial, drew 250,000 attendees and helped build momentum for the Civil Rights Act of 1964.
- **Southern States and "King-Lee Day"**: In Arkansas, Alabama, and Mississippi (until 2000), the holiday was combined with Robert E. Lee Day as "King-Lee Day," which civil rights advocates criticized as minimizing King's legacy.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국은 5월 1일 노동절 (Labor Day) 과 8월 15일 광복절이 가장 가까운 명절. 한국의 현충일 (6월 6일) 이 추모의 역할. 미국 MLK Day 와 같은 시민영웅 추모 명절이 없음. 최근 5·18 민주화 운동 기념일 (5월 18일, 1997년 지정) 이 가장 비슷한 시민의례.
- **Japonés**: 일본 2월 11일 建國記念の日 (건국기념일) 는 신화적 건국일. 미국 MLK Day 처럼 특정 인물 추모는 11월 3일 文化の日 (Bunka no Hi) 가 비슷한 구조. 일본은 개인 인물 추모가 명절이 되는 경우가 거의 없음.
- **Chino**: 중국은 4월 4-5일 청명 (清明) 이 추모 명절이지만 미국 MLK Day 처럼 시민 영웅 추모가 아님. 9월 10일 教师节 (스승의 날) 이 가장 가까운 인물 추모일. 미국식 시민운동 기념일은 없음.

«Martin Luther King Jr. Day, observed on the third Monday of January, honors the civil rights leader who advocated for racial equality through nonviolent protest.»

«King's "I Have a Dream" speech, delivered at the March on Washington in 1963, has become one of the most famous speeches in American history.»

1963년 워싱턴 행진에서 행해진 King 의 "I Have a Dream" 연설은 미국 역사에서 가장 유명한 연설 중 하나가 되었습니다.

«Many Americans honor MLK Day by participating in community service projects rather than treating the day as simply a day off work.»

많은 미국인들은 MLK Day 를 단순한 휴무일이 아닌 지역 봉사 활동에 참여함으로써 기념합니다.
