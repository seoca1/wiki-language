# American Civil War Legacy

**Overview:** The American Civil War (1861-1865) was the defining crisis of U.S. history — a four-year conflict between the Union (north) and Confederacy (south) over slavery, states' rights, and economic structure. The war's legacy shapes American politics, regional identity, race relations, and contested historical memory to this day.

## Key Points

- **1861-1865 Conflict**: 11 Southern states seceded to form the Confederate States of America. The war was triggered by secession following Lincoln's 1860 election. 620,000-750,000 soldiers died — more than all other U.S. wars combined until Vietnam.
- **Slavery as Root Cause**: While the Confederacy cited states' rights, the war's central cause was slavery. Confederate Vice President Alexander Stephens explicitly cited slavery in his 1861 "Cornerstone Speech." Emancipation Proclamation (1863) reframed the war as a fight against slavery.
- **Reconstruction (1865-1877)**: Postwar period of federal occupation of the South, Black political participation, and constitutional amendments (13th, 14th, 15th). Reconstruction ended with the Compromise of 1877 and federal troop withdrawal.
- **North-South Reconciliation Mythology**: The "Lost Cause" narrative, promoted by Southern writers like Edward Pollard and the United Daughters of the Confederacy (UDC), reframed the South's defeat as noble defense of states' rights rather than slavery. Famously adopted in D.W. Griffith's "Birth of a Nation" (1915).
- **Confederate Flag Controversies**: The Confederate battle flag, raised over state capitols in the mid-20th century during civil rights opposition, has been the subject of recurring controversy. South Carolina removed it from the State House in 2015 after the Charleston church shooting.
- **Civil War Battlefields as Memorials**: Gettysburg, Antietam, Vicksburg, and other sites are preserved as national military parks (since 1890s). They serve as both memorials and contested historical sites.
- **Modern Political Lines**: Regional political polarization (Republican dominance in the South) traces partly to Civil War-era alliances. The 2020s have seen renewed debates over monument removal and "critical race theory" in schools.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국에는 미국 남북전쟁에 해당하는 native 내전 없음. 한국전쟁 (1950-1953) 이 분단/통일 갈등으로 더 가까움. 6·25 전쟁 이후 가족 분단과 이념 갈등이 한국 사회에 지속.
- **Japonés**: 일본 西南戦争 (1877, 사쓰마 반란) 이 미국 남북전쟁 처럼 정부 vs 옛 봉건 세력의 충돌이지만 스케일이 훨씬 작음. 일본은 내전 패배자 문화의 공명대가 약함.
- **Chino**: 중국 國共內戰 (국공내전, 1927-1949) — 국민당 (KMT) vs 공산당 (CPC) 의 분단전쟁. 미국 남북전쟁과 같이 분할 → 재통일 패턴이지만 중국은 사회주의 혁명으로 귀결. 현재 대만/중국 관계가 잔존 갈등.

## Examples

«The American Civil War, fought from 1861 to 1865, resulted in the deaths of more than 600,000 soldiers and ended with the defeat of the Confederate States of America.»

1861년부터 1865년까지 벌어진 미국 남북전쟁은 60만 명 이상의 군인 사망자를 냈으며, 남부 연합의 패배로 끝났습니다.

«The "Lost Cause" narrative, popular in the late 19th century, romanticized the South's defeat as a noble defense of states' rights while minimizing the role of slavery.»

19세기 후반에 유행한 "Lost Cause" 서사는 남부의 패배를 노예제의 역할을 최소화하면서 states' rights 의 고귀한 수호로 미화했습니다.

«The Confederate battle flag was removed from the South Carolina State House grounds in 2015, fifteen years after it had been moved to a monument on the Capitol grounds.»

남부연합 전투기는 2015년에 사우스캐롤라이나 주 의사당 광장에서 제거되었는데, 이는 주 의사당 광장 기념물로 옮겨진 지 15년 만이었습니다.
