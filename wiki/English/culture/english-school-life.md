# English-Speaking School Culture

**Type:** culture, education, social-norms
**Region:** US / UK / Canada / Australia / NZ

## Overview

English-speaking school culture emphasizes individualism, Socratic discussion, extracurricular activities, and preparing for college admission. The US system differs significantly from UK (more formal), Canadian (similar to US), and Australian/NZ (more casual, less competitive). This page complements `[[english-dating-culture]]`, `[[english-workplace-culture]]`, and `[[american-cultural-values]]`.

## High School Experience

### American High School

- **Sports culture**: Football (fall), basketball (winter), baseball/track (spring)
- **Prom** (Junior + Senior)
- **Homecoming** (fall)
- **Graduation** (ceremony + parties)
- **Yearbook**
- **Clubs**: 50+ typical (chess club, robotics, drama, etc.)
- **Lunch**: 30 min, cafeteria or off-campus
- **AP (Advanced Placement) courses**: College credit possible
- **SAT/ACT**: Junior year (optional but important)
- **Extracurriculars**: Critical for college applications
- **Spirit week, homecoming, prom**: Major events

### British High School

- **Sixth Form College** (Y12-Y13): Specialized A-Levels
- **A-Levels**: Subject-specific exams (3-4 subjects)
- **UCAS application**: For university admission
- **House system**: Common (Harry Potter-esque)
- **School uniform**: Standard (formal)
- **Sports**: Rugby, cricket, football (soccer)
- **Formal dinners, speech days**: Major events

### Canadian High School

- Similar to US system
- **Provincial variations** (Ontario, Quebec, BC, Alberta differ)
- **Quebec**: French-language schools (CÉGEP)
- **Sports**: Hockey (Canada's national sport!)
- **Co-op programs**: Work-integrated learning
- **Provincial exams** (varies)

### Australian High School

- **Less competitive** than US/Asia
- **ATAR**: University admission (state-specific)
- **Year 12 final exams**: Major event
- **Schoolies week**: Post-grad celebration
- **Sports**: AFL, cricket, rugby, NRL
- **Uniforms**: Standard (often distinctive colors)
- **Casual, friendly culture**: Less hierarchical than UK

## College/University Culture

### American College

- **Dorm life**: Freshman dorms, Greek life
- **Majors**: Declared sophomore year
- **Gen-Ed requirements**: First 2 years
- **Semester system**: Fall/Spring (+ optional Summer)
- **Tuition**: Very high ($30-70K/year private)
- **Sports culture**: NCAA, football Saturdays
- **Greek life (fraternities/sororities)**: Common but controversial

### British University

- **Halls of residence**: First year accommodation
- **Subject-focused**: Apply directly to course
- **3-year undergraduate** (typical, vs. US 4-year)
- **Tutorial system**: Oxford/Cambridge famous
- **Term system**: Michaelmas, Hilary, Trinity (Oxford)
- **Pub culture**: Central to social life
- **Tuition**: £9,250/year (capped for UK students)

### Canadian University

- Similar to US system but cheaper
- **Co-op programs**: Common (work + study)
- **U15 (top universities)**: Toronto, UBC, McGill, etc.
- **French immersion** (Quebec)
- **Undergraduate 4 years** (most programs)

### Australian University

- **3-year undergraduate** (typical)
- **Sandstone universities**: Oldest, prestigious (Melbourne, Sydney)
- **Group of Eight**: Top research universities
- **ATAR-based admission**: Similar to UK
- **Residential colleges**: On-campus accommodation
- **Pub culture**: Very strong (uni bars)
- **Casual, outdoorsy culture**: Less formal than UK

## Cultural Tips for Visitors

✅ **Ask about school culture** — Will engage in conversation
✅ **Participate in student activities** — Sports, clubs
✅ **Use "How was school?"** as small talk
✅ **Respect homework culture** — Children are busy
✅ **Try cafeteria food** — Cultural experience
✅ **Attend sports events** — Social bonding

❌ **Don't criticize US testing culture** — Sensitive topic
❌ **Don't ask about college debt** — Common pain point
❌ **Don't assume all schools are same** — US vs UK very different
❌ **Don't ignore dress codes** — Some schools strict
❌ **Don't skip graduation ceremonies** — Important family event
