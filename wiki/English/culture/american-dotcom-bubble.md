# American Dot-Com Bubble

**Overview:** The dot-com bubble (1995-2000) was a speculative frenzy in internet-related stocks that saw the NASDAQ composite index rise from ~1,000 in 1995 to a peak of 5,048 on March 10, 2000, before crashing to 1,114 by October 2002. The boom was fueled by venture capital, retail investor enthusiasm, and widespread belief that internet business models had disrupted traditional economics.

## Key Points

- **1995-2000 Boom**: Venture capital investment in internet startups exploded. The Netscape IPO (August 1995) is often cited as the symbolic start. By 2000, hundreds of internet companies had gone public, many with no profits.
- **pets.com Sock Puppet**: The most iconic failure. pets.com (founded 1998, IPO February 2000) raised $82.5 million. The sock puppet mascot became famous. The company shut down November 2000 after burning through cash. Its ad campaign ("Because pets can't drive") became the era's symbol.
- **NASDAQ Peak 2000**: Reached 5,048.62 on March 10, 2000, the peak of a 5-year run-up from 751.89 in 1995. ~$5 trillion in market value was erased over the next two years.
- **Major Failures**: Webvan (online grocery, $800M raised, bankrupt 2001), eToys (online toy retailer, bankrupt 2001), Kozmo.com (one-hour delivery, shut 2001), Boo.com (fashion e-commerce, shut 2000 within 18 months).
- **Survivors**: Amazon (stock fell from $107 to $7 in 2001, survived), eBay, Priceline, Yahoo (eventually acquired by Verizon 2017), Google (still private in 2001, IPO 2004).
- **Bubble Mechanics**: "Eyeballs" (page views) and "mindshare" replaced revenue and profit. "Get big fast" was the dominant strategy. Press coverage (CNBC, Red Herring, Business 2.0, Industry Standard) hyped unprofitable companies.
- **Bust and Aftermath**: Industry Standard magazine shut down (2001). Many venture capital firms collapsed. The 2001 recession coincided with the 9/11 attacks. By 2002, the NASDAQ had lost 78% of its value from peak. Lessons influenced subsequent boom-bust cycles (cryptocurrency, NFTs, AI 2020s).

## Cross-language Connections

- **Coreano**: 한국 닷컴 버블 (2000-2001) — 한컴 (Hangame), 인터파크 (Interpark), 드림위즈 (Dreamwiz) 등. 미국 닷컴 버블과 같은 시기 + "eyeball" 전략 + 2000-01 붕괴. 한국도 2001년 닷컴 기업 대량 퇴출. 차이점: 한국 닷컴은 게임/포털 중심.
- **Japonés**: 일본 IT バブル (1999-2000) — 일본판 닷컴 버블. 광섬유 투자 과잉 + 닷컴 기업 흥망. 소프트뱅크 (SoftBank) 가 가장 큰 사례. 미국 닷컴과 같은 시기 + 붕괴이지만 일본은 失われた10年 (잃어버린 10년) 의 일부.
- **Chino**: 중국 닷컴 버블 — 2000년 NASDAQ 동조 후 2001-2002 큰 폭락. BAT (Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent) 의 핵심 시기. 미국 닷컴 이후 2005-2010 모바일 인터넷으로 중국이 재도약.

## Examples

«The NASDAQ composite index reached its peak of 5,048 on March 10, 2000, before losing roughly 78% of its value over the following two years.»

NASDAQ 종합지수는 2000년 3월 10일에 최고치 5,048에 도달한 후, 이후 2년 동안 약 78%의 가치를 잃었습니다.

«pets.com, known for its sock puppet mascot, raised $82.5 million in its IPO in February 2000 but shut down nine months later after burning through its cash.»

양말 인형 마스코트로 유명했던 pets.com 은 2000년 2월 IPO에서 8,250만 달러를 모금했지만, 9개월 후 현금을 모두 소진하고 문을 닫았습니다.

«Amazon survived the dot-com bust by continuing to invest in long-term infrastructure, even as its stock price fell from $107 to $7 between 2000 and 2001.»

Amazon 은 2000년에서 2001년 사이에 주가가 $107에서 $7로 떨어졌지만 장기 인프라에 지속적으로 투자하면서 닷컴 버블의 붕괴를 살아남았습니다.
