# Expressions: Slang & Colloquial (English)

> **Theme:** Slang & Colloquial
> **Level:** B2-C2
> **Standard:** US/UK Internet + Gen Z slang

English slang/colloquial — Gen Z, internet culture, modern US/UK usage.

## Awesome

**Part of Speech:** expression (admiration)
**Definition:** "Awesome" — positive exclamation.

### Usage

Most common positive reaction.

### Variations

- **Awesome** — "Awesome"
- **That's awesome!** — "That's awesome!"
- **Awesome sauce** — "Awesome" (humorous)
- **Awe** — "Cool" (texting)

### Cultural Notes

- **Usage**: Universally understood (US/UK/AU)
- **Alternatives**: Cool, amazing, fantastic, brilliant (UK), rad (older)
- **Overuse**: Somewhat generic — varies for stronger emphasis
- **Sarcasm**: "Oh awesome" = not actually awesome

## You got this!

**Part of Speech:** expression (encouragement)
**Definition:** "You got this!" — encouragement.

### Usage

Before challenges, tests, presentations.

### Variations

- **You got this!** — "You can do it!"
- **Go for it!** — "Try it!"
- **You've got this in the bag** — "Definitely"
- **Knock 'em dead!** — "Do great!" (performances)

### Cultural Notes

- **Universal encouragement**: Common US/UK/AU
- **Sports**: "You've got this!" before big games
- **Work**: "You've got this presentation" before meetings
- **Friends**: Common supportive phrase
- **"Good luck!"**: Older expression (still common)

- `[[emotions-reactions]]`

## LOL / LMAO

**Part of Speech:** expression (text)
**Definition:** "Laugh out loud / Laughing my ass off" — internet laugh.

### Usage

Texting, social media.

### Variations

- **LOL** — "Laugh out loud"
- **LMAO** — "Laughing my ass off"
- **ROFL** — "Rolling on floor laughing"
- **Dead** — "I'm dead (laughing)"

### Cultural Notes

- **Generation gap**: Younger = literal laugh, older = habitual (not actual laugh)
- **Softener**: "Oh that's funny" without LOL = can sound rude
- **Emoji alternatives**: 😂 🤣 💀 (skull = dead)
- **Spelling**: Sometimes "lawl" (intentional misspelling for casual)

### Sources

- `[[emotions-reactions]]`

## Ghosting

**Part of Speech:** expression (modern)
**Definition:** "Ghosting" — suddenly cutting off all communication.

### Usage

Dating, friendships, professional.

### Variations

- **Ghosting** — "Suddenly disappearing"
- **He ghosted me** — "He disappeared"
- **Left on read** — "Read but didn't reply"
- **Breadcrumbing** — "Occasional low-effort contact"

### Cultural Notes

- **Modern dating phenomenon**: Common since ~2015
- **Reasons**: Avoidance, fear, lost interest
- **Better than ghosting**: Honest "I'm not interested" message
- **Workplace**: Less common but happens (job offers withdrawn)
- **Friendships**: Increasingly common (less socially acceptable)

### Sources

- `[[dating-romance]]`

## Spill the tea

**Part of Speech:** expression (slang)
**Definition:** "Spill the tea" — share gossip.

### Usage

Casual conversation, social media.

### Variations

- **Spill the tea** — "Share the gossip"
- **What's the tea?** — "What's the news?"
- **Tea?** — "Gossip?"
- **Give me the tea** — "Tell me everything"

### Cultural Notes

- **Origin**: Black/LGBTQ+ community, drag culture
- **Mainstream**: Gen Z adopted widely (TikTok)
- **"No tea no shade"**: "No offense, but..."
- **"Hot tea"**: Spicy/intense gossip
- **Variation**: "Spill" = tell, "sip" = hint

- `[[emotions-reactions]]`

## Burnout

**Part of Speech:** expression (modern)
**Definition:** "Burnout" — work/stress exhaustion.

### Usage

Workplace, mental health context.

### Variations

- **Burnout** — "Exhaustion from stress"
- **I'm burned out** — "I'm exhausted"
- **Career burnout** — "Job exhaustion"
- **Compassion fatigue** — "Caretaker exhaustion"

### Cultural Notes

- **WHO recognition**: Burnout is now officially recognized syndrome
- **Symptoms**: Exhaustion, cynicism, reduced productivity
- **Solutions**: Therapy, time off, career change, work boundaries
- **"Quiet quitting"**: Doing minimum, avoiding burnout
- **"The Great Resignation"**: 2021-22 mass resignations (burnout-driven)

### Sources

- `[[emotions-reactions]]`

## Adulting

**Part of Speech:** expression (slang)
**Definition:** "Adulting" — doing grown-up tasks.

### Usage

Millennial/Gen Z humor.

### Variations

- **Adulting** — "Being an adult"
- **Adulting is hard** — "Being grown-up is tough"
- **I can't even adult today** — "Can't function"
- **#adulting** — Social media tag

### Cultural Notes

- **Humor**: Light self-deprecation about responsibilities
- **Tasks**: Bills, laundry, taxes, groceries, cooking
- **Millennial generation**: Coined ~2010s
- **Memetic**: "Adulting wins" (small accomplishments celebrated)
- **Critique**: Reflects economic anxiety (housing costs, student debt)

### Sources

- `[[daily-life]]`

## Bro / Dude

**Part of Speech:** expression (address)
**Definition:** "Bro / Dude" — casual male address.

### Usage

Informal, friendly contexts.

### Variations

- **Bro** — "Brother/buddy"
- **Dude** — "Guy/person" (gender-neutral now)
- **Bruh** — "Bro" (AAVE)
- **Fam** — "Family/close friends"

### Cultural Notes

- **"Dude"**: Originally US surfer slang (1960s), now gender-neutral
- **"Bro"**: Originally male-only, now broader usage
- **Younger usage**: Gen Z = any friend (regardless of gender)
- **Setting**: Casual only (don't use in professional emails)
- **Cultural sensitivity**: Some find it exclusionary (esp. in workplace)
