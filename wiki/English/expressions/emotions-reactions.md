# emotions-reactions (English) expressions

> **Theme:** Surprise / delight / dismay interjections
> **Level:** A1-B2

**Overview:** English emotional interjections span casual exclamations ("Wow!", "Oh no!") to more elaborate reactions ("I can't believe it!", "Are you serious?"). Tone carries more weight than vocabulary — intonation conveys whether the speaker is delighted, horrified, or sarcastic.

---

## Wow!

**Literal Translation:** Same.

**Meaning:** Universal surprise / admiration exclamation. Positively inflected.

**Usage Context:** Casual. Friends, family, social media.

**Pattern:** Wow + (optional continuation).

### Examples

- Wow, that's amazing! — Admiration
- Wow, look at that view! — Aesthetic reaction
- Wow, really? — Surprise

### Cultural Background

"Wow" is mild and universally safe. For stronger reactions: "Oh my God!", "No way!", "Are you kidding me?"

## Oh my God! / OMG

**Literal Translation:** Same.

**Meaning:** Strong exclamation of surprise, delight, or dismay. The most versatile emotional interjection.

**Usage Context:** Casual. Friends, social media, sometimes exclamations in formal contexts (less appropriate).

**Pattern:** Oh my God! + (continuation or standalone).

### Examples

- Oh my God, I can't believe it! — Strong surprise
- Oh my God, that's hilarious! — Delight
- OMG, this is so good! — Text/informal

### Cultural Background

Religious-origin exclamation, but in modern usage neutral. Less intense alternative: "Oh my goodness!" Conservative alternative: "Oh my!"

- [[emotions-reactions]] — Wow!
- [[emotions-reactions]] — I can't believe it!

## That's amazing!

**Literal Translation:** Same.

**Meaning:** Strong positive reaction. Genuine admiration or enthusiasm.

**Usage Context:** Casual to neutral. Friends, family, professional compliments.

**Pattern:** That's + amazing!

### Examples

- That's amazing, congratulations! — Achievement
- That's amazing news! — Good news reaction
- That's an amazing idea. — Compliment

### Cultural Background

"Amazing" implies something unexpected and wonderful. Stronger than "great" or "nice." Casual: "That's awesome!"

- [[emotions-reactions]] — I can't believe it!

## How awful! / That's terrible!

**Literal Translation:** Same.

**Meaning:** Strong negative reaction to bad news. Sympathetic and emphatic.

**Usage Context:** Casual to neutral. Friends, family, news reaction.

**Pattern:** How awful! / That's terrible!

### Examples

- How awful, I'm so sorry to hear that. — Sympathy
- That's terrible news. — Reaction to bad news
- Oh no, how awful! — Combined exclamation

### Cultural Background

"How awful" is more emphatic than "That's too bad." Always pair with empathy: "I'm sorry to hear that."

## I can't believe it!

**Literal Translation:** Same.

**Meaning:** Exclamation of strong surprise — could be positive or negative depending on context.

**Usage Context:** Casual to neutral. Friends, family, news reaction.

**Pattern:** I + can't + believe + it!

### Examples

- I can't believe it, that's amazing! — Positive surprise
- I can't believe it, that's awful. — Negative surprise
- I can't believe he said that. — Disbelief

### Cultural Background

"I can't believe it" can carry any emotional charge depending on intonation and continuation. Useful for both positive and negative surprise.

## Are you serious?

**Literal Translation:** Same.

**Meaning:** Question challenging the credibility of what was just said. Can be positive (excited disbelief) or negative (skepticism).

**Usage Context:** Casual to neutral. Friends, family, news reaction.

**Pattern:** Are you + serious?

### Examples

- Are you serious? That's amazing! — Excited
- Are you serious? That's terrible. — Skeptical
- Wait, are you serious right now? — Skeptical, with "wait"

### Cultural Background

"Are you serious" asks the speaker to confirm. Positive surprise tone: rising intonation. Negative/skeptical: flat intonation.

- [[emotions-reactions]] — No way!

## No way!

**Literal Translation:** Same.

**Meaning:** Strong exclamation of disbelief or refusal.

**Usage Context:** Casual. Friends, family.

**Pattern:** No + way!

### Examples

- No way, that's amazing! — Excited disbelief
- No way, I'm not going. — Refusal
- No way! — Standalone exclamation

### Cultural Background

"No way" is highly colloquial. Slightly stronger than "really?" In formal contexts: "That's unbelievable."

## Oh no!

**Literal Translation:** Same.

**Meaning:** Exclamation of dismay, regret, or sympathy. The default negative reaction.

**Usage Context:** Casual to neutral. Universal.

**Pattern:** Oh + no!

### Examples

- Oh no, I forgot my keys. — Self-dismay
- Oh no, that's awful news. — Sympathy
- Oh no, are you okay? — Concern

### Cultural Background

"Oh no" is the most common mild negative exclamation. Universal across English dialects. Polite enough for most contexts.
