# Expressions: Polite & Honorific (English)

> **Theme:** Politeness & Honorifics
> **Level:** A1-C1
> **Standard:** US/UK English

English politeness expressions — formal/informal register, workplace etiquette, addressing others.

---

## Thank you

**Part of Speech:** expression (gratitude)
**Definition:** "Thank you" — most common gratitude expression.

### Usage

Universal gratitude.

### Variations

- **Thank you** — "Thanks" (formal)
- **Thanks** — "Thanks" (informal)
- **Thank you so much** — "Thanks a lot"
- **Thanks a million** — "Thanks very much" (emphatic)

### Cultural Notes

- **Frequency**: English = frequent use ("thanks" almost reflexive)
- **Comparison**: German/French = less frequent, English = expected
- **"Thanks" vs "Thank you"**: Latter more polite (formal/business)
- **Response**: "You're welcome" (most common), "No problem" (casual)
- **Service industry**: "Thanks!" expected (cashier, server)

## Please

**Part of Speech:** expression (polite)
**Definition:** "Please" — making requests polite.

### Usage

Every request.

### Variations

- **Please** — "Please"
- **Could you please ___?** — "Could you ___?"
- **Would you mind ___ing?** — "Would you ___?" (very polite)
- **If you don't mind** — "If it's not a problem"

### Cultural Notes

- **Children taught young**: "Say please and thank you"
- **Service culture**: "May I please have..." expected
- **Email**: Always include "please" in requests
- **Imperative without please**: Can sound rude ("Open the door")

## Excuse me

**Part of Speech:** expression (polite)
**Definition:** "Excuse me" — apology or attention-getter.

### Usage

Multiple contexts.

### Variations

- **Excuse me** — "Excuse me" (general)
- **Pardon me** — "Pardon" (more formal)
- **Excuse me? (rising intonation)** — "What did you say?"
- **Oh, excuse me** — "Sorry" (mild sarcasm)

### Cultural Notes

- **Three uses**:
  1. Apology (bumping someone) — "Excuse me!"
  2. Attention-getter (waiter) — "Excuse me, could I order?"
  3. Clarification (didn't hear) — "Excuse me?"
- **British**: "Sorry" used more broadly than US
- **Workplace**: "Excuse me" before interrupting meeting

- `[[apologies]]`

## I'm sorry

**Part of Speech:** expression (apology)
**Definition:** "I'm sorry" — apology.

### Usage

Apologizing.

### Variations

- **I'm sorry** — "Sorry" (formal)
- **Sorry** — "Sorry" (informal)
- **My apologies** — "Apologies" (very formal)
- **I apologize** — "Apologize" (formal/professional)

### Cultural Notes

- **British "sorry"**: Used very frequently (almost reflexive)
- **"Sorry to bother you"**: Polite opener for emails/calls
- **"My bad"**: Very casual (avoid in professional)
- **"I take responsibility"**: Higher-stakes apology
- **Accepting apology**: "It's okay" / "No worries" / "Don't worry about it"

- `[[apologies]]`

## You're welcome

**Part of Speech:** expression (response)
**Definition:** "You're welcome" — response to "thank you".

### Usage

Every "thank you" needs a response.

### Variations

- **You're welcome** — "Welcome" (formal)
- **No problem** — "No worries" (casual)
- **My pleasure** — "Pleasure" (formal/service)
- **Don't mention it** — "Don't mention" (British)

### Cultural Notes

- **British**: "Not at all" / "Don't mention it" common
- **US**: "You're welcome" still most polite
- **"No problem"**: Increasing in US (slightly less formal)
- **Service industry**: "My pleasure" (Chick-fil-A trademarked feel)
- **Avoid**: "Yeah" / "Uh huh" (feels dismissive)

- `[[greetings]]`

## May I ___?

**Part of Speech:** expression (polite)
**Definition:** "May I ___?" — polite permission request.

### Usage

Formal permission.

### Variations

- **May I ___?** — "Can I ___?" (formal)
- **Could I ___?** — "Can I ___?" (polite)
- **Would you mind if I ___ed?** — "Would you mind?" (very polite)
- **Do you mind if I ___?** — "Is it okay?"

### Cultural Notes

- **"May" vs "Can"**: 
  - "May" = formal (correct grammar)
  - "Can" = informal (common but technically wrong for permission)
- **Workplace**: "May I" = appropriate
- **Phone calls**: "May I speak with ___?" (formal)
- **Email**: "May I ask a favor?" (polite opener)

### Sources

- `[[requests]]`

## I appreciate it

**Part of Speech:** expression (gratitude)
**Definition:** "I appreciate it" — expressing gratitude + respect.

### Usage

Professional/formal gratitude.

### Variations

- **I appreciate it** — "Thanks" (formal)
- **I appreciate your help** — "Thanks for helping"
- **Much appreciated** — "Thanks" (very formal)
- **I can't thank you enough** — "Incredibly grateful"

### Cultural Notes

- **Workplace**: Very common (more formal than "thanks")
- **Service industry**: "I appreciate your patience"
- **Relationships**: Sincere, deeper than "thanks"
- **Email sign-off**: "Thanks for your help — much appreciated"
- **Emotion**: More weight than casual "thanks"

- `[[business-basics]]`

## It's been a pleasure

**Part of Speech:** expression (closing)
**Definition:** "It's been a pleasure" — closing formal interaction.

### Usage

End of business meetings, professional relationships.

### Variations

- **It's been a pleasure** — "Pleasure" (formal)
- **It's been lovely** — "Lovely" (British)
- **A pleasure working with you** — "Pleasure" (work context)
- **I look forward to working together** — "Future collaboration"

### Cultural Notes

- **Closing meetings**: Always close positively
- **Networking**: Common at end of conversations
- **British**: "It's been lovely" more common
- **Authenticity**: Should be sincere (not overused)
- **"Thank you for your time"**: Often precedes
