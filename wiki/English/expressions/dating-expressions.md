# Expressions: Dating & Romance (English)

> **Theme:** Dating & Romance
> **Level:** B1-C1
> **Standard:** US/UK English

Dating/romance expressions — confession, affection, relationship stages, breakup.

---

## I like you

**Part of Speech:** expression (confession)
**Definition:** "I like you" — direct confession.

### Usage

Most direct expression of interest.

### Variations

- **I like you** — "I like you"
- **I really like you** — "I really like you"
- **I have feelings for you** — "I have feelings"
- **I'm falling for you** — "Falling for you"

### Cultural Notes

- **Direct vs. indirect**: US = very direct, UK = indirect ("I quite like you"), Asia = indirect
- **"I love you"**: Reserved for serious relationships (not casual)
- **Confession timing**: After 1-3 months of dating
- **Friend zone**: "Let's just be friends" — soft rejection

## Will you be my boyfriend/girlfriend?

**Part of Speech:** expression (relationship)
**Definition:** "Will you be my boyfriend/girlfriend?" — official relationship start.

### Usage

When transitioning from casual to official.

### Variations

- **Will you be my boyfriend/girlfriend?** — "Be my bf/gf?"
- **Are we exclusive?** — "Exclusive?"
- **I want us to be official** — "Make it official"
- **What are we?** — "Define the relationship" (DTR)

### Cultural Notes

- **"DTR" (Define The Relationship)**: Common in US dating culture
- **Exclusivity talk**: Usually 1-3 months in
- **Social media**: Making it "Facebook official" (legacy tradition)
- **LGBTQ+**: Same questions apply, more direct in many cultures

### Sources

- `[[dating-romance]]`

## I love you

**Part of Speech:** expression (love)
**Definition:** "I love you" — deep affection.

### Usage

Long-term relationships, marriage.

### Variations

- **I love you** — "I love you"
- **I love you so much** — "Love you so much"
- **I'm in love with you** — "In love with you"
- **You mean the world to me** — "You mean everything"

### Cultural Notes

- **"I love you" timing**: US = after a few months, UK = much later
- **Family**: US = "I love you" common among family, UK/Asia = less common
- **Pet names**: Honey, babe, baby, sweetheart, darling, love
- **"ILY"**: Text abbreviation (informal)

### Sources

- `[[dating-romance]]`

## I want to break up

**Part of Speech:** expression (breakup)
**Definition:** "I want to break up" — ending relationship.

### Usage

Initiating breakup.

### Variations

- **I want to break up** — "Break up"
- **I think we should see other people** — "See others"
- **This isn't working out** — "Not working"
- **I need space** — "Need space"

### Cultural Notes

- **In-person**: Breakup should be in person (text/phone = cowardly)
- **Ghosting**: Sudden silence (avoided as disrespectful)
- **"It's not you, it's me"**: Common (and cliched) breakup line
- **Mutual breakups**: Less common, but most amicable
- **Stay friends?**: Often doesn't work; consider before promising

### Sources

- `[[dating-romance]]`

## Blind date

**Part of Speech:** expression (cultural)
**Definition:** "Blind date" — meeting someone new through friends/family.

### Usage

Setup dates.

### Variations

- **Blind date** — "Blind date"
- **Setup / Set up** — "Setup date"
- **Double date** — "Date with another couple"
- **Group date** — "Group outing"

### Cultural Notes

- **Friends setting up**: Common (US/UK)
- **Family setup**: More common in Asia/Africa/Latin America
- **Online dating**: Now majority of US couples meet online
- **Apps**: Tinder, Bumble, Hinge (US), Bumble (UK), Pairs (Japan)
- **Safety**: Always meet in public first

- `[[dating-romance]]`

## Moving in together

**Part of Speech:** expression (relationship)
**Definition:** "Moving in together" — cohabitation decision.

### Usage

Relationship milestone.

### Variations

- **Moving in together** — "Move in together"
- **Let's get a place together** — "Get a place"
- **Should we live together?** — "Live together?"
- **I'm ready to take the next step** — "Next step"

### Cultural Notes

- **US/UK**: Common after 1-2 years (unmarried couples)
- **Marriage first**: Asia/Middle East = marriage expected first
- **Roommate situation**: Many start as roommates, then date
- **Rent vs. buy**: Rent first (don't commit financially together early)
- **Lease considerations**: Both names on lease (legal protection)

### Sources

- `[[dating-romance]]`

## Online dating

**Part of Speech:** expression (modern)
**Definition:** "Online dating" — meeting via apps/websites.

### Usage

Modern dating reality.

### Variations

- **Online dating** — "Online dating"
- **Dating apps** — "Dating apps"
- **Swipe right** — "Swipe right" (Tinder = interested)
- **Catfish** — "Catfish" (fake profile)

### Cultural Notes

- **Apps (US)**: Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Match
- **Apps (UK)**: Bumble, Hinge, Tinder
- **Apps (Asia)**: Pairs (JP), TanTan (CN), Amanda (KR)
- **Photo tips**: Natural light, smile, group photos ok, no sunglasses
- **Profile red flags**: No bio, only selfies, "just ask me"

- `[[dating-romance]]`

## Meeting the parents

**Part of Speech:** expression (relationship)
**Definition:** "Meeting the parents" — introducing partner to family.

### Usage

Relationship milestone.

### Variations

- **Meeting the parents** — "Meet the parents"
- **Meeting the family** — "Meet the family"
- **Dinner with my folks** — "Dinner with family"
- **They're excited to meet you** — "Family is excited"

### Cultural Notes

- **US/UK**: Usually 6-12 months in (urban) or sooner (smaller towns)
- **Family dynamics**: Nuclear vs. extended family (cultural variation)
- **Gift**: Small (flowers, dessert, wine) — polite gesture
- **Topics**: Avoid politics, religion, exes on first meeting
- **"How did you two meet?"**: Common opening question

- `[[dating-romance]]`
