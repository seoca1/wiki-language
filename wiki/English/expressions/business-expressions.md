# Expressions: Business (English)

> **Theme:** Business & Workplace
> **Level:** B2-C1
> **Standard:** US/UK Business English

Business expressions — emails, meetings, contracts, titles, workplace etiquette.

---

## It's a pleasure to meet you

**Part of Speech:** expression (greeting)
**Definition:** "It's a pleasure to meet you" — formal first meeting.

### Usage

Initial business introduction.

### Variations

- **It's a pleasure to meet you** — "Pleased to meet you" (formal)
- **Great to meet you** — "Nice meeting you" (semi-formal)
- **I've heard great things about you** — "Great reputation"
- **Looking forward to working with you** — "Excited to collaborate"

### Cultural Notes

- **Handshake**: Firm, brief, eye contact (US/UK/EU/AU)
- **Business card**: Two-handed exchange (Asia), one-handed (US/UK)
- **Title usage**: First name basis after introductions (US), titles (EU/Asia)
- **"Pleased to meet you"**: Always respond in kind

## Thank you for your time

**Part of Speech:** expression (gratitude)
**Definition:** "Thank you for your time" — closing meeting or call.

### Usage

End of meeting, after calls.

### Variations

- **Thank you for your time** — "Thanks for your time" (formal)
- **I appreciate your time** — "I appreciate..."
- **Thanks again for meeting today** — "Thanks for meeting"
- **Looking forward to next steps** — "Excited for next steps"

### Cultural Notes

- **Follow-up email**: Always send thank-you within 24 hours
- **Next steps**: Confirm in writing
- **Calendar invites**: Follow up with calendar block for follow-up
- **Relationships**: Business built on trust + follow-through

- `[[business-basics]]`

## We'd like to get a quote

**Part of Speech:** expression (business)
**Definition:** "We'd like to get a quote" — pricing inquiry.

### Usage

Initial supplier/vendor communication.

### Variations

- **We'd like to get a quote** — "Need a quote"
- **Can you send us a proposal?** — "Send a proposal"
- **What's your pricing for ___?** — "Pricing for ___?"
- **Are there volume discounts?** — "Bulk pricing?"

### Cultural Notes

- **Quote contents**: Itemized, valid for X days, terms (Net 30, etc.)
- **Negotiation**: Expected for large orders/services
- **MOQ**: Minimum Order Quantity (especially manufacturing)
- **Payment terms**: Net 30 (30 days), Net 60, COD (cash on delivery)
- **Multiple quotes**: Always get 3+ for comparison

- `[[business-basics]]`

## What are the contract terms?

**Part of Speech:** expression (legal)
**Definition:** "What are the contract terms?" — contract negotiation.

### Usage

Reviewing contracts.

### Variations

- **What are the contract terms?** — "Terms?"
- **What's the payment schedule?** — "Payment schedule?"
- **What's the cancellation policy?** — "Cancellation?"
- **Are there any penalties?** — "Penalties?"

### Cultural Notes

- **Key clauses**: Payment, delivery, IP, termination, liability
- **Always have lawyer review**: Especially international contracts
- **Force majeure**: Pandemic/war/act of God (verify wording)
- **Dispute resolution**: Arbitration vs. litigation
- **NDAs**: Non-Disclosure Agreements (sign before sharing IP)

- `[[business-basics]]`

## Could I have an invoice?

**Part of Speech:** expression (accounting)
**Definition:** "Could I have an invoice?" — billing request.

### Usage

After business transactions.

### Variations

- **Could I have an invoice?** — "Invoice please"
- **Can you send a receipt?** — "Receipt?"
- **Net 30 terms** — "30-day payment"
- **Purchase order** — "PO number"

### Cultural Notes

- **Invoice requirements**: Tax ID, line items, payment terms
- **VAT/GST**: International invoices need VAT numbers
- **Expense reporting**: Most companies require itemized receipts
- **Late payment**: Triggers late fees (1-2% interest)
- **1099 contractors**: US tax form (year-end)

### Sources

- `[[business-basics]]`

## I need to take some time off

**Part of Speech:** expression (HR)
**Definition:** "I need to take some time off" — leave request.

### Usage

Requesting PTO/vacation/sick leave.

### Variations

- **I need to take some time off** — "Need time off"
- **I'd like to request vacation** — "PTO request"
- **I'm sick today** — "Sick today"
- **Personal day** — "Personal day off"

### Cultural Notes

- **US PTO**: 10-20 days/year (varies wildly by company)
- **EU PTO**: 20-30 days/year (legal minimum)
- **Sick leave**: Separate from PTO (US), combined (EU)
- **Maternity/Paternity**: US 12 weeks unpaid (FMLA), EU generous paid leave
- **Notice**: 2-4 weeks for vacation (longer for senior roles)

### Sources

- `[[business-basics]]`

## Let's set up a call

**Part of Speech:** expression (communication)
**Definition:** "Let's set up a call" — scheduling meeting.

### Usage

Proposing meeting time.

### Variations

- **Let's set up a call** — "Schedule a call"
- **Are you free for a meeting?** — "Free for meeting?"
- **Does Tuesday at 2pm work?** — "Tuesday 2pm?"
- **I'll send a calendar invite** — "Sending invite"

### Cultural Notes

- **Time zones**: Always specify (PT/ET/UTC)
- **Tools**: Zoom, Google Meet, Teams (industry standard)
- **Calendly**: Personal scheduling links (popular)
- **Confirm attendance**: Day before + 30 min before
- **Time zone converter**: World Time Buddy (avoid confusion)

- `[[business-basics]]`

## Is the manager available?

**Part of Speech:** expression (request)
**Definition:** "Is the manager available?" — asking to speak to manager.

### Usage

Reception, front desk, escalation.

### Variations

- **Is the manager available?** — "Manager available?"
- **Could I speak with ___?** — "Speak with ___?"
- **Is there someone I can talk to about ___?** — "Who can I talk to?"
- **I'd like to speak to someone in charge** — "Speak to person in charge"

### Cultural Notes

- **"Speak to the manager"**: Sometimes seen as escalation (US customer service)
- **Front desk**: First point of contact (filtering calls)
- **LinkedIn**: Find contact info for direct outreach
- **Email vs. phone**: Email for non-urgent, phone for urgent
- **Direct approach**: Senior people often prefer direct contact
