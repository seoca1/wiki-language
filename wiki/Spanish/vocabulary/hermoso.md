# hermoso

**Part of Speech:** adjetivo (adjective)
**Level:** A2

**Definition:** hermoso (masc.) / hermosa (fem.) — beautiful, gorgeous. Used for people (especially women), places, things, ideas. More literary/intense than "bonito."

**Gender Agreement:**
- Masculino: hermoso / hermosos (pl.)
- Femenino: hermosa / hermosas (pl.)

**Pronunciation:**
- IPA: /eɾˈmoso/ (Spain) /eɾˈmoso/ (LatAm)
- Syllables: her-mo-so (3 syllables)
- Stress: 2nd syllable (her-MO-so)

**Memory Tip:** "**HER**moso" sounds like "**her**-most-o" — the "her" makes it beautiful. Or: HERMOSO = "hermoso cielo" (beautiful sky) = Spanish romance cliché!

**Common Mistakes:**
- ❌ "Es hermosa" for a man → use **hermoso** (masc)
- ❌ "El mujer es hermosa" → "**La** mujer es hermosa" (fem)
- ❌ "Muy hermoso" only → also "qué hermoso" (how beautiful!)
- ✅ "¡Qué hermoso día!" (What a beautiful day!)
- ✅ "Es una mujer hermosa" (She's a beautiful woman)
- ✅ "Hermoso lugar" (Beautiful place)
- Note: Stronger than "bonito/lindo". Use carefully — can sound excessive.

**Register:** semi-formal. More literary than "bonito" but less than "bello" (poetic).

## Examples

- "¡Qué hermoso atardecer!" — ¡Qué hermosa puesta de sol!
- "Es un hombre hermoso." — He's a handsome man.
- "Tu casa es hermosa." — Your house is beautiful.
- "Una sonrisa hermosa" — A beautiful smile
- "Qué día más hermoso" — What a beautiful day

## Mini-Dialogue

```
A: ¿Qué te parece esta ciudad?   ← How do you like this city?
B: Es hermosa, me encanta.   ← It's beautiful, I love it.
A: Sí, especialmente al atardecer.   ← Yes, especially at sunset.
B: Totalmente de acuerdo.   ← Totally agree.
```

## Cultural Notes

- **Intensity scale** (least → most): lindo → bonito → hermoso → bello → precioso.
- **"¡Qué hermoso!"** very common exclamation — more emotional than English "beautiful".
- **Poetry & songs**: "hermoso/hermosa" frequent in love songs and poems.
- **"Hermosura"** = beauty (noun). "Eres una hermosura" = "You're a beauty."
- **LatAm vs Spain**: Both use "hermoso", but Spain often uses "guapo/guapa" for both genders more casually.
