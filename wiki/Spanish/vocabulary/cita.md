# cita

**Part of Speech:** sustantivo (noun, feminine)
**Level:** A2

**Definition:** Date (romantic); appointment (medical, business, professional). Two main senses, distinguished by context.

**Gender:** feminine (la cita, las citas)

**Etymology:** From Latin "citare" (to summon, call upon). Originally meant "summons" → "appointment" (going to be called) → "engagement on calendar" → modern "date."

**Pronunciation:**
- IPA: /ˈθita/ (Spain) /ˈsita/ (LatAm)
- Syllables: ci-ta
- Stress: 1st syllable (CI-ta)
- Note: "c" before "i" = /θ/ in Spain, /s/ in LatAm — major pronunciation difference!

**Memory Tip:** "**CI**ty **TA**xi" — a **cita** is when you arrange to meet someone (like calling a taxi to a city). Or: "**CIT**a" sounds like English "sit" + "a" — you sit down together for a date.

**Common Mistakes:**
- ❌ "La cita" for a man asking out a woman → use "**pedirle una cita a alguien**" (ask someone out)
- ❌ "Tengo una cita con el doctor a las 3" → that's fine! (medical appointment)
- ❌ "Vamos a una cita" → "**Tenemos una cita**" (have a date, not go to one)
- ❌ "Cita con mis amigos" → "**Quedada con mis amigos**" (cita implies romantic or formal)
- ✅ "Tengo una cita" (I have a date/appointment)
- ✅ "Primera cita" (First date)
- ✅ "Quedamos para una cita" (We arranged a date)
- ✅ "Cita a ciegas" (Blind date)
- ✅ "Pido una cita" (I request an appointment — medical)
- Note: For a casual "let's hang out," use "quedar" or "verse" — "cita" is more intentional/romantic.

**Register:** standard. Universally understood. Slight romantic connotation by default.
**Frequency:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (top 800 ES words)

**Visual:**

## Examples (Romantic)

- "Tengo una cita" — I have a date
- "Primera cita" — First date
- "Quedamos para una cita" — We arranged a date
- "La cita fue increíble" — The date was amazing
- "Llevar a alguien a una cita" — To take someone on a date
- "¿Quieres tener una cita conmigo?" — Want to go on a date with me?

## Examples (Appointment)

- "Tengo cita con el médico" — I have a doctor's appointment
- "Pedir cita previa" — To request a prior appointment
- "Sacar cita" — To make an appointment (LatAm)
- "Confirmar la cita" — To confirm the appointment

## Cultural Notes

- **"Cita" implica intención romántica** (a diferencia de "quedar" que es ambiguo). Confirmar: "¿Esto es una cita?" es válido si quieres aclarar.
- **Spanish dating culture**:
  - **Día de San Valentín** (Feb 14) = cita importante
  - **Cita a ciegas** (blind date) is common, often arranged by mutual friends
  - **La persona que invita paga** (the inviter pays) — traditional, but increasingly "invita y pagan a medias" (split) or "invita y luego invitas tú" (alternating)
  - **Punctuality**: arriving 10-15 min late is OK in Spain, less so in Mexico/other LatAm
  - **The check**: increasingly common to go Dutch
- **"Quedar"** = meet up, more casual
- **"Cortejar"** = to court (very old-fashioned, almost literary now)
- **"Pedir una cita"** = ask someone out (literal: "request a date")
- **첫 데이트 장소**: 보통 café, restaurant, walk in park. Movie는 대화 어려워서 덜 선호.
