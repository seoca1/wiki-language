# amigo

**Part of Speech:** sustantivo (noun, masculine)
**Level:** A1

**Definition:** Amigo (masc.) / Amiga (fem.) — friend, pal. Used for close non-romantic relationship.

**Género:** masculino (amigo) / femenino (amiga)

**Pronunciation:**
- IPA: /aˈmiɣo/ (Spain) /aˈmiɡo/ (LatAm)
- Syllables: a-mi-go (3 syllables)
- Stress: 2nd syllable (a-MI-go)

**Memory Tip:** "AmiGO" sounds like "amigo" — your "go-to" person is your amigo! Or: AMIGO = A Mi GO (I go to you).

**Common Mistakes:**
- ❌ "amigo" for a woman → use **amiga**
- ❌ "el amigo" for a female friend → "**la** amiga"
- ❌ "mis amigos" (correct for group of male friends) vs "mis amigas" (female group)
- ❌ Confusing with "novio" (boyfriend) — amigo = friend, novio = boyfriend
- ✅ "Es mi amigo" (He's my friend)
- ✅ "Somos amigos" (We're friends)
- ✅ "Mis mejores amigos" (My best friends)
- Note: "Amiga" can mean female friend OR girlfriend in informal usage (use "novia" for clarity).

**Register:** universal — appropriate for all contexts.
**Frequency:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (top 200 Spanish words)

## Examples

- "Es mi mejor amigo." — He's my best friend. (male)
- "Ella es mi amiga." — She's my friend.
- "Somos amigos desde la infancia." — We've been friends since childhood.
- "Mis amigos me ayudan mucho." — My friends help me a lot.
- "¿Salimos con amigos?" — Are we going out with friends?

## Mini-Dialogue

```
A: ¿Quién es tu mejor amigo?   ← Who is your best friend? (asking a male)
B: Pedro, desde la universidad.   ← Pedro, since college.
A: Qué bien. Son amigos íntimos?   ← That's great. Are you close friends?
B: Sí, es como un hermano.   ← Yes, he's like a brother.
```

## Cultural Notes

- **Importance**: Friendships (amistades) are deeply valued in Hispanic culture. Strong friend networks are crucial.
- **Sobrinos honorarios**: "Honorary nephews/nieces" — close family friends may be called tía/tío by their friends' kids.
- **Spanish vs English "friend"**: Spanish "amigo" is more intimate than English "friend" (acquaintance = "conocido").
- **Comadrazgo / compadrazgo**: Godparent-like friendship bonds, important in many Latin American cultures.
