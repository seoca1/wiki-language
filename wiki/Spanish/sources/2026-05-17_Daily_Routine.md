# Daily Routine (Rutina Diaria) — 2026-05-17

**Type:** Spanish Lesson (Daily Routine)
**Date Added:** 2026-05-17
**Language Level:** Beginner (A1-A2)

## Summary

Teaches how to describe a daily routine in Spanish, anchored by the reflexive verb system that governs most self-directed actions (waking up, showering, dressing). Pairs the present-tense conjugation pattern with time-telling and time-of-day adverbs so the learner can narrate a full day. The lesson functions as the practical application of the broader reflexive grammar concept for an absolute-beginner audience.

## Key Takeaways

- Reflexive verbs cover most self-care actions in Spanish — concept has no direct Korean equivalent
- The 6 reflexive pronouns (me/te/se/nos/os/se) attach to a conjugated -se infinitive and slot before the verb
- Time-telling uses **Es la una** / **Son las dos** with `y media` and `y cuarto` for half/quarter
- Parts of day: **por la mañana / tarde / noche** vs **de día / de noche**
- ~40 words across 9 daily-activity verbs + time expressions + day-structure adverbs
- Goal: describe morning, daytime, and evening routines in 5–10 coherent sentences

- Me levanto a las siete. — I get up at seven.

## Cultural Insights

- Spanish mealtimes (desayuno light, almuerzo 14:00, cena 21:00) reshape how a "daily routine" sounds in Spanish vs Korean
- Korean speakers must internalize that "I shower" requires a reflexive pronoun — no English/Korean parallel
