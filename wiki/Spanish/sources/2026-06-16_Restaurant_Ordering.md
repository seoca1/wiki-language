# Restaurant Ordering (Pedir Comida) — 2026-06-16

**Type:** Spanish Lesson (Restaurant Ordering)
**Date Added:** 2026-06-16
**Language Level:** Beginner-Intermediate (A2)

## Summary

Covers the full restaurant experience in Spanish — from seating through ordering, dietary restrictions, and the check. Built around two core grammatical features: the **gustar** construction (which reverses the English/Korean "I like X" structure) and the **quisiera** conditional form for polite requests. Bundles vocabulary for 20+ food/drink items, 6 restaurant verbs, and 7 common dishes, plus a meal-time comparison table across Spain, Argentina, Mexico, and Korea.

## Key Takeaways

- **Gustar** means "to please" — structure is `indirect pronoun + gusta/gustan + subject` (e.g., *Me gusta la paella*)
- **Quisiera** (conditional of querer) is the polite request form — softer than *quiero*
- 6 indirect object pronouns (me/te/le/nos/os/les) drive gusta/gustan agreement
- Singular subject → **gusta**, plural/multiple → **gustan**
- 20 food/drink nouns + 6 restaurant verbs (pedir, querer, probar, reservar, pagar, incluir)
- Spanish dinner 21-23 vs Korean dinner 18-20 — major cultural shift

## Expressions Extracted

- Para mí, la paella, por favor. — For me, the paella, please.
- La cuenta, por favor. — The check, please.
- Soy alérgico a los frutos secos. — I'm allergic to nuts.
- ¿Está incluida la propina? — Is the tip included?
- ¡Buen provecho! — Enjoy your meal!

## Cultural Insights

- Meal-time differences: Spain 21-23 cena, Argentina 21-23 (with merienda at 17-19), Mexico earlier (19-21), Korea 18-20
- Tipping customs: Spain 5-10% (often included as service), Argentina 10% (usually not), Mexico 10-15%, Korea not customary
- Spain dining etiquette: greet on entering, wait to be seated, "Buen provecho" to companions
- Pinch fingers to summon waiter is common in Spain
- Korean learners should snack around 18:00 to handle Spanish dinner timing
