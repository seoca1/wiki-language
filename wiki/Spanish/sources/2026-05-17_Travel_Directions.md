**Date Added:** 2026-05-17
**Language Level:** Beginner-Intermediate (A2-B1)

## Summary

Covers how to ask for, understand, and give directions in Spanish-speaking cities. The grammatical core combines prepositions (`a`, `de`, `en`, `hasta`, `desde`, `entre`) with the formal Ud. imperative (`siga`, `gire`, `vaya`, `tome`) used when addressing strangers. Built around practical scenarios — finding a museum, taking the metro, reading urban signage — so the learner can navigate confidently in Argentina, Mexico, or Spain.

## Key Takeaways

- Prepositions `a / de / en / hasta / desde / entre` form the spine of location/direction phrases
- Contractions `al` (a + el) and `del` (de + el) are mandatory in fixed phrases
- Formal Ud. command: `-AR` verbs end in `-e`, `-ER/-IR` verbs end in `-a`
- Irregular commands: vaya, haga, ponga, tenga, salga, venga, diga
- 6 transport words (metro, autobús, taxi, tren, avión, coche) + 13 place nouns + 13 direction expressions
- Three core asking patterns: ¿Cómo llego a…? / ¿Dónde está…? / ¿Está lejos?

## Cultural Insights

- Spanish address etiquette: use Ud. commands with strangers — softer than English imperatives, similar to Korean polite endings (-세요)
- Urban signage: calle, plaza, esquina are the basic address vocabulary across all Spanish-speaking countries
- Buenos Aires/Madrid metro systems use Línea + number, but boarding rules differ
