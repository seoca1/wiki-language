# Weather and Seasons (El Clima y las Estaciones) — 2026-06-13

**Type:** Spanish Lesson (Weather & Seasons)
**Date Added:** 2026-06-13
**Language Level:** Beginner (A1-A2)

## Summary

Teaches how to describe weather conditions and the four seasons in Spanish, organized around three distinct grammatical patterns that English/Korean speakers routinely mix up: **hacer** for general weather, **estar** + adjective for current state, and dedicated verbs **llover**/**nevar** for specific phenomena. Combines seasonal vocabulary, regional climate descriptions (Buenos Aires, Madrid, CDMX, Bogotá, Lima), and a small dialogue corpus so the learner can chat about today's weather, weekend plans, and trip destinations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Hacer** + weather noun: Hace calor/frío/sol/viento — the default pattern for general conditions
- **Estar** + adjective: Está soleado/nublado/lloviendo/nevando — current state, present continuous for right now
- **Llover** (o→ue) and **nevar** (e→ie) are 3rd-person-only verbs in practice — memorize *llueve* and *nieve*
- Don't say "Es caliente" for weather — that's "hot to the touch" (soup); weather requires **Hace calor**
- 4 seasons + 17 weather phenomena nouns + 7 temperature adjectives (~60 words total)
- Regional climate descriptions: Buenos Aires temperate, Madrid continental, Bogotá year-round cool, Lima cool+damp

## Expressions Extracted

- ¿Qué tiempo hace hoy? — What's the weather like today?
- ¿Está lloviendo? — Is it raining?
- Va a llover mañana. — It's going to rain tomorrow.
- ¿Cuál es la temperatura? — What's the temperature?
- Hace buen tiempo / Hace mal tiempo — The weather is nice / bad.

## Cultural Insights

- Buenos Aires has temperate seasons (verano calor / invierno frío) — pair with [[tango-argentino]] culture entry
