# Japanese Cultural Traditions & Customs

**Type:** culture, social-ritual, daily-life
**Region:** Japan (with East Asian context)

## Overview

Japanese cultural traditions blend Shinto, Buddhist, and Confucian influences into everyday life. From bowing to removing shoes, from seasonal festivals (季節行事) to family rituals, these customs shape daily Japanese behavior. This page complements `[[japanese-dating-culture]]`, `[[japanese-school-life]]`, and `[[japanese-holiday-culture]]`.

## Daily Etiquette Customs

### Bowing (お辞儀 Ojiigi)

The most fundamental Japanese gesture — universal across situations.

| Bow Type | Angle | Duration | Use |
|----------|-------|----------|-----|
| **会釈 (Eshaku)** | 15° | Brief | Casual greeting |
| **敬礼 (Keirei)** | 30° | Standard | Polite, respectful |
| **最敬礼 (Saikeirei)** | 45° | Long | Very respectful, apology |

**Bowing Rules**:
- **Men**: Hands at sides
- **Women**: Hands on thighs
- **Higher rank bows longer/deeper**
- **Lower rank bows first/longer**
- **Business cards**: Bow when offering/receiving
- **Phone calls**: Stand and bow (even when on phone)

### Bowing vs. Handshakes

- **Handshakes** (握手 akushu): Common in business with foreigners
- **Bows first, then handshake**: Mixed-meeting protocol
- **Women may avoid handshakes** in traditional settings (just bow)

### Removing Shoes (靴を脱ぐ)

| Place | Remove Shoes? |
|-------|---------------|
| **Japanese homes** | Always |
| **Traditional restaurants (座敷)** | Always |
| **Ryokan (inn)** | Always |
| **Temples/shrines** | Sometimes (look for sign) |
| **Public bath (温泉)** | Always (lockers provided) |
| **Some modern offices** | Variable |
| **Shoes in entryway (玄関)** | Always (place on genkan lower step) |

**Shoe Etiquette**:
- **Genkan (玄関)**: Entryway, remove outdoor shoes here
- **Slippers (スリッパ)**: Indoor shoes (use if provided)
- **Toilet slippers**: Special slippers for bathroom only
- **Bare feet**: Sometimes required (tatami, hot springs)
- **Tabi (足袋)**: Traditional split-toe socks

### Chopsticks (箸 Hashi) Etiquette

#### Forbidden Actions

| Action | Reason | Severity |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Stick upright in rice** | Resembles funeral incense | Very offensive |
| **Pass food chopstick-to-chopstick** | Funeral bone-passing ritual | Very offensive |
| **Point chopsticks at people** | Rude | Rude |
| **Wave chopsticks around** | Rude | Rude |
| **Stick in food upright** | Generally avoided | Depends |
| **Rub disposable chopsticks together** | Implies they're cheap | Mildly rude |
| **Use one chopstick to pull other** | Awkward | Mildly rude |

#### Proper Chopstick Use

| Rule | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Two hands when passing** | When giving/receiving chopsticks |
| **Rest on chopstick rest (箸置き hashioki)** | When not using |
| **Rest on bowl edge** | Acceptable (across top) |
| **Don't eat directly from shared dishes** | Use serving chopsticks |
| **Lift bowl to mouth** | Acceptable (rice, soup) |
| **Don't cross chopsticks** | Rude |

### Sitting (座る Suwaru)

| Style | Description | Where |
|-------|-------------|-------|
| **正座 (Seiza)** | Formal kneeling | Tea ceremony, tatami rooms |
| **あぐら (Agura)** | Cross-legged | Casual tatami seating |
| **椅子座り (Isu-zwari)** | Chair sitting | Most modern contexts |
| **胡座 (Agura)** | Cross-legged (casual) | Tatami, casual |

### Gift-Giving (贈り物 Okurimono)

| Occasion | Direction |
|----------|-----------|
| **お中元 (Ochūgen)** | Younger → Elder (mid-year) |
| **お歳暮 (Oseibo)** | Younger → Elder (year-end) |
| **お年玉 (Otoshidama)** | Elder → Children (New Year) |
| **お見舞い (Omimai)** | Friend → Sick person |
| **結婚祝い (Kekkon iwai)** | Friend → Newly married couple |
| **内祝い (Uchi iwai)** | Host → Guests (wedding/birth) |

### Gift Etiquette

- **Two hands**: Always when giving/receiving
- **Remove price tag**: Before presenting
- **Wrap (tsutsumu)**: Important gifts should be wrapped
- **Reciprocate**: 御返し (okaeshi) expected
- **Gift form**: Practical gifts, not luxurious (modesty)

## Religious Traditions

### Shinto (神道 Shintō)

- **神社 (Jinja)**: Shrines — most Japanese neighborhoods have one
- **鳥居 (Torii)**: Gates marking sacred space
- **お賽銭 (Saisen)**: Monetary offering at shrine (5 yen = good luck, 10 yen = perfect)
- **二礼二拍手一礼 (Nirei Ni Hakushu Ippyi)**: Two bows, two claps, one bow
- **お守り (Omamori)**: Amulets/talismans for protection/luck
- **絵馬 (Ema)**: Wooden prayer plaques
- **巫女 (Miko)**: Shrine maidens (often high school/college part-time)
- **初詣 (Hatsumōde)**: New Year's shrine visit (Jan 1-3)

### Buddhist (仏教 Bukkyō)

- **お寺 (Otera)**: Temples
- **本堂 (Hondō)**: Main temple hall
- **仏壇 (Butsudan)**: Family Buddhist altar (home)
- **仏壇 (Buddhist altar)**: At home with memorial tablets
- **位牌 (Ihahi)**: Memorial tablet for deceased ancestors
- **数珠 (Juzu)**: Prayer beads (108 beads)
- **合掌 (Gasshō)**: Palms-together prayer position
- **写経 (Shakyō)**: Sutra copying (merit-making practice)
- **座禅 (Zazen)**: Seated meditation (Zen)
- **写経 (Shakyō)**: Writing sutras (meditation)

### Funeral vs. Wedding Traditions

| Tradition | Shinto | Buddhist |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| **Funeral** | Rare (historical) | Standard (90%+) |
| **Wedding** | Standard (神前式) | Modern (仏教式) |
| **Cremation** | Acceptable | Strong tradition |
| **Tomb visits** | Optional | Standard (お盆) |
| **Obon** | — | Annual ancestor memorial |

## Life-Cycle Rituals

### Birth & Childhood

| Age | Event | Description |
|------|-------|-------------|
| **7 days** | お七夜 (Oshichiya) | 7th night naming |
| **14 days** | Naming ceremony (命名式) | Official name |
| **1 month** | 初宮参り (Hatsumiyamairi) | First shrine visit |
| **100 days** | 百日 (Hyakunichi) | 100-day celebration |
| **1 year** | 初誕生 (Hatsu Tanjō) | First birthday |
| **3-5-7** | 七五三 (Shichi-Go-San) | Shrine visit at ages 3/5/7 |
| **15** | 元服 (Genpuku) | Historical coming-of-age (obsolete) |
| **20** | 二十歳 (Hatachi) | Modern Coming of Age Day |

### 七五三 (Shichi-Go-San)

- **Ages**: 3 (girls), 5 (boys), 7 (girls)
- **Date**: November 15
- **Custom**: Visit shrine, pray for healthy growth
- **Photos**: Often professional (in kimono)
- **千歳飴 (Chitose ame)**: Special candy in red-white bag
- **Location**: Major shrines busy (Meiji Shrine etc.)

### Marriage (結婚 Kekkon)

| Style | Description | Popularity |
|-------|-------------|-----------|
| **神前式 (Shinzen-shiki)** | Shinto ceremony at shrine | ~20% |
| **仏前式 (Butsuzen-shiki)** | Buddhist ceremony | ~5% |
| **人前式 (Hitomae-shiki)** | Secular ceremony | ~10% |
| **教会式 (Kyōkai-shiki)** | Christian-style chapel | ~65% (often non-religious) |
| **海外挙式 (Kaikai Kyoshiki)** | Destination wedding | Growing |

### Wedding Customs

- **ご祝儀 (Goshūgi)**: Money gift (50,000-300,000¥)
- **ご祝儀袋 (Goshūgi bukuro)**: Special envelope
- **袱 (Fukuro)**: Wrapping money in special cloth
- **新札 (Shinsatsu)**: Use new bills (fresh start symbolism)
- **式場 (Shikijō)**: Ceremony venue
- **披露宴 (Hirōen)**: Wedding reception
- **スピーチ (Supīchi)**: Best man/woman speeches
- **余興 (Yokyō)**: Reception entertainment
- **お色直し (Oiro-naoshi)**: Costume change mid-reception

### Funeral (葬式 Sōshiki)

- **通夜 (Tsuya)**: Wake (night before)
- **告別式 (Kokubetsushiki)**: Funeral service
- **火葬 (Kasō)**: Cremation (~99% cremation rate)
- **お通夜 (Otsuya)**: Overnight wake
- **喪主 (Moshū)**: Chief mourner (eldest son typically)
- **香典 (Kōden)**: Condolence money
- **位牌 (Ihahi)**: Memorial tablet
- **49日忌 (Shijūkunichi-ki)**: 49th day memorial
- **初盆 (Hatsubon)**: First Obon after death
- **一周忌 (Isshūki)**: First anniversary memorial

## Food Culture Customs

### Eating Customs

| Custom | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **いただきます (Itadakimasu)** | Before meals ("I humbly receive") |
| **ごちそうさまでした (Gochisōsama deshita)** | After meals ("Thank you for the meal") |
| **Itadakimasu → eat → Gochisōsama** | Standard meal ritual |
| **いただきます alone** | Less formal |
| **Eating in public while walking** | Generally avoided |
| **Blowing nose at table** | Very rude |
| **Sticking chopsticks in rice** | Forbidden (funeral association) |
| **Sharing food from same plate** | Common (family, friends) |

### Tea Ceremony (茶道 Sadō)

- **Matcha (抹茶)**: Powdered green tea
- **Wagashi (和菓子)**: Sweet accompanying tea
- **Tea room (茶室 chashitsu)**: Small intimate room
- **Tatami floor**: Sitting on heels (seiza)
- **Four principles**: 和 (wa, harmony), 敬 (kei, respect), 清 (sei, purity), 寂 (jaku, tranquility)
- **Matcha bowl (茶碗 chawan)**: Held with both hands

### Sake Culture

- **乾杯 (Kanpai)**: Toast (literally "dry the glass")
- **お酌 (Oshaku)**: Pouring for others (not yourself)
- **両手 (Ryōte)**: Two hands when serving
- **One cup shared**: Often for sake (san-san-kudo)
- **飲む前に (Nomu mae ni)**: "Before drinking" (raise slightly)
- **酒豪 (Sakegō)**: "Sake hero" (good drinker)

## Family & Social Customs

### Family Structure

- **家族 (Kazoku)**: Family
- **核家族 (Kaku kazoku)**: Nuclear family
- **大家族 (Daikazoku)**: Extended family (rare now)
- **父 (Chichi)**: Father (humble)
- **お父さん (Otōsan)**: Father (respectful)
- **父ちゃん (Tōchan)**: Father (casual)
- **お母さん (Okāsan)**: Mother (respectful)
- **お母ちゃん (Okāchan)**: Mother (casual)

### Marriage and Names

| Item | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **婿 (Muko)**: Husband living with wife's family |
| **嫁 (Yome)**: Wife living with husband's family |
| **入籍 (Nyūseki)**: Legal marriage registration |
| **婚姻届 (Kon'in Todoke)**: Marriage registration form |
| **姓 (Sei) / 氏 (Shi)** | Family name |
| **名前 (Namae)** | Given name |
| **ニックネーム (Nikku nēmu)** | Nickname |

### Seniority System (先輩後輩)

- **先輩 (Senpai)**: Senior (precedence)
- **後輩 (Kōhai)**: Junior
- **上下関係 (Jōge kankei)**: Vertical relationship
- **目上 (Meue)**: Person above (in status)
- **目下 (Meshita)**: Person below (in status)
- **先輩文化 (Senpai bunka)**: Senior culture
- **Aniki/Aniki** | Gangster/yakuza "older brother" (avoid)

## Daily Customs

### Morning Routines

| Activity | Japanese | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Wake | 起きる (okiru) | Often 6:00-7:00 |
| Wash face | 顔を洗う (kao o arau) | Cold water |
| Eat breakfast | 朝ごはん (asa gohan) | Often rice + miso + egg |
| Brush teeth | 歯を磨く (ha o migaku) | Electric toothbrush common |
| Go to work/school | 出勤/登校 (shukkin/tōkō) | Often by train |

### Evening Routines

| Activity | Japanese | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Come home | 帰宅 (kitaku) | Often late |
| Eat dinner | 夕飯 (yūhan) | 19:00-21:00 typical |
| Bath | お風呂 (ofuro) | Before bed! |
| Watch TV | テレビ (terebi) | News, dramas, anime |
| Sleep | 寝る (neru) | 23:00-01:00 typical |

### Bath Culture (お風呂 Ofuro)

- **Bathtub (浴槽 yokusō)**: For soaking (not cleaning!)
- **Shower outside tub**: Clean body FIRST
- **Deep tub soak**: Long hot soak (40-42°C)
- **Family bath**: Often shared (kids with parents)
- **温泉 (Onsen)**: Natural hot springs
- **銭湯 (Sentō)**: Public bathhouse
- **入浴剤 (Nyūyokuzai)**: Bath additives
- **朝風呂 (Asaburo)**: Morning bath (rare but exists)

## Tourist & Visitor Customs

### Do

✅ **Remove shoes** when entering homes/temples/some restaurants
✅ **Bow slightly** when greeting
✅ **Say "いただきます"** before meals
✅ **Receive items with both hands** (business cards, gifts)
✅ **Be quiet on public transport**
✅ **Tip NOT expected** (high-end hotels excluded)
✅ **Respect queues** (often by physical markers)
✅ **Say "Sumimasen"** ("Excuse me") often

### Don't

❌ **Tip at restaurants** (offensive)
❌ **Blow nose loudly** in public
❌ **Talk loudly** on trains
❌ **Walk and eat** (generally avoided)
❌ **Stick chopsticks upright** in rice (funeral association)
❌ **Enter bath before washing** (deeply offensive)
❌ **Enter hot spring with tattoos** (controversial)
❌ **Speak casually to seniors** in hierarchy

## 例文

「お辞儀」(ojigi) は角度で意味が変わる: 軽く 15° = 軽い挨拶、深く 45° = 深い敬意、土下座 (dorozuwari) = 最大限の謝罪。
「温泉」(onsen) 入浴は裸で入り、タオルは湯に入れない。入浴前に「かけ湯」(kakeyu) で体を清める。
「正座」(seiza) は床に座る正式な姿勢で、改まった場で行う。足の痺れは美徳とされます。
