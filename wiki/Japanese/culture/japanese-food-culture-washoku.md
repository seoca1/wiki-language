# Japanese Food Culture: Washoku and Itadakimasu

**Overview:** Japanese cuisine (和食, washoku) is recognized by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage, centered on the ritual of saying "itadakimasu" before meals.

## Key Points

- **Itadakimasu (いただきます)**: Said before eating — "I humbly receive" — gratitude to chef, ingredients, and life. Counter: "ごちそうさま (gochisōsama)" after meals — "it was a feast."
- **Washoku (和食) Characteristics**:
  - **Fresh ingredients, seasonal (旬, shun)**: Spring bamboo, summer eel, autumn matsutake, winter crab
  - **Variety of cooking methods**: Raw (刺身 sashimi), grilled (焼き yaki), simmered (煮物 nimono), steamed (蒸物 mushimono), deep-fried (揚げ agemono)
  - **Dashi (出汁)**: Umami foundation — kombu (kelp) + katsuobushi (bonito flakes)
  - **Five colors (五色)**: White, black, red, yellow, green for visual variety
  - **Five flavors (五味)**: Sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami
  - **Five methods (五法)**: Raw, grill, simmer, steam, fry
- **Table Setting (和食の配膳)**:
  - **Ichiju-Sansai (一汁三菜)**: One soup + three sides (standard meal structure)
  - **Hashi (箸, chopsticks)**: Placed horizontally in front (箸置き, hashioki)
  - **Donburi (丼)**: Bowl with rice + toppings
  - **Sushi**: 握り (nigiri) + 巻き (maki) + ちらし (chirashi)
- **Dining Etiquette**:
  - Never stick chopsticks upright in rice (funeral association)
  - Don't pass food chopstick-to-chopstick
  - Slurping noodles (ラーメン, そば, うどん) = OK and signals enjoyment

## Language Connections

- `和食` (washoku) — Japanese cuisine
- `いただきます` (itadakimasu) — Pre-meal greeting
- `ごちそうさま` (gochisōsama) — Post-meal greeting
- `出汁` (dashi) — Soup stock
- `刺身` (sashimi) — Raw fish
- `寿司` (sushi) — Vinegared rice + fish
- `旬` (shun) — Season (for ingredients)
