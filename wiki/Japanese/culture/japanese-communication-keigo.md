# Japanese Communication: Keigo and Reading the Air

**Overview:** Japanese communication emphasizes harmony (wa, 和) through honorific language (keigo) and reading the atmosphere (kūki wo yomu, 空気を読む).

## Key Points

- **Keigo (敬語) System** (see workplace culture for full breakdown):
  - **Sonkeigo (尊敬語)**: Elevate the listener
  - **Kenjōgo (謙譲語)**: Lower yourself
  - **Teineigo (丁寧語)**: Polite speech
- **Kuuki wo Yomu (空気を読む)**: "Reading the air" — picking up unspoken social cues. Critical workplace/social skill.
- **Honne to Tatemae (本音と建前)**: True feelings vs. public facade. Common in business and social settings.
- **Indirect Communication**: Japanese often avoids direct refusal:
  - **検討する (kentō suru)**: "I will consider it" = likely refusal
  - **考えおきます (kangaete okimasu)**: "I will think about it" = soft no
  - **難しいですね (muzukashii desu ne)**: "It's difficult" = not happening
- **Forms of Address**:
  - **-san (さん)**: Standard polite
  - **-kun (くん)**: Junior males, casual
  - **-chan (ちゃん)**: Children, intimate
  - **-sama (様)**: Highly respectful (customers, deities)
- **Family Terms (for outsiders)**: お父さん (father), お母さん (mother) — used for others' parents, not your own.

## Language Connections

- `敬語` (keigo) — Honorific language
- `空気を読む` (kūki wo yomu) — Read the air
- `本音` (honne) — True feelings
- `建前` (tatemae) — Public facade
- `検討する` (kentō suru) — Consider (often means no)
- `さん/くん/ちゃん/様` — Honorifics
