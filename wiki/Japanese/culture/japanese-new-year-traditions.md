# Japanese New Year Traditions (お正月)

**Overview:** Japanese New Year (お正月, Oshōgatsu) is the most important traditional holiday, marked by family reunions, shrine visits, and symbolic foods.

## Key Points

- **Dates**: January 1-3 (元日, 2日, 3日). Many businesses closed until January 4.
- **Hatsumode (初詣)**: First shrine/temple visit of the new year (January 1-3). Millions visit shrines like Meiji Shrine (Tokyo), Fushimi Inari (Kyoto). Omikuji (おみくじ) fortune, ema (絵馬) wooden plaques, omamori (お守り) amulets.
- **Osechi (おせち)**: Traditional New Year foods in lacquered boxes (重箱, jūbako). Symbolic meanings:
  - **黒豆 (kuromame)**: Black beans — health/longevity
  - **数の子 (kazunoko)**: Herring roe — fertility/children
  - **田作り (tazukuri)**: Sardines — abundant harvest
  - **紅白かまぼこ (kōhaku kamaboko)**: Red/white fish cake — celebration
  - **栗きんとん (kurikinton)**: Sweet chestnut — gold/wealth
- **Toshikoshi Soba (年越しそば)**: Year-crossing noodles — long life. Eaten at midnight on December 31.
- **Otoshidama (お年玉)**: Money given to children in decorative envelopes (ポチ袋, pochibukuro). Amount varies by age.
- **Hatsugama (初釜)**: First tea ceremony of the year.
- **Nengajo (年賀状)**: New Year greeting postcards. Must be delivered January 1.

## Language Connections

- `お正月` (oshōgatsu) — New Year
- `初詣` (hatsumōde) — First shrine visit
- `おせち料理` (osechi ryōri) — New Year food
- `年越しそば` (toshikoshi soba) — Year-crossing noodles
- `お年玉` (otoshidama) — Children's New Year money
- `年賀状` (nengajō) — New Year card
- `おみくじ` (omikuji) — Fortune slip
