# emotions-reactions (Japanese) expressions

> **Theme:** Spontaneous Emotional Reactions (Exclamations & Interjections)
> **Level:** A1-A2 (casual register; matching pitch-accent patterns noted)

**Overview:** Japanese emotional exclamations are predominantly **casual** (friendly/peer register). In polite or formal settings, these are usually suppressed in favor of measured comments (驚きました, 嬉しゅうございます). Each entry below includes pitch-accent notation (Tokyo standard) because these high-frequency interjections carry meaning through intonation as much as vocabulary.

---

## すごい！

**Literal Translation:** "Amazing!" / "Incredible!"

**Meaning:** Spontaneous exclamation of awe, surprise, or admiration. Functions like English "Wow!" or "That's awesome!"

**Usage Context:** Casual. Friends, family, peers. Avoid in business or with superiors. Female speakers may use すっごい for emphasis.

**Pattern:** い-adjective (すごい) used as standalone exclamation with ！

**Pitch Accent:** す**ご**い — odaka (尾高型), pitch drops after ご and rises on い.

### Examples

- すごい！それ、めっちゃ綺麗。 — Wow! That's super pretty.
- すごい！テスト満点だったの？ — Wow! You got a perfect score?
- すごいね、頑張ってたもんね。 — That's amazing, you've been working hard.

### Cultural Background

すごい derives from 凄 い ("fearsome/dreadful"), historically negative, but modern usage is overwhelmingly positive among younger speakers. Pronounced intensity comes from the casual particle ね or mecha/めっちゃ prefixes.

## やった！

**Literal Translation:** "I did it!" / "Yes!"

**Meaning:** Exclamation of personal triumph or excitement. Used when something good happens to the speaker or their team.

**Usage Context:** Casual. Friends, classmates, teammates. Common in sports, gaming, and achievement contexts. Strongly personal triumph-oriented.

**Pattern:** Verb past form of やる (to do) used as exclamation

**Pitch Accent:** **や**った — atamadaka (頭高型), high pitch on や then drops.

### Examples

- やった！合格した！ — Yes! I passed!
- やったー！勝った！ — Yay! We won!
- やったね、二人とも。 — You both did it, congrats.

### Cultural Background

やる ("to do") in this context is the triumph/achievement verb, not the casual "to do" used with inferiors. The stretched form やったー adds childlike enthusiasm and is common in casual celebrations.

## まあ！

**Literal Translation:** "My!" / "Oh my!"

**Meaning:** Soft exclamation of surprise, admiration, or mild disbelief. More feminine and refined than すごい.

**Usage Context:** Casual / polite. Used by women of all ages; older women especially. In polite company it reads as gentle and considerate. Some men use it to sound refined.

**Pattern:** Interjection まあ used standalone

**Pitch Accent:** **ま**あ — heiban (平板型), flat high pitch.

### Examples

- まあ！きれいな花ね。 — My, what a beautiful flower.
- まあ、そんなこともあるわ。 — My, things like that happen.
- まあ、ありがとう。 — My, thank you.

### Cultural Background

まあ carries an aesthetic of refined feminine reserve. The trailing ね/わ adds softness; using まあ in casual male speech can sound affected, while using it appropriately conveys politeness and warmth in social settings.

## 残念！

**Literal Translation:** "How disappointing!" / "What a pity!"

**Meaning:** Expression of regret or mild disappointment about an outcome.

**Usage Context:** Casual / polite. Versatile across registers. Used to acknowledge a friend's disappointment, comment on a missed opportunity, or react to bad news.

**Pattern:** Na-adjective (残念) + ！

**Pitch Accent:** **ざ**んねん — odaka (尾高型), pitch drops on ん and rises on ね.

### Examples

- 残念！今日は雨だ。 — Too bad! It's raining today.
- 残念だけど、行けないんだ。 — Unfortunately, I can't go.
- 残念でした、また今度ね。 — Too bad, next time then.

### Cultural Background

残念 (zannen) literally means "remaining feeling," i.e., something left unfulfilled. It is one of the standard, register-flexible expressions for disappointing news, more neutral than 嫌だ ("I hate this").

## 信じられない

**Literal Translation:** "I can't believe it"

**Meaning:** Strong expression of disbelief or shock. Used for both positive and negative surprises.

**Usage Context:** Casual / polite. Versatile. Intensified by まじで or ほんとに in casual speech. Polite register: 信じられません.

**Pattern:** Potential-negative form of 信じる (to believe) + ない

**Pitch Accent:** し**ん**じられない — odaka (尾高型), pitch drops on ん and rises on じ.

### Examples

- 信じられない、彼が優勝したの。 — I can't believe he won.
- まじで信じられない！ — Seriously, I can't believe this!
- 信じられない話だけど、本当なんだ。 — Hard to believe, but it's true.

### Cultural Background

For maximum emphasis, casual speakers may use 信じられん or shorten to しんじらんない. The verb 信じる carries strong personal commitment in Japanese (not just intellectual assent), so "I can't believe" implies a deep emotional disruption.

## まさか

**Literal Translation:** "No way!" / "It can't be!"

**Meaning:** Exclamation of strong disbelief, often in response to unexpected or shocking news.

**Usage Context:** Casual. Used when something seems impossible or implausible. Often followed by a clarifying sentence.

**Pattern:** Interjection まさか used standalone or with follow-up clause

**Pitch Accent:** **ま**さか — atamadaka (頭高型), high pitch on ま then drops.

### Examples

- まさか！彼が結婚したの？ — No way! He got married?
- まさかそんなことないよ。 — No way, that can't be true.
- まさかの展開だね。 — What an unexpected development.

### Cultural Background

まさか implies "surely not" and frames the speaker as having considered the opposite outcome certain. Common in drama and everyday conversation; functions as both genuine disbelief and rhetorical surprise.

## 困ったね

**Literal Translation:** "That's troubling, isn't it"

**Meaning:** Expression of shared difficulty or exasperation. Less emotional than 嫌だ and more collaborative.

**Usage Context:** Casual / polite. Used between friends, coworkers, and family to acknowledge a problem together. The ね makes it inclusive rather than individual.

**Pattern:** Verb past of 困る (to be troubled) + ね

**Pitch Accent:** **こ**まったね — atamadaka (頭高型), high pitch on こ then drops.

### Examples

- 困ったね、電車が止まってるよ。 — This is bad, the trains are stopped.
- 困ったね、どうしよう。 — This is a problem, what should we do?
- 困ったね、また遅刻か。 — Oh no, late again?

### Cultural Background

困る sits between "trouble" and "bother" in register. Using 困ったね with friends acknowledges a shared predicament without assigning blame; with ね it implicitly invites the listener into the problem-solving.

## 嬉しい

**Literal Translation:** "I'm happy" / "Glad"

**Meaning:** Direct statement of happiness or pleasure. Can stand alone or modify a follow-up sentence.

**Usage Context:** Casual (as a statement). Polite equivalent: 嬉しいです. The plain form is used with close friends; with others 嬉しゅうございます is more respectful.

**Pattern:** い-adjective 嬉しい (casual past: 嬉しかった)

**Pitch Accent:** **う**れしい — atamadaka (頭高型), high pitch on う then drops.

### Examples

- 嬉しい、ありがとう。 — I'm happy, thank you.
- 本当に嬉しい！ — I'm really happy!
- 会えて嬉しい。 — I'm glad we could meet.

### Cultural Background

嬉しい (ureshii) is the standard "I am happy" word. Its high pitch accent (unlike 楽しい "fun" which is heiban) helps distinguish it in conversation. Polite situations often use 嬉しゅうございます, a humble-influenced form common among women.

## よかった

**Literal Translation:** "That's good" / "Glad to hear it"

**Meaning:** Expression of relief or satisfaction. Used to acknowledge good news about someone else or a positive outcome.

**Usage Context:** Casual / polite. Universally versatile. Common reply when a friend shares good news ("I passed the test" → "よかったね").

**Pattern:** い-adjective past form of いい (よい → よかった)

**Pitch Accent:** **よ**かった — atamadaka (頭高型), high pitch on よ then drops.

### Examples

- よかったね、合格したんだって。 — Good for you, I heard you passed.
- よかった、無事で。 — Good, you're safe.
- それはよかった。 — That's good.

### Cultural Background

よかった derives from 良い (yoi, "good"), with the past tense marking "this good outcome is now in the past/settled." It conveys both affirmation and relief, making it one of the most useful casual expressions for social bonding.

## うわー / わぁ

**Literal Translation:** "Whoa!" / "Ooh!"

**Meaning:** Spontaneous exclamation of awe, surprise, or amazement. Among the most versatile and neutral interjections.

**Usage Context:** Casual. Friends, family, peers. Used for both positive and negative surprises. わぁ is more feminine; うわー is gender-neutral.

**Pattern:** Interjection used standalone

**Pitch Accent:** う**わ**ー — pitch rises on わ; わ**ぁ** — rises then trails down.

### Examples

- うわー、すごい景色！ — Whoa, amazing view!
- わぁ、きれい！ — Ooh, pretty!
- うわー、それは驚いた。 — Whoa, that surprised me.

### Cultural Background

Both interjections are phonetically expressive — the vowel length and pitch convey emotional intensity. They're often used as a "first reaction" before more articulate comment, marking the speaker's spontaneity.
