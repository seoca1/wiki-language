# complaints (Japanese) expressions

> **Theme:** Complaints, Grievances & Diplomatic Objections
> **Level:** B1-B2 (with keigo escalations through C1)

**Overview:** Japanese complaints are heavily mitigated to preserve 関係 (kankei, relationships) and 面子 (mentsu, face). Direct accusations are rare; instead, speakers describe problems as shared inconveniences, suggest improvements, or use apologetic framing. Each entry below specifies the keigo level (casual / polite / humble / formal) and the appropriate audience.

---

## 申し訳ございませんが

**Literal Translation:** "I am without excuse, but ~"

**Meaning:** Formal apologetic opener used to introduce a complaint, objection, or inconvenient request. Stronger than すみません.

**Usage Context:** Polite / formal. Business contexts, customer service, and situations where the speaker must challenge or interrupt.

**Pattern:** 申し訳ございません + が (connective, "but")

### Examples

- 申し訳ございませんが、再度ご確認ください。 — I'm sorry, but please check again.
- 申し訳ございませんが、本日は定休日でございます。 — I apologize, but today is our regular holiday.
- 申し訳ございませんが、対応できかねます。 — I'm sorry, but we are unable to accommodate this.

### Cultural Background

申し訳ございません is the humble (謙譲語) form of 済ませる ("to finish/settle"), implying "I have no way to excuse myself." In service contexts it signals that the speaker owns the problem even when it is not their fault.

## 困っております

**Literal Translation:** "I am in a troubled state"

**Meaning:** Soft way to indicate a problem. Implies "I need help" rather than "you are at fault."

**Usage Context:** Polite. Common in written complaints, service emails, and customer interactions where the speaker wants to describe difficulty without assigning blame.

**Pattern:** 困る (te-form) + おります (humble form of います)

### Examples

- 商品の到着が遅れており、困っております。 — The product is delayed and we are in a difficult position.
- ご注文の内容に誤りがあり、困っております。 — There is an error in the order, which is troubling us.
- 少々困っておりますので、ご確認いただけますでしょうか。 — We are in some difficulty; could you check?

### Cultural Background

The humble おります (instead of います) lowers the speaker's status while describing their predicament. This creates space for the listener to help without feeling accused.

## ご検討いただけませんか

**Literal Translation:** "Would you consider it?"

**Meaning:** Polite suggestion that the listener reconsider a decision, course of action, or policy. A face-saving alternative to "please change this."

**Usage Context:** Polite / formal. Used when escalating a complaint to a manager, when requesting a refund exception, or when proposing an alternative to a stated policy.

**Pattern:** ご + 検討 (consideration) + いただけ (humble potential of いただく) + ませんか

### Examples

- もう一度ご検討いただけませんか。 — Could you reconsider once more?
- 別のプランもご検討いただけませんか。 — Could you consider an alternative plan?
- 割引をご検討いただけませんか。 — Could you consider a discount?

### Cultural Background

検討 (kentō) literally means "deliberation." Asking the listener to "consider" rather than "decide" preserves their authority while still conveying the complaint. This is a hallmark of Japanese consensus-building (根回し nemawashi).

## 〜について苦情を申し上げます

**Literal Translation:** "I wish to lodge a complaint regarding ~"

**Meaning:** Formal declaration of a formal complaint. Reserved for serious grievances.

**Usage Context:** Formal / business. Used in writing to corporate customer service, government offices, or institutional bodies. The phrase signals that the speaker is moving from informal complaint to formal record.

**Pattern:** Noun + について + 苦情を + 申し上げる (humble "to say")

### Examples

- 製品の品質について苦情を申し上げます。 — I wish to lodge a complaint regarding the product's quality.
- 接客の対応について苦情を申し上げます。 — I wish to complain about the service.
- 騒音について苦情を申し上げます。 — I wish to complain about the noise.

### Cultural Background

申し上げる is the highest-formality humble form of 言う (to say). Using it signals that the speaker is treating the complaint as an official action requiring documentation, often in writing.

## 問題がございまして

**Literal Translation:** "There is a problem, and ~"

**Meaning:** Soft introduction of a problem. Presents the issue as a neutral fact rather than the listener's fault.

**Usage Context:** Polite / formal. Customer service, business reports, and any situation where the speaker needs to flag an issue without confrontation.

**Pattern:** 問題 + が + ございまして (humble て-form of ございます)

### Examples

- ご注文に問題がございまして、ご連絡差し上げました。 — There is a problem with your order, so we are reaching out.
- システムに問題がございまして、現在対応中です。 — There is a system issue and we are addressing it.
- 配送に遅れの問題がございまして、お詫び申し上げます。 — There is a delivery delay; we apologize.

### Cultural Background

ございます is the humble form of ある (to exist/there is). Using it for problems is significantly softer than ある, and the て-form ございまして invites the listener to respond with empathy before the actual complaint details arrive.

## 恐れ入りますが

**Literal Translation:** "I am filled with fear/respect, but ~"

**Meaning:** Polite connector used to introduce a request or mild objection. Softens the impact of what follows.

**Usage Context:** Polite / formal. Phone calls, customer service, and any polite interruption. Often used as a slightly more deferential alternative to すみません.

**Pattern:** 恐れ入る + ます + が (connective)

### Examples

- 恐れ入りますが、もう一度お名前をお願いします。 — Pardon me, could I have your name again?
- 恐れ入りますが、本日は closed しております。 — I am sorry, but we are closed today.
- 恐れ入りますが、少々お待ちいただけますでしょうか。 — Pardon me, could you wait a moment?

### Cultural Background

恐れ入る is a humble idiom ("to be overwhelmed with deference") originally meaning "I am filled with awe." In modern usage it is a polite filler for introducing inconveniences, complaints, or requests to someone whose time is valuable.

## 失礼ですが

**Literal Translation:** "It is rude, but ~"

**Meaning:** Polite connector used when correcting someone or making an objection. Acknowledges the social weight of disagreement before stating it.

**Usage Context:** Polite. Common in meetings, customer calls, and any situation where the speaker needs to push back without hostility.

**Pattern:** 失礼 (rudeness) + です + が (connective)

### Examples

- 失礼ですが、お名前を頂戴できますか。 — Pardon, may I have your name?
- 失礼ですが、少しお待ちください。 — Excuse me, please wait a moment.
- 失礼ですが、いただいた情報に誤りがあるようです。 — Pardon me, but the information you provided appears to contain an error.

### Cultural Background

The speaker frames their objection as a violation of etiquette (失礼) on their own part, rather than accusing the listener. This is a "self-deprecating politeness" pattern common in formal speech.

## お引きたて申し訳ございませんが

**Literal Translation:** "I apologize for the imposition, but ~"

**Meaning:** Very polite framing for a complaint or counter-request. Implies "I know this is a burden."

**Usage Context:** Formal / business writing. Used when making a request that the speaker knows will inconvenience the listener, such as asking for a deadline extension or an exception to policy.

**Pattern:** お引きたて (humble imposition) + 申し訳ございません + が

### Examples

- お引きたて申し訳ございませんが、再度ご検討いただけますでしょうか。 — I apologize for the imposition, but could you reconsider?
- お引きたて申し訳ございませんが、納期を延長いただけますでしょうか。 — Sorry to impose, but could you extend the deadline?
- お引きたて申し訳ございませんが、別途ご対応いただけますと幸いです。 — Sorry to trouble you, but it would be appreciated if you could handle this separately.

### Cultural Background

お引きたて (o-hikitate) is a humble noun meaning "troubling someone." It is used primarily in formal writing to soften the speaker's complaint or request, particularly in business correspondence.

## ご一考いただけますでしょうか

**Literal Translation:** "Would you give it a thought?"

**Meaning:** Even more polite / literary alternative to ご検討いただけませんか. Implies "please think it over once more."

**Usage Context:** Formal / written. Used when a previous request has been declined and the speaker wants to re-raise it without pressure. Common in diplomatic correspondence.

**Pattern:** ご + 一考 (a thought) + いただけます + でしょうか

### Examples

- 再度ご一考いただけますでしょうか。 — Could you think it over once more?
- 別の選択肢をご一考いただけますでしょうか。 — Could you consider an alternative option?
- 当方の事情をご一考いただけますと幸いです。 — It would be appreciated if you would consider our circumstances.

### Cultural Background

一考 (ikkō) is more literary than 検討 (kentō), carrying a sense of "weighing deeply" rather than "studying options." It is appropriate when the speaker wants to add emotional weight to a reconsideration request.

## 改善をお願いできますでしょうか

**Literal Translation:** "Could I ask for an improvement?"

**Meaning:** Direct but polite complaint that explicitly asks for action. A common final step before escalation.

**Usage Context:** Formal. Used in customer complaints, vendor feedback, or institutional reports where the speaker has already tried indirect approaches.

**Pattern:** 改善 (improvement) + を + お願いする (potential) + でしょうか

### Examples

- サービス改善をお願いできますでしょうか。 — Could I request a service improvement?
- 騒音の改善をお願いできますでしょうか。 — Could I request that the noise be reduced?
- 配送時間の改善をお願いできますでしょうか。 — Could I request an improvement in delivery time?

### Cultural Background

改善 (kaizen) is a near-universal Japanese business term for "improvement," with positive connotations rooted in the kaizen philosophy of continuous improvement. Framing a complaint as 改善 invites cooperation rather than defensiveness.
