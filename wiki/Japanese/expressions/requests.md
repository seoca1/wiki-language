# requests (Japanese) expressions

> **Theme:** Daily Requests & Politeness Strategies
> **Level:** A2-B1 (with keigo escalations through B2)

**Overview:** Japanese request expressions range from casual (~て) to formal business keigo (〜いただけますでしょうか). Choosing the right level is essential because *downward* politeness errors (over-formal to a friend) sound distant, while *upward* errors (casual to a superior) are face-threatening. Each entry below marks the register (casual / polite / formal / honorific / humble) and the typical context.

---

## 〜てもらえますか

**Literal Translation:** "Can I have [you] do ~ for me?"

**Meaning:** Polite request asking someone to do something for the speaker. Uses the potential form + てもらえる (receiving form).

**Usage Context:** Polite (丁寧語 teineigo). Safe in most daily situations: coworkers, acquaintances, service encounters. Soften with すみません or 恐れ入ります at the start.

**Pattern:** Verb て-form + もらえますか / もらえますでしょうか (more formal)

### Examples

- ちょっと手伝ってもらえますか。 — Can you help me for a moment?
- これをコピーしてもらえますか。 — Could you copy this for me?
- すみません、窓を開けてもらえますでしょうか。 — Excuse me, could you open the window? (more formal)

### Cultural Background

The てもらう construction places the listener in the position of grantor, which frames the request as a small favor rather than an order. This is less face-threatening than the imperative (〜てください) when used with strangers or superiors.

## 〜てください

**Literal Translation:** "Please do ~"

**Meaning:** Standard polite request using the verb て-form. Neutral politeness suitable for everyday situations.

**Usage Context:** Polite (teineigo). Default request form for service, instructions, and casual professional exchanges. Avoid with superiors — use いただく forms instead.

**Pattern:** Verb て-form + ください

### Examples

- ちょっと待ってください。 — Please wait a moment.
- もう一度言ってください。 — Please say it again.
- ここに名前を書いてください。 — Please write your name here.

### Cultural Background

くださる is the honorific (尊敬語 sonkeigo) counterpart of くれる, marking the listener's action as worthy of respect. Using てください is the everyday default; over-reliance on it sounds blunt only in very formal business settings.

## 〜ていただけますか

**Literal Translation:** "Could you possibly do ~ for me?"

**Meaning:** Polite-to-formal request using the humble いただく form. One step above てもらえますか.

**Usage Context:** Polite / formal. Appropriate with senior coworkers, business contacts, and customers. Slightly more deferential than てもらえますか.

**Pattern:** Verb て-form + いただけますか

### Examples

- 確認していただけますか。 — Could you verify this?
- 明日までに送っていただけますか。 — Could you send it by tomorrow?
- こちらにお座りいただけますか。 — Could you have a seat here? (to a customer)

### Cultural Background

いただく is the humble (謙譲語 kenjougo) form of 食べる/飲む, extended to mean "to receive." Using いただく for requests elevates the listener's action while lowering the speaker's expectation.

## 〜ていただけますでしょうか

**Literal Translation:** "Would it be possible for you to do ~?"

**Meaning:** Formal business request. The でしょうか softening adds a layer of indirectness.

**Usage Context:** Formal / business. Email requests to clients, senior management, or people you have just met. Common in written business Japanese.

**Pattern:** Verb て-form + いただけますでしょうか

### Examples

- ご検討いただけますでしょうか。 — Would you consider it?
- ご都合がよろしければ、ご出席いただけますでしょうか。 — If it's convenient, could you attend?
- 至急ご確認いただけますでしょうか。 — Could you check this urgently?

### Cultural Background

The full construction (いただけます + でしょう + か) is one of the safest high-formality requests in business Japanese. It implies "if it would not be too much trouble" without explicitly saying so.

## お願いしてもいいですか

**Literal Translation:** "May I make a request?"

**Meaning:** Soft opener for a request. Signals "I am about to ask you something" before stating the actual ask.

**Usage Context:** Polite. Common in face-to-face conversation with peers, classmates, and coworkers. Pairs naturally with a follow-up sentence containing the actual request.

**Pattern:** Verb てもいい form of お願いする + か

### Examples

- お願いしてもいいですか。 — May I ask you something?
- 少し遅れてもよろしいですか、お願いしてもいいですか。 — Can I ask if I can be a little late?
- 一つお願いしてもいいですか。 — Can I ask one favor?

### Cultural Background

願い (negai) literally means "wish." Framing a request as a "wish" rather than a demand preserves the listener's freedom to decline, which is central to Japanese politeness (配慮 hairyo).

## ご都合はいかがでしょうか

**Literal Translation:** "How is your convenience?"

**Meaning:** Indirect way to ask about someone's availability or preferences without forcing a yes/no answer.

**Usage Context:** Polite / formal. Used when proposing a meeting time, asking for a favor, or offering choices. Common in business scheduling.

**Pattern:** ご + 都合 (convenience) + は + いかがでしょうか

### Examples

- 来週のご都合はいかがでしょうか。 — How does next week look for you?
- ご都合はいかがでしょうか、お時間ございますか。 — Are you available?
- 土曜日か日曜日のご都合はいかがでしょうか。 — Would Saturday or Sunday work for you?

### Cultural Background

This phrase embodies the "answer space" (回答余地) principle: the listener can reply with their actual schedule without feeling cornered. Direct questions like "来週会えますか" can feel pressuring.

## お時間よろしいですか

**Literal Translation:** "Is your time okay?"

**Meaning:** Soft way to check if someone is free to talk. The polite お + よろしい construction avoids the blunt いい.

**Usage Context:** Polite. Phone calls, casual office check-ins, and service encounters (e.g., approaching a busy clerk).

**Pattern:** お時間 (honorific time) + よろしい (polite good) + です + か

### Examples

- お時間よろしいですか。 — Do you have a moment?
- すみません、お時間よろしいでしょうか。 — Excuse me, do you have a moment? (more polite)
- 今からお時間よろしいですか。 — Do you have time from now?

### Cultural Background

Using お (honorific prefix) and よろしい (polite form of いい) instead of the plain word signals consideration for the listener's time. It also gives the listener a graceful exit ("I'm sorry, I'm busy right now").

## 〜いただければ幸いです

**Literal Translation:** "I would be grateful if you could ~"

**Meaning:** Humble, slightly literary request. Emphasizes that the favor would be a boon to the speaker.

**Usage Context:** Formal / written. Business emails, especially to clients or external partners. Pairs with ます forms of いただく.

**Pattern:** Verb ial form (ければ / て) + いただければ + 幸いです

### Examples

- ご確認いただければ幸いです。 — I would appreciate your confirmation.
- 早めにご返信いただければ幸いに存じます。 — I would be grateful for an early reply.
- ご理解いただければ幸いです。 — I would appreciate your understanding.

### Cultural Background

幸い (saiwai, "happiness/fortune") is humble (謙譲語) vocabulary: the speaker positions their need as small and the listener's help as fortunate. Often paired with 存じます for extra humility.

## 〜いただければ幸いに存じます

**Literal Translation:** "I would deem it fortunate if you could ~"

**Meaning:** Highest-formality humble request. 存じます is the humble form of 思う (to think).

**Usage Context:** Formal / business writing. Reserved for situations where extra humility is needed: first contact with clients, requests involving large favors, apologies with action items.

**Pattern:** Verb て-form + いただければ + 幸いに + 存じます

### Examples

- ご検討いただければ幸いに存じます。 — I would be grateful for your consideration.
- ご指導いただければ幸いに存じます。 — I would be honored by your guidance.
- お力添えいただければ幸いに存じます。 — Your assistance would be greatly appreciated.

### Cultural Background

The triple stack — いただく (humble receive) + 幸い (humble noun) + 存じます (humble think) — makes this one of the most respectful request formulas. Overusing it in casual contexts can sound stiff or sarcastic.

## お願いできますでしょうか

**Literal Translation:** "Would it be possible to make a request?"

**Meaning:** Formal request framing, slightly more deferential than お願いしてもいいですか. Implies "if I may be so bold."

**Usage Context:** Formal. Business meetings, polite email openings, and situations where you want to ask something potentially inconvenient.

**Pattern:** お願いする (potential できる) + でしょうか

### Examples

- 一つお願いできますでしょうか。 — May I make one request?
- お名前をお願いできますでしょうか。 — Could I have your name?
- ご注文の確認をお願いできますでしょうか。 — Could I confirm your order?

### Cultural Background

The potential form (できる) and でしょうか softening convert a direct ask ("お願いしたい") into a hypothetical "would it be possible" — letting the listener decline without explicit refusal.
