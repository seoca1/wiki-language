# Mood systems across languages (cross-linguistic comparison)
**Last updated**: 2026-08-10 (5-language matrix expansion)

**Scope:** Indicative vs. subjunctive vs. modal comparison across 5 languages.
**Languages compared:** Spanish, English, Japanese, Korean, Chinese.
**Theme:** mood-grammar, verbal-modality, cross-linguistic-typology.

## Overview

Languages differ in how they encode **mood** (the speaker's attitude toward the proposition). The Spanish/Italian/French **subjunctive** system is particularly rich; English uses modal auxiliaries (may, would, should); Japanese and Korean use verb conjugations and sentence-final particles; Chinese relies on modal adverbs.

## Mood system comparison

| Language | Indicative | Subjunctive/Modal | Imperative | Conditional |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | regular conjugations | subjuntivo (que-clauses) | imperative (tú/usted/vosotros) | condicional |
| English | regular | may/might/would/should | bare infinitive | would |
| Japanese | 常体 (jōtai) | 命令形 (meirei-kei) + たら/ば/と | 命令形 | ば-form |
| Korean | 평서문 | -도록/-다면 | 명령문 | -(으)면 |
| Chinese | 陈述句 | modal adverbs (会, 要) | bare verb | 会/要 + verb |

## Mood Types Across Languages

| Mood Type | English | Spanish | Japanese | Korean | Chinese |
|-----------|---------|---------|----------|--------|---------|
| **Indicative (statement)** | I go | Yo voy | 行く (iku) | 간다 (ganda) | 我去 (wǒ qù) |
| **Subjunctive (doubt/wish)** | If I were | Si fuera | だったら (dattara) | -(이)라면 | 如果 (rúguǒ) |
| **Imperative (command)** | Go! | ¡Ve! | 行け (ike) | 가! (ga!) | 去! (qù!) |
| **Conditional (if-then)** | If I go, ... | Si voy, ... | 行くなら (iku nara) | -(으)면 | 如果...就 (rúguǒ...jiù) |
| **Jussive (let's)** | Let's go | ¡Vamos! | しよう (shiyō) | 가자 (gaja) | 我们走 (wǒmen zǒu) |
| **Optative (wish)** | If only... | Ojalá... | ほしい (hoshii) | -(으)면 좋겠다 | 要...就好了 (yào...jiù hǎo le) |
| **Hortative (urge)** | May he... | Que venga... | 来い (koi) | -아/어라 | 愿... (yuàn...) |
| **Potential (can/may)** | He can go | Puede ir | 行ける (ikeru) | 갈 수 있다 (gal su itda) | 能去 (néng qù) |

## Per-Language Detail

### English
- **Key patterns**: Modal auxiliaries (can/could/may/might/will/would/should/must)
- **Examples**: "If I were rich, I would travel" — past subjunctive "were" + conditional "would"
- **Notes**: English has only 2-3 productive subjunctive forms (were, be); modal auxiliaries do most "mood" work

### Spanish
- **Key patterns**: Subjuntivo (-ra, -se endings) for doubt/wish/emotion; Imperativo for commands (tú/usted/vosotros forms)
- **Examples**: "Quiero que **vengas**" (I want you to come) — subjunctive after "que"
- **Notes**: Spanish is the only language in the set with a true morphological subjunctive; most common errors from English speakers

### Japanese
- **Key patterns**: Verb conjugation forms (辞書形/ます形/て形/た形/ない形/意向形/命令形/可能形/仮定形)
- **Examples**: "行け" (ike — go!); "行ったら" (ittara — if [you] go)
- **Notes**: Japanese uses 仮定形 (katei-kei) for conditional; 意向形 (ikō-kei) for volitional

### Korean
- **Key patterns**: Speech levels (해체/해요체/합쇼체/하소서체) carry mood/respect; verb endings -(으)면/-(으)라면/도록
- **Examples**: "가자" (gaja — let's go); "간다면" (gandamyeon — if I go)
- **Notes**: Speech level system partially overlaps with mood — politeness is encoded in verb form

### Chinese
- **Key patterns**: Modal adverbs (会/要/能/可以/应该/必须/敢); sentence-final particles (吧/呢/吗)
- **Examples**: "去" (qù — go!); "要去" (yào qù — want to go / should go)
- **Notes**: Chinese is "modal-poor" — uses adverbs and context rather than inflections

## Cross-Language Mood Patterns

| Pattern | EN | ES | JP | KR | CH |
|---------|----|----|----|----|-----|
| **Wish/hope** | I wish I were | Ojalá fuera | だったらなあ | -(으)면 좋겠다 | 要...就好了 |
| **Hypothetical past** | If I had... | Si hubiera... | だったら... | -(었)다면 | 如果...就 |
| **Probability** | might/should | quizás/debería | かもしれない | -을 수도 있다 | 也许/应该 |
| **Hearsay** | supposedly | se dice que | そうだ | -(다)고 하다 | 据说/听说 |
| **Hypothesis** | if I were | si fuera | としたら | -(으)면 | 如果...的话 |

## Key contrasts (Synthesis)

| Contrast | Insight |
|----------|---------|
| **Spanish subjunctive** maps to **English modal** + **Japanese conditional** + **Korean mood suffix** + **Chinese modal adverb** |
| All 5 languages distinguish imperative for 2nd person; some (Japanese, Korean) have more politeness levels |
| Spanish is the only language in the set with a true subjunctive mood inflection; others use analytic constructions |
| Mood and politeness overlap: Korean/Japanese politeness (경어법/敬語) partially serves mood function |
| Chinese uses adverbs/word order rather than inflections — "mood-poor" but contextually rich |

## Common Learner Errors

| Error | Languages | Why |
|-------|-----------|-----|
| Using indicative where subjunctive needed | EN→ES | English "if I were" doesn't trigger Spanish subjunctive in learners |
| Overusing potential form | JP→KR/KR→JP | Both have potential but used differently |
| Mixing speech levels | All→KR/JP | Politeness systems in CJK are stricter than EN/ES |
| Wrong conditional form | All | Each language has 2-4 conditional forms (real/unreal/past) |
| Modal verb confusion | All | Many languages have 6+ modal verbs (can/could/may/might/will/would) |
