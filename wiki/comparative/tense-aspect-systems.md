# Tense-aspect systems across languages (cross-linguistic comparison)
**Last updated**: 2026-08-10 (5-language matrix expansion)

**Scope:** Tense and aspect encoding across 5 languages.
**Languages compared:** Spanish, English, Japanese, Korean, Chinese.
**Theme:** tense-morphology, aspect-encoding, cross-linguistic-typology.

## Overview

Languages differ in how they encode **tense** (when an event occurs) and **aspect** (whether an event is ongoing, completed, or habitual). The 5 languages in this wiki exhibit a full spectrum from richly-inflected (Spanish) to aspect-light (Chinese).

## Tense system comparison

| Language | Past | Present | Future | Aspect distinction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | preterite/imperfect/perfect/pluperfect | present | future/conditional | rich (perfective/imperfective) |
| English | simple past/present perfect/pluperfect | present | will + verb | simple (perfective only) |
| Japanese | た形/ていた/ていた/てしまう | 常体/辞等 | ます形/だろう | very rich (完了, 経験, 進行) |
| Korean | 았/었/었었/겠 | 다/는다 | 겠/을 것 | rich (完了, 経験, 進行, 持続) |
| Chinese | 了/过/曾经 | present | 会/要/将 | minimal (了=completed, 过=experienced) |

|--------|---------|---------|----------|--------|---------|

## Per-Language Detail

### English
- **Tense morphology**: 2 tenses (past/present) + future via modal
- **Aspect morphology**: Limited; progressive via "be + -ing"; perfect via "have + past participle"
- **Examples**: "I eat" (present), "I ate" (past simple), "I have eaten" (present perfect)
- **Notes**: Tense-light, aspect-light; relies on adverbs and context

### Spanish
- **Tense morphology**: 3 tenses (preterite/imperfect/future) + conditional + pluperfect + future perfect
- **Aspect morphology**: Built into tenses (preterite = perfective, imperfect = imperfective)
- **Examples**: "Como" (present), "Comí" (preterite), "Comía" (imperfect), "He comido" (present perfect)
- **Notes**: Most complex tense system among the 5; common errors from English speakers with preterite vs imperfect

### Japanese
- **Tense morphology**: 2 tenses (past/present-future unmarked) + だろう for future
- **Aspect morphology**: VERY rich — ている (progressive), てしまう (completive), てある (resultative), ことがある (experiential)
- **Examples**: "食べる" (present/future), "食べた" (past), "食べている" (progressive), "食べたことがある" (experiential)
- **Notes**: Tense-poor, aspect-rich — key distinction from English/Spanish

### Korean
- **Tense morphology**: 2 tenses (past 았/었/겠 + present 다/는다) + future via -(으)ㄹ 것이다
- **Aspect morphology**: Rich — 고 있다 (progressive), 았/었 (perfect), 어 보다 (experiential)
- **Examples**: "먹다" (present), "먹었다" (past), "먹고 있다" (progressive)
- **Notes**: Like Japanese — tense-poor, aspect-rich; combines with speech levels

### Chinese
- **Tense morphology**: NONE — no verb inflection for tense
- **Aspect morphology**: Aspect particles (了=completed, 过=experiential, 在=progressive) + modal verbs
- **Examples**: "我吃" (present/future), "我吃了" (past/perfect), "我在吃" (progressive)
- **Notes**: Most aspect-light among the 5 languages; relies on particles and word order

## Key contrasts (Synthesis)

| Contrast | Insight |
|----------|---------|
| **Spanish** has both past perfective (pretérito) and past imperfective (imperfecto) — uniquely detailed past tense system |
| **Japanese and Korean** are tense-light but aspect-rich — they use verb conjugations to encode completed (完了) vs ongoing (進行) vs experienced (経験) states |
| **Chinese** is the most minimal — relies on aspect particles (了, 过) and modal verbs (会, 要) rather than verb inflection |
| Romance vs CJK | Romance languages (Spanish) have tense morphology; CJK languages (JP/KR) have aspect morphology; Chinese is most minimal |
| East Asian aspect | JP and KR share similar aspect systems (完了/経験/進行); they often use similar forms for these concepts |

## Common Learner Errors

| Error | Languages | Why |
|-------|-----------|-----|
| Forgetting preterite vs imperfect | EN→ES | English only has simple past |
| Overusing progressive | All→JP/KR | East Asian aspect is aspect-rich, not tense-based |
| Using 了 for everything | All→CH | 了 is aspect, not tense — overuse confuses time vs completion |
| Mixing tense markers | JP→KR | Both have past markers but different usages |
| Confusing past simple and present perfect | All→EN | "I ate" vs "I have eaten" — Chinese/Japanese have simpler systems |
