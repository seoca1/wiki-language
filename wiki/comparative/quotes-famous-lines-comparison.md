## Translation Strategies

| Strategy | English | Spanish | Japanese | Korean | Chinese |
|----------|---------|---------|----------|--------|---------|
| **Literal translation** | Common | Common | Possible (literal) | Possible | Possible |
| **Adaptation (preserve meaning)** | Standard | Often (cultural context) | Common (anime subs) | Common (drama subs) | Common |
| **Localization (replace with native phrase)** | Sometimes | Sometimes | Very common | Very common | Sometimes |
| **Subtitle conventions** | Original + localized | Subtitulado | 字幕 (jimaku) | 자막 (jamak) | 字幕 (zìmù) |

## Per-Language Detail

### English
- **Key patterns**: Direct quotation common; famous lines often kept verbatim in translation
- **Sources**: `[[quotes-vocabulary]]` (movie-quotes source)

### Spanish
- **Key patterns**: Telenovela dialogues highly stylized; "¡Ay, mi amor!" stereotypical
- **Cultural adaptation**: Often uses Spanish idioms replacing English originals
- **Sources**: N/A — Spanish lacks explicit quotes theme yet

### Japanese
- **Key patterns**: 字幕 (jimaku = subtitles) translate cultural references to JP equivalents
- **Translation notes**: "I'll be back" often rendered as adaptation, not direct; 言った/言われた (itta/iwareta) for quote framing
- **Sources**: `[[quotes-vocabulary]]`

### Korean
- **Key patterns**: Drama subtitle conventions; K-drama clichés like "도망쳐!" (run away!)
- **Cultural notes**: K-pop/K-drama quote exports growing (BTS, Blackpink)
- **Sources**: N/A — Korean lacks explicit quotes theme yet

### Chinese

- **Cultural notes**: 古文 (classical Chinese) quotations still influential

| Contrast | Insight |
