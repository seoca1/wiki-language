## Per-Language Detail

### English
- **Key terms**: "Trail" (marked path), "Summit" (reached peak), "Base camp" (start point)
- **Patterns**: "Go hiking" / "Go camping" — verb + gerund common
- **Sources**: `[[adventure-vocabulary]]`, `[[travel-essentials]]`

### Spanish
- **Key terms**: "Senderismo" (Spain), "Andinismo" (Andes region); "Refugio" = mountain hut
- **Patterns**: -ismo nouns; "hacer senderismo" = to go hiking
- **Sources**: N/A — Spanish lacks adventure theme yet

### Japanese
- **Key terms**: 登山 (tozan = mountain climbing), 縦走 (jūsō = ridge traverse), 山小屋 (yamagoya = mountain hut)
- **Patterns**: Verb: 〜をする; honorific: 気をつけて (ki wo tsukete = take care)
- **Sources**: `[[adventure-vocabulary]]`

### Korean
- **Key terms**: 등산 (deungsan), 등산로 (deungsanno), 등산객 (deungsangaek = hiker)
- **Patterns**: Verb: 〜을/를 가다 (go do); 산 (san) appears in many compound words
- **Sources**: `[[adventure-vocabulary]]` (currently EN/JP only — KR planned)

### Chinese
- **Key terms**: 登山 (dēngshān), 徒步 (túbù), 户外 (hùwài = outdoor)
- **Patterns**: Verb: 〜去 (qù = go); measure word: 座山 (zuò shān = mountains classifier)
- **Sources**: N/A — Chinese lacks adventure theme yet

## Key Contrasts (Synthesis)

| Contrast | Insight |
|----------|---------|
| **Mountain culture** | KR has strongest modern hiking culture; JP has Mt. Fuji pilgrimage tradition; CN has Buddhist mountain heritage |
| **Terminology** | All languages have borrowed "trekking", "hiking", "camping" — basic outdoor vocabulary international |
| **Verb patterns** | EN: verb+ing; ES: hacer+noun; JP/KR/ZH: noun+auxiliary verb |
| **Emergency vocab** | 助けて/도와주세요/救命 (call for help) — common pattern across all 5 languages |

## Quick Reference Card

> **Outdoor safety phrases**:
> - "助けて" (tasukete) / "도와주세요" (dowa juseyo) / "救命" (jiùmìng) = HELP
