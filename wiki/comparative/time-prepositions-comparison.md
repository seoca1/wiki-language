**Languages**: English · Spanish · Japanese · Korean · Chinese
**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

> How each language marks time-of-day, days, and spatial/temporal location using prepositions or particles.

## Japanese Particles — Time & Location

| Particle | Function | Example | Meaning |
|----------|----------|---------|---------|
| **に (ni)** | Time point, destination, indirect object | 7時に (shichiji ni) | "at 7 o'clock" |
| **で (de)** | Location of action, means/instrument | 公園で遊ぶ (kōen de asobu) | "play at the park" |
| **を (o)** | Direct object | 本を読む (hon o yomu) | "read a book" |
| **へ (e)** | Direction/toward | 東京へ行く (Tōkyō e iku) | "go to Tokyo" |
| **から (kara)** | From (time or place) | 9時から (kuji kara) | "from 9 o'clock" |
| **まで (made)** | Until / up to | 5時まで (goji made) | "until 5 o'clock" |
| **の (no)** | Possessive, "of" | 私の (watashi no) | "my" |

## Per-Language Detail

### English
- **Key terms**: at, in, on, by, from, to, with, without, before, after, during
- **Patterns**: Prepositions precede noun; same form regardless of case; time/place distinctions are rigid (at/in/on)
- **Sources**: `[[wiki/English/grammar/prepositions]]`

### Spanish
- **Key terms**: a, de, en, con, por, para, desde, hasta, sin, sobre
- **Patterns**: Por vs para distinction (means vs purpose); a + el = al; de + el = del
- **Regional variations**: Spain uses "desde" for "since"; some regions prefer "de" for "from"
- **Sources**: `[[wiki/Spanish/grammar/prepositions]]`

### Japanese
- **Key terms**: に (ni), で (de), を (o), へ (e), から (kara), まで (made), の (no)
- **Patterns**: Postpositions follow noun; に marks existence point, で marks action location
- **Keigo / politeness level**: Particles are neutral; politeness comes from verb endings
- **Sources**: `[[wiki/Japanese/grammar/particles]]`

### Korean
- **Key terms**: 에 (e), 에서 (eseo), 로/으로 (ro/euro), 를/을 (reul/eul), 의 (ui), 부터, 까지
- **Patterns**: 에 = static location/time point; 에서 = action location or "from"; particle choice changes meaning
- **Speech level**: Particles are neutral; verb endings carry politeness
- **Sources**: `[[wiki/Korean/grammar/particles]]`

### Chinese
- **Key terms**: 在 (zài), 从 (cóng), 到 (dào), 给 (gěi), 用 (yòng), 跟 (gēn)
- **Patterns**: Prepositions precede noun; no inflection; verbs (在/到/给) double as prepositions
- **Register / honorifics**: 您 (nín) honorific in pronoun position; particles are neutral
- **Sources**: `[[wiki/Chinese/grammar/prepositions-zh]]`

## Key Contrasts (Synthesis)

| Contrast | Implication for Learners |
|----------|--------------------------|
| **Pre vs post** | EN/ES/CH use prepositions (before noun); JP/KR use postpositions (after noun) — opposite logic |
| **Static vs action** | JP/KR distinguish に/에서 and 에/에서; EN collapses both to "in/at" |
| **Time particles** | EN "at/in/on" must be memorized; JP/KR/CH reuse spatial logic for time |
| **Means/instrument** | EN "by"; ES "en"; JP/KR "で/로"; CH "用 (yòng)" — all distinct forms |
