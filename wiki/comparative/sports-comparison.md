## Per-Language Detail

### English
- **Key terms**: "Game" vs "Match" — Game = scheduled event, Match = head-to-head
- **Patterns**: Strong distinction between "sport" (organized competitive) and "recreation" (leisure activity)
- **Register notes**: Casual — "Let's ball" (informal for "let's play"); Formal — "shall we engage in athletic activity"
- **Sources**: `[[sports-vocabulary]]`

### Spanish
- **Key terms**: "Fútbol" (universal term); "Fútbol americano" = American football, "Fútbol sala" = futsal
- **Patterns**: -ar verb conjugation for sports (jugar, practicar, entrenar)
- **Regional variations**: Spain "fútbol" = LatAm "fútbol" (consistent); "guante" (glove) for boxing varies by country
- **Sources**: `[[sports-and-hobbies]]`

### Japanese
- **Key terms**: スポーツ vs 運動 (undō = exercise/physical activity); 部活 (bukatsu = club activities)
- **Patterns**: Verb endings: 〜する (suru = to do), 〜をやる (wo yaru = to play)
- **Keigo / politeness level**: Casual — "野球やる?" (yakyū yaru?); Polite — "野球をなさいますか?"; Humble — "野球をいたします"
- **Sources**: `[[sports-vocabulary]]`

### Korean
- **Key terms**: 체육 (cheyuk = physical education) vs 스포츠 (seupocheu = sport)
- **Patterns**: Verb: 〜하다 (hada = to do), 〜을 하다 (eul hada = object+do)
- **Speech level**: Casual — "야구해?" (yaguhae?); Polite — "야구를 해요" (yagueul haeyo); Formal — "야구를 합니다" (yagueul hamnida)
- **Sources**: `[[sports-vocabulary]]`

### Chinese
- **Key terms**: 体育 (tǐyù = physical education) vs 运动 (yùndòng = sport/exercise)
- **Patterns**: Verb: 〜进行 (jìnxíng = to carry out), 〜打 (dǎ = to play hit sports)
- **Register / honorifics**: Casual — "打球吗?" (dǎ qiú ma?); Polite — "我们一起打球吧" (wǒmen yìqǐ dǎ qiú ba)
- **Sources**: N/A — Chinese wiki lacks sports theme yet

## Key Contrasts (Synthesis)

| Contrast | Insight |
|----------|---------|
| **Borrowing patterns** | All languages borrow English sports terms ("baseball", "basketball", "golf") — but JP/KR add native readings, ZH uses 棒球/篮球/golf transliterations |
| **Verbal expression** | EN uses light verbs ("play", "do"); ES uses -ar conjugations; JP/KR/ZH use noun+auxiliary verb (をやる/을 하다/打) |
| **Cultural weight** | Sports carry strong cultural identity — soccer in LatAm, baseball in JP/KR, basketball in CN — different sports = different cultural anchors |
| **School integration** | JP/KR have mandatory 部活/체육 in schools; EN/CN less integrated into formal education |
