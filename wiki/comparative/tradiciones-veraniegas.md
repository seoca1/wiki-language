# Tradiciones veraniegas (cross-cultural comparison)
**Last updated**: 2026-08-10

**Scope:** Comparison of summer/siesta traditions across 5+ languages.
**Period:** Summer months (June–August, Northern Hemisphere).
**Theme:** seasonal-rest, leisure, temperature-adaptation.

## Overview

The summer siesta or equivalent pause varies across cultures. This page compares the Spanish **siesta** with Italian **riposo**, Japanese **hirune** (siesta), Korean **낮잠 (natjam)**, and Anglophone **power nap**. All share a common function: thermoregulation during peak daytime heat, but each culture has adapted the practice differently.

## Traditions by culture

| Culture | Practice | Duration | Active hours | Status (2026) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spain | Siesta | 90-120 min | 14:00–17:00 | Declining in cities, alive in pueblos |
| Italy | Riposo | 60-90 min | 13:00–15:00 | Mostly discontinued in north, alive in south |
| Japan | Hirune (昼寝) | 15-30 min | 12:00–15:00 | Common in offices (inemuri 文化) |
| Korea | 낮잠 (natjam) | 20-30 min | 12:00–14:00 | Common in children, rarer in adults |
| Anglophone | Power nap | 10-20 min | 13:00–15:00 | Increasing since 2000s corporate wellness movement |

## Cross-cultural patterns

- **Heat as trigger**: All traditions peak in July-August when midday temperatures exceed 30°C.
- **Post-meal timing**: All coincide with the post-prandial dip (natural circadian low).
- **Urban decline**: All show steepest decline in capital cities (Madrid, Tokyo, Seoul, NYC) but persistence in rural areas.
- **Economic function**: All historically allowed agricultural workers to resume labor in cooler evening hours.
