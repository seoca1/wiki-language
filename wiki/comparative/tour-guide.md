#### Learning Chinese

## Paths by Starting Language

### English Speakers (L1) → Other Languages

**Biggest surprises**:
1. **Spanish**: Look similar but false friends everywhere → `[[confusion-hotspots]]`
2. **Japanese**: Completely different pronoun system → `[[pronouns-reference]]`
3. **Korean**: Speech levels matter more than grammar → `[[politeness-honorifics]]`
4. **Chinese**: Tones are non-negotiable → `[[pronunciation-challenges]]`

**Suggested order**: Master cheatsheet → greetings → politeness → confusion-hotspots

### Spanish Speakers → Other Languages

**Biggest surprises**:
1. **English**: Tense-based vs Spanish aspect-based
2. **Japanese/Korean**: SOV word order, particles, no articles
3. **Chinese**: Tones, measure words, no gender

**Suggested order**: Master cheatsheet → writing-systems → politeness-honorifics

### Japanese Speakers → Other Languages

**Biggest surprises**:
1. **English/Korean**: Different honorific systems
2. **Spanish**: Gender agreement (none in JP)
3. **Chinese**: Tones (JP has pitch accent, not tones)
4. **Korean**: Similar to JP grammatically but DIFFERENT politics

**Suggested order**: Master cheatsheet → cultural-values → writing-systems

### Korean Speakers → Other Languages

**Biggest surprises**:
1. **English/Spanish**: No speech levels
2. **Japanese**: Different honorific system (keigo)
3. **Chinese**: Tones, no speech levels (Chinese has 您 instead)

**Suggested order**: Master cheatsheet → politeness-honorifics → confusion-hotspots

### Chinese Speakers → Other Languages

**Biggest surprises**:
1. **English/Spanish**: Tense system (Chinese has aspect, not tense)
2. **Japanese/Korean**: SOV order, particles
3. **Tones**: Most unique feature of Chinese

**Suggested order**: Master cheatsheet → writing-systems → grammar-difficulty-map
