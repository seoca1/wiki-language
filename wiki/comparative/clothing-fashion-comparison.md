## Per-Language Detail

### English
- **Key terms**: "Apparel" (formal), "Clothes" (general), "Outfit" (specific ensemble)
- **Patterns**: "Wear" + noun; "Dress" as verb vs noun (different meanings)
- **Sources**: `[[clothing-vocabulary]]`

### Spanish
- **Key terms**: "Ropa" (general clothing), "Indumentaria" (formal/industry)
- **Patterns**: -ar verb: usar (to wear); reflexive: "vestirse" (to dress oneself)
- **Sources**: `[[clothing-vocabulary]]` (theme exists but limited content)

### Japanese
- **Key terms**: 服 (fuku = clothes), 服飾 (fukushoku = apparel), 衣類 (irui = garments)
- **Patterns**: Verb: 〜を着る (wo kiru); Verb: 〜を履く (wo haku = to wear below waist)
- **Sources**: `[[clothing-vocabulary]]`

### Korean
- **Key terms**: 옷 (ot = clothes), 의류 (eoryu = clothing/apparel), 한복 (hanbok = traditional)
- **Patterns**: Verb: 입다 (ipda = to wear above waist); 신다 (sinda = to wear footwear); 쓰다 (sseuda = to wear on head)
- **Sources**: `[[clothing-vocabulary]]`

### Chinese
- **Key terms**: 衣服 (yīfu), 服装 (fúzhuāng = clothing), 汉服 (hànfú)
- **Patterns**: Verb: 穿 (chuān); measure word: 件 (jiàn) for garments
- **Sources**: N/A — Chinese lacks clothing theme yet

## Key Contrasts (Synthesis)

| Contrast | Insight |
|----------|---------|
| **Verb distinctions** | JP/KR have separate verbs for upper/lower/footwear wear; EN/ES/ZH use single "wear/穿" verb |
| **Traditional dress** | JP/KR/ZH all preserve distinct traditional garments (kimono/hanbok/hàn fú) — modern fashion more globalized |
| **Borrowed terms** | All languages adopt "jeans" (ジーンズ/청바지/牛仔裤) — but with different translations |
| **Size/color** | EN/ES use Latin words; JP/KR/ZH use native or 한자/汉字 terms |
