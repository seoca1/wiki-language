# Emotions — Cross-Language Comparison

**Languages**: English · Spanish · Japanese · Korean · Chinese
**Last updated**: 2026-07-19

> How 5 languages encode emotional states. Each culture expresses happiness, sadness, anger, fear, and love differently — from word choice to politeness norms.

## Per-Language Detail

### English

**Verb forms**:
- **Be + adjective**: I am happy / sad / angry
- **Feel + adjective**: I feel sad / anxious / happy

**Intensity adverbs**:
- **Very + adj**: very happy, very tired
- **So/Really + adj**: so tired, really happy
- **Adjective + -er/-est**: happier (comparative), happiest (superlative)

**Common phrases**:
- **"I'm feeling..."** — softer, more reflective
- **"I'm so..."** — casual emphasis
- **"I get..."** — habitual (I get nervous)

### Spanish

**Verb forms**:
- **Estar + adjective** (temporary states): Estoy feliz = "I'm happy"
- **Ser + adjective** (permanent): Soy nervioso = "I'm a nervous person"
- **Sentir + noun**: Siento alegría = "I feel joy"

**Intensity**:
- **Muy + adj**: muy feliz
- **-ísimo/a**: contentísimo = "very happy"
- **Tan + adj**: tan cansado = "so tired"

**Regional variations**:
- **Spain**: agobiado (overwhelmed)
- **LatAm**: estresado (stressed)
- **Argentina**: al horno (famished / "in the oven")
- **Mexico**: bien contento (very happy)

### Japanese

**Verb forms**:
- **Adjective (i-adj/na-adj) + です**: 嬉しいです (Ureshii desu)
- **Verb + たい**: 食べたい = "I want to eat" (desire)
- **Adjective + なる**: 寒くなる = "It becomes cold"

**Adjective types**:
- **i-adjectives**: 嬉しい (ii-ending): 嬉しい, 楽しい, 悲しい, 辛い
- **na-adjectives**: 好き (na-ending): 好き, 嫌い, 綺麗

**Special emotional expressions**:
- **〜たい (tai)**: Want to do X
- **〜がる (garu)**: Show emotion externally (3rd person): 怖がっている
- **寂しい (sabishii)**: Lonely — uniquely Japanese emotional concept

### Korean

**Verb forms**:
- **Adjective + -다 (da)**: 기쁘다 = "is happy" (declarative)
- **Adjective + -어요 (eoyo)**: 기뻐요 = "am happy" (polite)
- **Adjective + -ㅂ니다 (seumnida)**: 기쁩니다 = "am happy" (formal)
- **Noun + 이/가 + adjective**: 슬프다, 좋다

**Special emotional expressions**:
- **정 (jeong)**: Deep relational bond (no English equivalent)
- **눈치 (nunchi)**: Social awareness (no English equivalent)
- **한 (han)**: Collective sorrow (cultural concept)
- **그리움 (gereum)**: Longing/missing (not just missing — emotional state)

**Emotional intensity**:
- **너무 (neomu)**: Too much
- **진짜 (jinjja)**: Really
- **완전 (wanjeon)**: Completely

### Chinese

**Verb forms**:
- **Adjective + 了 (le)**: 高兴了 = "became happy" (state change)
- **很 + adjective**: 很高兴 = "very happy"
- **不 + adjective**: 不高兴 = "not happy"

**Special emotional expressions**:
- **舒服 (shūfu)**: Comfortable (physical + emotional)
- **累 (lèi)**: Tired (physical + mental)
- **羡慕 (xiànmù)**: Envious (different from 嫉妒 "jealous")
- **开心 (kāixīn)**: Happy (open heart)
- **伤心 (shāngxīn)**: Heart-broken/sad
- **担心 (dānxīn)**: Worried (literally "worry heart")

**Verb particles**:
- **了 (le)**: State change
- **过 (guò)**: Past experience
- **着 (zhe)**: Continuous state

## Cultural Display Rules

### Cultural Display Rules (面子, Nunchi, etc.)

| Culture | Display Rule | Example |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| **English (US)** | Moderate expression OK | "I'm sad" direct OK |
| **English (UK)** | Understatement ("I'm a bit tired" = exhausted) | "Not bad" = good |
| **Spanish** | Expressive OK | "¡Estoy loco!" (I'm crazy!) = "I'm very frustrated!" |
| **Japanese** | 謙遜 (kenson) — understatement | "大丈夫です" (daijōbu = "I'm OK") = may mean stressed |
| **Korean** | 눈치 + 정 — restraint with warmth | "괜찮아요" (gwaenchanayo) = "I'm OK" (may mean not OK) |
| **Chinese** | 面子 (miànzi) — face-saving | "还行" (hái xíng) = "It's OK" (may mean not great) |

### Social Context Differences

| Emotion | US Direct | UK Understatement | JP/KR Restraint | CN Pragmatic |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Disagreement** | "No" | "I'm not sure" | "難しいです" / "어려워요" | "不太方便" (not convenient) |
| **Tired** | "I'm exhausted" | "I'm a bit tired" | "疲れた" / "피곤해" (honest) | "有点累" (a bit) |
| **Sad** | "I'm really sad" | "I'm a bit down" | Rarely expressed | "心情不好" (mood not good) |
| **Disgust** | "That's gross" | "Not my cup of tea" | "嫌だ" / "싫어" | "不喜欢" |
| **Love** | "I love you" | "I love you" (sometimes understated) | "好き" / "愛してる" (rare in early stages) | "我爱你" (direct) |
| **Anger** | "I'm so mad" | "I'm a bit upset" | "怒ってる" (honest) | "我很生气" (direct) |

## Expressing Love & Affection

### "I Love You" Across Languages

| Language | Casual | Standard | Intense | Cultural Note |
|----------|--------|----------|---------|---------------|
| **EN (US)** | "I like you a lot" | "I love you" | "I love you so much" | Direct, early |
| **EN (UK)** | "I really like you" | "I love you" (less often) | "I love you with all my heart" | Reserved |
| **ES** | "Me gustas mucho" | "Te quiero" (love) / "Te amo" (deep love) | "Te amo con todo mi corazón" | Te quiero = family/partner, Te amo = romantic deep |
| **JP** | "好きです (Suki desu)" | "好きです (Suki desu)" (same!) | "愛してる (Aishiteru)" (rare/heavy) | Suki = like, Aishiteru = love (big step) |
| **KR** | "좋아해 (Joahae)" | "좋아해요 (Joahaeyo)" / "사랑해 (Saranghae)" | "너무 사랑해 (Neomu saranghae)" | Joahae = like, Saranghae = love (big step) |
| **CH** | "我喜欢你 (Wǒ xǐhuān nǐ)" | "我喜欢你 (Wǒ xǐhuān nǐ)" | "我爱你 (Wǒ ài nǐ)" | Same; 喜欢 = like, 爱 = love (big step) |

### **Cultural Pattern**: EN/ES direct > CN direct > JP/KR gradual

- **EN**: Love expressed quickly (often in early dating)
- **ES**: Te quiero (very common); Te amo (deep love/marriage)
- **JP**: Suki → Aishiteru (big jump, often after confession or marriage)
- **KR**: Joahae → Saranghae (similar to JP, gradual)
- **CH**: 喜欢 → 爱 (similar to JP/KR, gradual but more direct than JP/KR)

## Honesty vs Harmony in Emotional Expression

### The "Politeness Constraint"

| Culture | Honesty with Strangers | Contextual Truth |
|---------|---------------------|------------------|
| **English** | Moderate directness | "I'm fine" usually literal |
| **Spanish** | High expressiveness | "¡Estoy bien!" genuinely positive |
| **Japanese** | "本音 (honne)" hidden, "建前 (tatemae)" shown | "大丈夫です" often means "not OK" |
| **Korean** | 눈치 + 정 — read the room | "괜찮아요" can mean "I don't want to bother you" |
| **Chinese** | 面子 (miànzi) — face-saving | "还好" (still okay) = "not great" |

### Saying "I'm Not OK" Across Languages

| Situation | English | Japanese | Korean | Chinese |
|-----------|---------|----------|--------|---------|
| **Work stress** | "I'm really stressed" | "ちょっと疲れました" (a bit tired) | "좀 힘들어요" (a bit hard) | "最近比较累" (recently tired) |
| **Relationship** | "I'm sad" | "元気ないです" (not well) | "기분이 안 좋아요" (mood not good) | "心情不好" (mood not good) |
| **Loneliness** | "I'm lonely" | "寂しいです" (rare) | "외로워요" (rare) | "有点孤独" (a bit lonely) |
