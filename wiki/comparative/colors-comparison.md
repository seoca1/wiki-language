## Linguistic Notes

| Pattern | English | Spanish | Japanese | Korean | Chinese |
|---------|---------|---------|----------|--------|---------|
| **Blue/Green distinction** | Strict (blue vs green) | Strict (azul vs verde) | 青 covers blue+green (青信号 = green light!) | Strict (파랑 vs 초록) | Strict (蓝 vs 绿) |
| **Red/Orange** | Strict | Rojo vs Naranja | 赤 (red) / 橙 (orange) | 빨강 vs 주황 | 红 vs 橙 |
| **Compound colors** | Light blue, dark blue | Azul claro/oscuro | 水色 (mizuiro = light blue), 紺 (kon = dark blue) | 연파랑 (yeonparang = light blue), 남색 (dark blue) | 浅蓝 (qiǎnlán), 深蓝 (shēnlán) |
| **Adjective form** | "Red book" | "Libro rojo" | "赤い本" (akai hon) | "빨간 책" (ppalgan chaek) | "红色的书" (hóngsè de shū) |

## Per-Language Detail

### English
- **Key terms**: "Hue", "Tint", "Shade", "Tone"; primary (red/yellow/blue) vs secondary (orange/green/purple)
- **Sources**: `[[colors-vocabulary]]`

### Spanish
- **Key terms**: "Rojo" / "Roja" (gender agreement); "Anaranjado" (orangey) vs "Naranja"
- **Patterns**: Adjective must match noun gender (rojo/roja, blanco/blanca)
- **Sources**: N/A — Spanish lacks explicit colors theme yet

### Japanese
- **Key terms**: 色 (iro = color); 色彩 (shikisai); 色合い (iroai = color tone)
- **Patterns**: Adjective: 〜い (kurompai); Noun+の: 赤の (aka no)
- **Sources**: `[[colors-vocabulary]]` (limited — basic only)

### Korean
- **Key terms**: 색깔 (saekkka), 색 (saek = color); 노란색/�간색 (color + suffix)
- **Patterns**: Adjective modifies noun directly (no gender agreement)
- **Sources**: `[[colors-vocabulary]]`

### Chinese
- **Key terms**: 颜色 (yánsè), 色彩 (sècǎi); 五色 (wǔsè = traditional five colors)
- **Patterns**: Adjective + 的 (de) + noun: 红色的书
- **Sources**: N/A — Chinese lacks explicit colors theme yet

## Key Contrasts (Synthesis)

| Contrast | Insight |
|----------|---------|
| **青 (ao) in JP** | Covers both blue and green (青信号 = green light, 青い空 = blue sky) — unique linguistic feature |
| **Gender agreement** | ES requires gender agreement (rojo/roja); other 4 languages don't |
| **Basic color count** | All 5 languages have ~6-11 basic color terms; cultural additions vary |
| **Cultural symbolism** | White = mourning in JP/KR/CN; Red = luck in KR/CN; opposite of Western associations |
