## Workplace Hierarchy & Formality

| Aspect | English | Spanish | Japanese | Korean | Chinese |
|--------|---------|---------|----------|--------|---------|
| **Hierarchy emphasis** | Medium (title-based) | Low-Medium (tú/usted) | Very High (keigo system) | Very High (speech levels) | Medium-High (titles + respect) |
| **Address by name** | First name common | First name (informal); Title+last (formal) | Last name + 様/さん (san) | Last name + 님 (nim) / 씨 (ssi) | Last name + 老师/经理 (titles) |
| **Greeting to superior** | Hello / Hi / Good morning | Buenos días (formal); Hola (informal) | おはようございます (ohayō gozaimasu); いつもお世話になっております | 안녕하세요 / 수고하십니까 (sugohamnika) | 您好 / 您早 (formal) |
| **Self-reference** | "I" | Yo | 私 (watashi) / 僕 (boku) casual | 저 (jeo) / 나 (na) casual | 我 (wǒ) |
| **Email closing** | Best / Thanks / Regards | Saludos / Atentamente / Un saludo | よろしくお願いいたします / 敬具 (keigu) | 감사합니다 / 드림 (deurim) / 배상 (baesang) | 此致敬礼 (cǐ zhì jìng lǐ) / 顺祝商祺 |

## Per-Language Detail

### English
- **Key terms**: "Promotion" (upward), "Lateral move" (sideways), "Termination" (out); "Cubicle" (small office)
- **Patterns**: Light verb: "I'm in charge of..." (responsibility); passive constructions common
- **Register notes**: Casual — "Hey boss"; Formal — "Dear Mr./Ms. [Last name]"
- **Sources**: `[[career-vocabulary]]`

### Spanish
- **Key terms**: "Trabajo" (work/job) vs "Empleo" (employment); "Curro" = slang for job (Spain)
- **Patterns**: -ar verb: trabajar (to work); reflexive: "se me hace tarde" (running late)
- **Regional variations**: Spain: "curro" (slang); LatAm: "chamba" (Mexico, slang); "laburo" (Argentina, slang)
- **Sources**: N/A — Spanish lacks career-vocabulary theme yet

### Japanese
- **Key terms**: 仕事 (shigoto = work), 業務 (gyōmu = business duties), 職 (shoku = job/position)
- **Patterns**: Verb: 〜をする (wo suru); Honorific: お疲れ様です (otsukaresama desu = thanks for your work)
- **Keigo / politeness level**: 尊敬語 (sonkeigo = respect for superior); 謙譲語 (kenjōgo = humble); 丁寧語 (teineigo = polite)
- **Sources**: `[[career-vocabulary]]` (EN/JP/KO themes; ES/ZH pending)

### Korean
- **Key terms**: 직장 (jikjang = workplace), 직업 (jigeop = occupation), 업무 (eomu = business)
- **Patterns**: Verb: 〜을/를 하다 (eul/reul hada); Title + 님: 과장님 (gwajangnim = section chief)
- **Speech level**: Casual — "야, 이거 해" (informal); Polite — "이거 해요"; Formal — "이거 합니다" / "이거 하겠습니다"
- **Sources**: `[[business-vocabulary]]`, `[[career-vocabulary]]`

### Chinese
- **Key terms**: 工作 (gōngzuò = work), 职业 (zhíyè = profession), 公司 (gōngsī = company)
- **Patterns**: Verb: 〜进行 (jìnxíng = to conduct); 在 + verb pattern (在工作中 = at work)
- **Register / honorifics**: 您 (nín = formal you); "您辛苦了" (nín xīnkǔle = you've worked hard)
- **Sources**: N/A — Chinese wiki lacks career-vocabulary theme yet

## Key Contrasts (Synthesis)

| Contrast | Insight |
|----------|---------|
| **Hierarchy** | JP/KR/ZH have explicit formal hierarchy via language; EN/ES less so |
| **Email closings** | All languages have formal closings, but JP/KR/ZH are notably more elaborate (敬具, 배상, 此致敬礼) |
| **Work-life concept** | All languages adopted English "work-life balance" (ワークライフバランス, 워라밸, 工作生活平衡) |
| **After-work culture** | 회식/飲み会 have no EN/ES equivalent — these are core workplace cultural practices in JP/KR |

## Quick Reference Card

> **Career advice across cultures**:
> - JP/KR: Respect seniority, use formal speech with superiors
> - ES: Use usted vs tú based on hierarchy
> - EN: First names common but titles in formal settings
> - ZH: Use 您 with superiors, address by title
