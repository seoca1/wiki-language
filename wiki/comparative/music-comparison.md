## Per-Language Detail

### English
- **Key terms**: "Pop" (popular), "Classical" (European art music), "Folk" (traditional); "songwriter" vs "composer"
- **Patterns**: Heavy use of borrowed genre names globally; English is the lingua franca for music terms
- **Sources**: N/A — music theme not yet ingested in any language wiki

### Spanish
- **Key terms**: "Música" (music), "Canción" (song), "Banda" (band); "Folclore" / "Folclórica" varies by country
- **Patterns**: Flamenco, salsa, tango, reggaetón are distinct regional traditions; "música" is feminine noun
- **Sources**: N/A — Spanish wiki lacks explicit music theme yet

### Japanese
- **Key terms**: 音楽 (ongaku = music); J-Pop, J-Rock, Enka (演歌 = traditional Japanese ballad); Vocaloid (ボカロ)
- **Patterns**: おんがく is Sino-Japanese; loanwords (katakana) dominate modern genres; 歌 (uta) for song
- **Sources**: N/A — Japanese wiki lacks explicit music theme yet

### Korean
- **Key terms**: 음악 (eumak = music); K-Pop, K-Indie, 트로트 (teuroteu = trot, older Korean pop); 아이돌 (aidorul = idol)
- **Patterns**: 음악 is Sino-Korean; loanwords (K-Pop, 댄스) coexist with native terms (노래 norae = song)
- **Sources**: N/A — Korean wiki lacks explicit music theme yet

### Chinese
- **Key terms**: 音乐 (yīnyuè); C-Pop, Mandopop, Cantopop (粤语流行); 民乐 (mínyuè = traditional Chinese music)
- **Patterns**: 古风 (gǔfēng) = ancient-style modern music; regional styles (粤 Yue, 沪 Hu); 乐 is multiregistered (yuè = music, lè = joy/happiness)
- **Sources**: N/A — Chinese wiki lacks explicit music theme yet

## Key Contrasts (Synthesis)

| Contrast | Insight |
|----------|---------|
| **Traditional instruments** | Each culture has its own iconic string instrument: erhu (CN), shamisen (JP), gayageum (KR); Spanish has guitar; English has none culturally unique |
| **Borrowed vs native terms** | Modern genres (rock, jazz, pop) are loanwords in all 5; traditional forms use native vocabulary (演歌, 트로트, 民乐) |
| **Pitch and tone in music vs language** | ZH and KR speakers more attuned to pitch nuance (tonal/pitch-accent languages); may aid absolute pitch perception |
| **Music as cultural export** | K-Pop and J-Pop are deliberate national exports; "Hallyu" (한류 / 韩流) refers to Korean Wave; C-Pop less globally packaged |
| **Compound structure** | CJK languages use 2-character compounds (音乐, 음악, 音楽) with shared character/hanja/kanji roots; ES/EN use Latinate/Anglo roots |
