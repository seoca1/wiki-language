## Linguistic Notes

| Pattern | English | Spanish | Japanese | Korean | Chinese |
|---------|---------|---------|----------|--------|---------|
| **Gender/number agreement** | None (except this/that) | Required (bueno/buena, grande for both) | None | None | None |
| **Plural formation** | -s/-es (books) | -s/-es (libros); adj. unchanged | None | None | None (的) |
| **Placement (default)** | Before noun (big house) | After noun (casa grande) | Before noun (大きい家) | Before noun (큰 집) | Before noun, often with 的 (大的房子) |
| **Adjective form** | No inflection (good book) | -o/-a must match noun | -i (い-adj) / -na (な-adj) | -ㄴ/-은 (consonant/vowel) | Bare (好书) or 的 (好的书) |
| **Comparative** | -er / more (bigger) | más + adj (más grande) | より (yori) / の方が | 더 (deo) | 更 (gèng) / 比 (bǐ) |
| **Superlative** | -est / most (biggest) | el más + adj | 一番 (ichiban) | 가장 (gajang) | 最 (zuì) |

## Per-Language Detail

### English
- **Key terms**: Gradable (big, very big) vs non-gradable (perfect, *very perfect ✗); attributive vs predicative
- **Patterns**: Order rules: opinion-size-age-shape-color-origin-material (e.g., "big old red wooden box")
- **Sources**: `[[adjectives-vocabulary]]`

### Spanish
- **Key terms**: Adjectives agree in gender and number with noun; "bueno/buena", "malo/mala"; "grande" shortens to "gran" before singular nouns
- **Patterns**: Placement varies by meaning (default after noun; some before for emphasis: "pobre hombre" = unfortunate man)
- **Sources**: `[[adjectives-vocabulary]]`

### Japanese
- **Key terms**: い-adjectives (白い shiroi) and な-adjectives (静かな shizuka na); both conjugate for tense
- **Patterns**: Adjective directly modifies noun without particle; past form -かった (katta); negative -くない (kunai)
- **Sources**: N/A — Japanese wiki lacks explicit adjectives theme yet

### Korean
- **Key terms**: Descriptive verbs (형용사, hyeong-yongsa) conjugate like verbs; 좋다 → 좋은 / 좋습니다 / 좋아요
- **Patterns**: Past -았/었-; negative -지 않다; modifies noun with -(으)ㄴ: 좋은 사람 (good person)
- **Sources**: N/A — Korean wiki lacks explicit adjectives theme yet

### Chinese
- **Key terms**: 单音节 (monosyllabic) vs 双音节 (disyllabic); 的 (de) optional for short adj+noun
- **Patterns**: No inflection; degree adverbs 很 (hěn) often used even when "very" not meant: 很好 = "good"
- **Sources**: N/A — Chinese wiki lacks explicit adjectives theme yet

## Key Contrasts (Synthesis)

| Contrast | Insight |
|----------|---------|
| **Agreement** | Spanish alone requires gender/number agreement; EN/JP/KR/ZH do not |
| **Placement** | Spanish default is post-noun; other 4 languages prefer pre-noun |
| **Adjective as verb** | JP/KR treat descriptive adjectives as verb-like (conjugating for tense); EN/ES/ZH treat as noun modifiers |
| **Degree words** | ZH uses 很 (hěn) so often it functions as a grammatical particle; EN/ES/JP/KR need explicit "very" |
| **Comparative forms** | EN uses inflection (-er/-est); ES/JP/KR/ZH use separate words (más/より/더/更) |
