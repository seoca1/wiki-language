# Lunch and rest patterns across cultures (cross-linguistic comparison)

**Scope:** Midday meal + rest practices across 5 cultures.
**Languages compared:** Spanish, English, Japanese, Korean, Chinese.
**Theme:** daily-rhythm, food-culture, work-life-balance.

## Overview

Cultures differ dramatically in when and how they structure the midday meal and rest. This page compares the 5 languages' cultures on the question: "what happens at noon?"

## Lunch and rest traditions

| Culture | Lunch time | Lunch duration | Post-meal rest | Active siesta? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spain | 14:00-15:30 | 60-90 min | 30-60 min (siesta) | yes (rural) |
| England/US | 12:00-13:00 | 30-45 min | none | no |
| Japan | 12:00-13:00 | 30-45 min | inemuri (15-30 min) | partial (inoffice) |
| Korea | 12:00-13:00 | 30-45 min | 낮잠 (20-30 min) | partial (children) |
| China | 11:30-12:30 | 30-45 min | 午休 (wǔxiū, 30-60 min) | yes (offices) |

## Cross-cultural patterns

- **Lunch time is earlier in East Asia** (11:30-13:00) than in southern Europe (14:00-15:30).
- **Post-meal rest is universal** but duration varies (0-60 min).
- **Workplace nap culture** (inemuri in Japan, 午休 in China) has become more acceptable in modern offices.
- **Spain's siesta is the only "official" cultural practice** of the group; others are pragmatic adaptations.
