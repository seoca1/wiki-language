# Diatopic variation patterns across languages (cross-linguistic comparison)

**Scope:** Regional dialectal variation across 5 languages.
**Languages compared:** Spanish, English, Japanese, Korean, Chinese.
**Theme:** dialectal-variation, regional-registers, sociolinguistics.

## Overview

All languages exhibit **diatopic variation** — systematic differences in pronunciation, lexicon, grammar, and pragmatics across geographical regions. This page compares the 5 languages' patterns of regional variation.

## Diatopic variation patterns

| Language | Major dialect groups | Pronunciation | Lexicon | Grammar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | castellano, mexicano, rioplatense, andino, caribeño | s/θ distinction, yeísmo, seseo | regional vocabulary (coche/carro/auto) | voseo, leísmo, ustedes |
| English | RP, General American, Australian, Indian, etc. | rhoticity, vowel quality | regional slang (sub/bus) | tag questions, y'all |
| Japanese | 東京弁, 関西弁, 広島弁, 博多弁 | vowel pitch accent | 関西弁 vocabulary | 敬語 levels |
| Korean | 서울, 부산, 제주 dialects | ㄹ/l realization | regional vocabulary | honorific variations |
| Chinese | 普通话, 粤语, 闽南语, 吴语 | tonal system, retroflex | regional vocabulary | sentence-final particles |

## Key contrasts

- **Spanish** has the most standardized written form with wide dialectal speech variation.
- **Japanese** has the most stratified register system (敬語) with regional accents (方言).
- **Chinese** has the most divergent spoken varieties (普通话 vs 粤语 are nearly mutually unintelligible).
- **English** has the most political significance attached to dialect choice (prestige varieties).
- **Korean** has the most centralized standard (서울말) with regional variation seen as less prestigious.
