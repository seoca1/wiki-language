# Chinese New Year Traditions (春节, Chūnjié)

**Overview:** Chinese New Year (春节, Chūnjié) is the most important traditional holiday, marked by family reunions, red envelopes, fireworks, and symbolic foods.

## Key Points

- **Dates**: Lunar New Year (正月初一) + 15-day festival ending with Lantern Festival (元宵节, Yuánxiāo Jié). Late January or February.
- **Chunlian (春联)**: Red couplets on door — good luck for new year. Pairs of red paper with black/gold calligraphy.
- **Hongbao (红包)**: Red envelopes with money. Given by elders to children, married couples to unmarried, bosses to employees. Amounts often avoid 4 (四, sì = death).
- **Nianye Fan (年夜饭)**: New Year's Eve dinner — reunion feast. Family members travel (春运, Chūnyùn — world's largest annual human migration).
- **Symbolic Foods**:
  - **鱼 (yú)**: Fish — homophone for "surplus" (余, yú). Whole fish served head-to-tail.
  - **饺子 (jiǎozi)**: Dumplings — shaped like gold ingots. Eaten at midnight.
  - **汤圆 (tāngyuán)**: Glutinous rice balls — "reunion" roundness.
  - **年糕 (niángāo)**: Rice cake — "higher year."
  - **春卷 (chūnjuǎn)**: Spring rolls.
  - **长寿面 (chángshòu miàn)**: Longevity noodles.
- **Firecrackers (鞭炮, biānpào)**: Set off to scare away evil spirit "年 (Nián)."
- **Red Decorations**: Red (红, hóng) everywhere — symbolizes good luck and wards off evil.
- **Lion/Dragon Dance (舞狮, 舞龙)**: During Spring Festival parades, especially in Chinese communities.
- **Lucky Money Amounts**: 8 (八, bā = wealth), 6 (六, liù = smooth), 168 (一路發). Avoid 4 (四, sì = death).

## Language Connections

- `春节` (chūnjié) — Spring Festival
- `新年` (xīnnián) — New Year
- `红包` (hóngbāo) — Red envelope
- `年夜饭` (niányèfàn) — New Year's Eve dinner
- `饺子` (jiǎozi) — Dumplings
- `春联` (chūnlián) — Spring couplets
- `春晚` (chūnwǎn) — Spring Festival Gala (CCTV)
- `春运` (chūnyùn) — Spring travel season
