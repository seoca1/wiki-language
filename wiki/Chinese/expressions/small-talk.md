# small-talk (Chinese) expressions

> **Theme:** Safe-Topic Small Talk (寒暄 / 闲聊)
> **Level:** A2-B1

**Overview:** Chinese small talk (寒暄 / 闲聊) uses safe topics: weather, food, work, family, regional origin. Personal questions (income, age, marital status) are common in Chinese culture as relationship-builders — not taboo as in English, but require appropriate register. 您 (nín) honorific vs 你 (nǐ) choice matters for strangers/elders.

---

## 今天天气真好

**Literal Translation:** "Today's weather is really nice"

**Meaning:** Default safe-topic opener. Weather is universally acceptable.

**Usage Context:** Polite / casual. Strangers, neighbors, casual acquaintances.

**Pattern:** 今天 (today) + 天气 (weather) + 真 (really) + 好 (good).

### Examples

- 今天天气真好。 — Today's weather is really nice.
- 今天天气真不错。 — Today's weather is really not bad (softer variant).
- 天气真好, 适合出去走走。 — Nice weather, good for going out.

### Cultural Background

Weather is the universal Chinese small-talk opener, just as in English. 不错 (bùcuò, "not bad") is a softer way to say "good" and is very common in everyday speech.

## 你吃了吗

**Literal Translation:** "Have you eaten?"

**Meaning:** Classic Chinese greeting-as-question. Functions like "How are you?" rather than literal food inquiry.

**Usage Context:** Casual. Friends, neighbors, older relatives greeting younger.

**Pattern:** 你 (you) + 吃 (eat) + 了 (completed action particle) + 吗 (question).

### Examples

- 你吃了吗? — Have you eaten? (greeting)
- 吃过了, 你呢? — Yes, and you?
- 还没呢。 — Not yet.

### Cultural Background

你吃了吗 is one of the most traditional Chinese greetings, especially among older generations. It reflects the cultural importance of food and wellbeing. Modern Chinese also use 你好 (nǐ hǎo, hello) more commonly.

## 你在哪里工作

**Literal Translation:** "Where do you work?"

**Meaning:** Standard inquiry about occupation.

**Usage Context:** Casual. New acquaintances, networking.

**Pattern:** 你 (you) + 在 (at) + 哪里 (where) + 工作 (work).

### Examples

- 你在哪里工作? — Where do you work?
- 我在阿里巴巴工作。 — I work at Alibaba.
- 你做什么工作? — What kind of work do you do? (variant)

### Cultural Background

Workplace (and company prestige) is a normal small-talk topic in Chinese culture. Mentioning a famous employer (BAT, 字节跳动, etc.) is a status signal.

## 你家是哪里的

**Literal Translation:** "Where is your family from?"

**Meaning:** Standard inquiry about regional origin / hometown.

**Usage Context:** Casual. New acquaintances.

**Pattern:** 你家 (your home/family) + 是 (is) + 哪里 (where) + 的 (of).

### Examples

- 你家是哪里的? — Where is your family from?
- 我是北京人。 — I'm from Beijing.
- 你老家在哪里? — Where is your old home? (more nostalgic)

### Cultural Background

Regional origin (家乡 / 老家) is very important in Chinese culture. People often identify strongly with their home province, and same-province connections form immediate bonds.

## 最近忙不忙

**Literal Translation:** "Have you been busy lately?"

**Meaning:** Casual check-in. Standard greeting-as-question.

**Usage Context:** Casual. Friends, coworkers.

**Pattern:** 最近 (lately) + 忙 (busy) + 不 (not) + 忙 (busy).

### Examples

- 最近忙不忙? — Have you been busy lately?
- 最近挺忙的。 — Been pretty busy.
- 不忙, 你呢? — Not busy, you?

### Cultural Background

The "X 不 X" structure (verb-not-verb) is the natural Chinese way to ask yes/no questions. Literal "are you busy?" without 不 would be awkward.

- [[small-talk]] — 最近怎么样
- [[small-talk]] — 你最近好吗

## 最近怎么样

**Literal Translation:** "How have you been lately?"

**Meaning:** Casual check-in. Standard "how are you" for ongoing relationships.

**Usage Context:** Casual. Friends, coworkers, acquaintances.

**Pattern:** 最近 (lately) + 怎么样 (how is it).

### Examples

- 最近怎么样? — How have you been lately?
- 还不错, 你呢? — Pretty good, you?
- 最近挺好的。 — Been pretty good lately.

### Cultural Background

最近怎么样 is the most common casual check-in among Chinese speakers. 不错 (not bad) is the most common reply.

## 吃过饭了吗

**Literal Translation:** "Have you eaten (a meal)?"

**Meaning:** Variants of 你吃了吗 — food-as-greeting pattern.

**Usage Context:** Casual. Family, older relatives.

**Pattern:** 吃过 (have eaten) + 饭 (meal) + 了 (completed) + 吗 (question).

### Examples

- 吃过饭了吗? — Have you eaten?
- 吃过了。 — Yes I have.
- 还没吃呢。 — Not yet.

### Cultural Background

Both 你吃了吗 and 吃过饭了吗 are common food-greetings. 吃 (chī) + 过 (guò, experience particle) emphasizes completion of the action.

- [[small-talk]] — 你吃了吗

## 周末有什么安排

**Literal Translation:** "Any plans for the weekend?"

**Meaning:** Friday closing small-talk opener.

**Usage Context:** Casual. Coworkers, friends.

**Pattern:** 周末 (weekend) + 有 (have) + 什么 (what) + 安排 (plans).

### Examples

- 周末有什么安排? — Any plans for the weekend?
- 没什么安排。 — No plans.
- 打算出去走走。 — Planning to go out a bit.

### Cultural Background

周末 (zhōumò, weekend) is a relatively recent concept in Chinese work culture (改革开放后普及). Today it's a common small-talk topic.

- [[small-talk]] — 周末打算做什么
- [[small-talk]] — 这周末怎么过
