# Tone Pairs (Tone Sandhi Practice)

**Type:** lesson
**Date Added:** 2026-07-13
**Language Level:** beginner (HSK 1)

## Summary

This lesson covers the three tone sandhi (变调) rules that govern how Mandarin tones shift inside multi-syllable words and across word boundaries: the 3+3 → 3+2 rule (你好 becomes ní hǎo), the "一" alternation (yī / yí / yì depending on the following tone), and the "不" softening (bù → bú only before 4th tone). Each rule is presented with the abstract pattern, a worked example table, and a real conversational example. Tone-pair practice sets (mā/má/mǎ/mà, bā/bá/bǎ/bà, yī/yí/yǐ/yì) drive home that pitch alone distinguishes lexically distinct words, and the lesson closes with 20 model sentences that combine all three sandhi rules in context.

## Key Takeaways

- Tone sandhi is a pronunciation adjustment, NOT a meaning change. 你好 still means "hello"; only the pitch contour of the second syllable shifts.
- 3+3 → 3+2 affects any two adjacent 3rd-tone syllables: 小姐, 口语, 总结, 友好, 演讲 all flip the second tone.
- "一" is yī alone, yí after 4th tone (十一), and yì after 1st/2nd/3rd tone (一起, 一只).
- "不" stays bù except directly before a 4th tone, where it becomes bú (不对, 不要, 不会).
- Korean intonation (sentence-level rise/fall) does not map to Mandarin tone sandhi. Pitch must be retrained, not borrowed.

## Cultural Insights

- Mandarin speakers apply tone sandhi automatically and unconsciously. Learners who memorize "the dictionary form" without sandhi sound stilted, even when their tones are technically correct.
- 3+3 → 3+2 is one of the most consistent sandhi rules and the easiest to drill. Mastery early unlocks more natural-sounding Mandarin.
- "一" and "不" shifts are positional rules, not memorization. Korean learners who internalize the trigger condition (the following tone) need only a handful of examples before the rule becomes automatic.
