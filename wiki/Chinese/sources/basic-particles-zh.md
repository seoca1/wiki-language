## Summary

This source covers the four essential Chinese particles: 的 (de - possessive), 了 (le - completed action), 在 (zài - location/progressive), and 有 (yǒu - possession/existence). These four particles form the foundation of basic Chinese grammar and enable construction of most HSK 1-3 level sentences.

## Key Takeaways

- **的 (de)** — possessive particle "of/'s", marks possession or attribution
- **了 (le)** — completed action marker, also indicates state change
- **在 (zài)** — location marker "at/in", also progressive aspect "doing"
- **有 (yǒu)** — possession "have", existence "there is", negative form 没有 (méi yǒu)

## Cultural Insights

- Korean learners often confuse Korean hanja readings with Chinese pronunciation (e.g., 의 ≠ de)
- 没有 (méi yǒu) is the only correct negative for 有 — 不有 (bù yǒu) is never used
- 在 (zài) serves dual function: location and progressive aspect
