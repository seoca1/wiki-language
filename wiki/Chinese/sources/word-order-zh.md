## Summary

This source covers Chinese word order (SVO structure) and its key differences from Korean (SOV). It provides 20 common sentence patterns, question formation, negation rules, and 30 practice sentences for Korean learners.

## Key Takeaways

- Chinese is **SVO** (Subject-Verb-Object) — Korean is SOV
- Time expressions precede the verb (我每天运动)
- Location expressions precede the verb with 在 (我在家工作)
- Adverbs precede the verb (我经常运动)
- Questions: add 吗 to statements, or use question words in-situ
- Negation: 不 (bù) for present/future, 没 (méi) for past

## Cultural Insights

- The #1 trap for Korean learners: translating SOV directly (我饭吃 instead of 我吃饭)
- Korean particles (은/는, 을/를) don't exist in Chinese — word order replaces them
- Adjectives function as predicates without 是 (她漂亮, not 她是漂亮)
