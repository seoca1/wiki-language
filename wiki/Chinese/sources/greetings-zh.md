# Greetings and Self-Introduction

**Type:** lesson
**Date Added:** 2026-07-13
**Language Level:** beginner (HSK 1)

## Summary

This lesson teaches the 20 most essential Mandarin greetings plus the self-introduction pattern that lets learners meet people, give their name, nationality, age, and occupation. It organizes greetings by usage context (time-of-day, meeting/parting, thanks/apology, encouragement/well-wishes) and explicitly contrasts the formal 你 vs 您 pronoun choice. The self-introduction section drills the 是 (shì, "to be") verb with concrete examples like 我是韩国人 and 我是一名学生, then expands into role-play scenarios covering first meetings and asking about age and profession. The lesson closes with a 25-expression reference table organized by category (greetings, introductions, gratitude, apology, miscellaneous) that learners can use as a quick-recall cheat sheet.

## Key Takeaways

- 你好 maps directly to Korean "안녕하세요" and is the safest default for any greeting. Time-of-day variants (早上好, 晚上好) add politeness but are not required.
- 您 (nín) is always honorific in Mandarin. Unlike Korean "당신" (which shifts meaning by register), 您 is a clean formal register marker and should be the default with elders, superiors, and strangers.
- The self-introduction pattern is 我 + 是 + [identity]. Drop 是 only when the identity is implied by context. Particles like 的 do not appear here.
- 他/她/它 all sound like "tā". Only the character differs. Context (gendered noun, animal, object) is the disambiguator.
- 是 is 4th tone (shì), NOT neutral "shi". Pronunciation accuracy matters; learners who confuse this sound robotic.

## Cultural Insights

- Chinese greetings are more time-of-day-aware than Korean ones. Saying 早上好 between 6-9am and 晚上好 after 6pm signals attention to social time, similar to Korean formal settings.
- 是 (shì) as the "to be" copula is straightforward in Mandarin compared to the more nuanced topic-comment structures in Korean. Learners often overthink this.
- Family/age/profession questions in Mandarin can feel direct to Korean learners, but are standard small-talk topics in Chinese culture and signal friendly interest, not prying.
