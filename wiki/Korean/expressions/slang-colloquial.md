## 대박

**Part of Speech:** expression (admiration)
**Definition:** "Daebak" — "Awesome!/Amazing!" (originally lottery).
**Form**: Daebak.

### Variations

- **대박! (Daebak!)** — "Awesome!"
- **대박 나 (Daebak na)** — "It's daebak"
- **대박 사건 (Daebak sageon)** — "Huge incident"

### Cultural Notes

- **Originally lottery**: 대박 = "Big hit" (lottery jackpot)
- **Modern**: Used for any amazing thing
- **Frequency**: Universal (all ages)
- **Variations**: 헐 (heol), 미쳤다 (micheotda = "crazy/great")
- **K-pop culture**: Used in shows (e.g., "대박 나!" reaction)

## 화이팅

**Part of Speech:** expression (encouragement)
**Definition:** "Fighting!" — "Hang in there!" (from English "fighting").
**Form**: Hwaiteing.

### Variations

- **화이팅! (Hwaiteing!)** — "Fighting!"
- **화이팅해요 (Hwaiteinghaeyo)** — "Hang in there"
- **힘내요 (Himnaeyo)** — "Stay strong"

### Cultural Notes

- **Origin**: From English "Fighting!" (Korean adopted it)
- **Universal**: All ages use it
- **Sports**: Standard before games
- **Work**: "오늘도 화이팅!" (Today too, fighting!)
- **"응원합니다"**: More formal cheer

## ㅋㅋㅋ / ㅎㅎ

**Part of Speech:** expression (text)
**Definition:** "kkk / hh" — Internet laugh.
**Form**: ㅋㅋㅋ / ㅎㅎ.

### Variations

- **ㅋㅋㅋ (kkk)** — "Lol" (most common)
- **ㅎㅎ (hh)** — "Haha" (formal/older)
- **ㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋ** — "LMAO"
- **ㅎㅎㅎ** — "Haha" (warm)

### Cultural Notes

- **ㅋ origin**: ㄱㅋ = "smile" (Hangul)
- **ㅋ vs ㅎ**: ㅋ = casual/loud, ㅎ = polite/soft
- **Frequency**: Every text message has laughs
- **다량**: Many ㅋ's = more funny
- **Variation**: ㅋㅋㅋ = LOL, ㅎㅎ = haha

### Sources

- `[[emotions-reactions]]`

## 플러팅

**Part of Speech:** expression (modern)
**Definition:** "Flirting" — modern dating.
**Form**: Peulleoting.

### Variations

- **플러팅 (Peulleoting)** — "Flirting"
- **작전 (Jakeon)** — "Tactics/strategy" (joking)
- **쎈 떡밥 (Ssen tteokbap)** — "Strong bait" (advance)

### Cultural Notes

- **플러팅 culture**: Modern (influenced by K-pop/West)
- **"떡밥 (tteokbap)"**: "Rice cake" = bait/hint
- **K-drama influence**: Many romantic lines adopted
- **Indirect vs direct**: Korean still often indirect
- **군대 (Military)**: Korean men often date during/after service

## 존맛탱 / 맛있어요

**Part of Speech:** expression (slang)
**Definition:** "Jonmattaeng / Masisseoyo" — "Delicious!" (slang + standard).
**Form**: Jonmattaeng / Masisseoyo.

### Variations

- **존맛탱 (Jonmattaeng)** — "Totally delicious" (very slang)
- **존맛 (Jonmat)** — "Totally delicious" (short)
- **실화? (Silhwa?)** — "Real story?" (when something is unreal-amazing)

### Cultural Notes

- **존맛**: 존 = "totally" + 맛 = "taste/delicious"
- **맛집 (Matjip)**: "Restaurant/famous food"
- **먹방 (Meokbang)**: "Eating broadcast"
- **K-food wave**: Korean food globally popular
- **Frequency**: Very common in younger generations

## 헬조선 / 탈조선

**Part of Speech:** expression (modern)
**Definition:** "Hell Joseon / Escape Joseon" — critique of Korean society.
**Form**: Heljoseon / Taljoseon.

### Variations

- **헬조선 (Heljoseon)** — "Hell Korea" (pessimistic)
- **탈조선 (Taljoseon)** — "Escape Korea" (immigration trend)
- **N포세대 (N-po sedae)** — "N-po generation" (giving up N things)

### Cultural Notes

- **헬조선**: Pessimistic view (housing, jobs, inequality)
- **탈조선**: Emigration trend (US, Canada, Australia)
- **N포**: N-po generation (연애, 결혼, 출산, 인간관계,买房, 취미 giving up)
- **Generational**: Mostly 20s-30s sentiment
- **Counter movement**: "기부천사" (giving angels), "착한 소비" (good consumption)

### Sources

- `[[emotions-reactions]]`

## 어른 / 직장인

**Part of Speech:** expression (humor)
**Definition:** "Adult / Worker" — adulting humor.
**Form**: Eoreun / Jikjangin.

### Variations

- **어른 (Eoreun)** — "Adult"
- **직장인 (Jikjangin)** — "Office worker / Salaryman"
- **월급쟁이 (Wolgeupjaengi)** — "Wage slave"
- **회사원 (Hoesaweon)** — "Company employee"

### Cultural Notes

- **직장인 (Salaryman)**: Iconic Korean work culture
- **996 culture**: 9am-9pm, 6 days a week (tech industry)
- **야근 (Overtime)**: Common in Korea
- **회식 (Company dinner)**: Often mandatory
- **Humor**: Self-deprecating about adult life

## 친 / 친구

**Part of Speech:** expression (address)
**Definition:** "Chin / Chinggu" — "Friend / Buddy".
**Form**: Chin / Chinggu.

### Variations

- **친 (Chin)** — Short for 친구 (friend)
- **친구 (Chinggu)** — "Friend"
- **단짝 (Danjjak)** — "Best friend"
- **베프 (Bepeu)** — "Best friend" (from English)

### Cultural Notes

- **친 (chin)**: Modern abbreviation (Gen Z)
- **베프 (bepu)**: Anglicism "best friend"
- **단짝 (danjjak)**: Childhood best friend (warm)
- **K-drama friendship**: Often dramatic (rich subplots)
- **Korean friendships**: Often school/work-based
