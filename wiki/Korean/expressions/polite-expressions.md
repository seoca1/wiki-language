# Expressions: Polite & Honorific (한국어)

> **Theme:** Politeness & Honorifics
> **Level:** A1-C1
> **Standard:** 표준어 (Standard Korean)
> **Romanization:** Romaja

Korean politeness — speech levels (존댓말), honorifics, business etiquette.

---

## 감사합니다

**Part of Speech:** expression (gratitude)
**Definition:** "Thank you" — formal gratitude.
**Form**: Gamsahamnida.

### Variations

- **감사합니다 (Gamsahamnida)** — "Thank you" (formal)
- **고맙습니다 (Gomapseumnida)** — "Thank you" (formal, different word)
- **고마워 (Gomawo)** — "Thanks" (casual)
- **진심으로 감사드립니다 (Jinsim-euro gamsadeulimnida)** — "Sincerely grateful"

### Cultural Notes

- **Frequency**: Korean = very frequent (especially formal)
- **Levels**: 고마워 (casual) → 고마워요 (standard) → 감사합니다 (formal) → 감사드립니다 (very formal)
- **Response**: 천만에요 (cheonmaneyo) / 별말씀을요 (byeolmalsseumeul-yo)
- **Service industry**: Always expected
- **Casual**: 미안 (informal gratitude in some contexts)

## ___ 해 주세요

**Part of Speech:** expression (request)
**Definition:** "Please do ___" — polite request.
**Form**: ___ hae juseyo.

### Variations

- **___ 해 주세요 (___ hae juseyo)** — "Please ___"
- **___ 해 주실 수 있나요? (___ hae jusil su issnayo?)** — "Can you ___?" (more polite)
- **___ 부탁드려요 (___ butakdeulyeoyo)** — "Please ___"
- **___ 해 주시겠어요? (___ hae jusigesseoyo?)** — "Would you ___?" (very polite)

### Cultural Notes

- **Polite forms**: 해 주세요 → 해 주실 수 있나요 → 해 주시겠어요
- **주세요 (juseyo)**: "Please give me" (very common polite request)
- **부탁 (butak)**: "Favor" (polite context)
- **존댓말 (Jondaetmal)**: Mandatory in formal settings
- **Verb levels**: 해 (casual), 해요 (polite), 합니다 (formal)

## 죄송합니다

**Part of Speech:** expression (apology)
**Definition:** "I'm sorry" — formal apology.
**Form**: Joesonghamnida.

### Variations

- **죄송합니다 (Joesonghamnida)** — "Sorry" (very formal)
- **죄송해요 (Joesonghaeyo)** — "Sorry" (polite)
- **미안해요 (Mianhaeyo)** — "Sorry" (polite, lighter)
- **미안 (Mian)** — "Sorry" (casual)

### Cultural Notes

- **Apology levels**: 미안 (casual) → 미안해요 (polite) → 죄송해요 (polite) → 죄송합니다 (formal)
- **Formal vs casual**: 미안 = lighter, 죄송 = more serious
- **Business**: Always use 죄송합니다
- **Family/friends**: 미안 OK
- **심한 사과**: "진심으로 사과드립니다" (sincerely apologize)

## 죄송해요 / 미안해요

**Part of Speech:** expression (apology)
**Definition:** "I'm sorry" — polite apology.
**Form**: Joesonghaeyo / Mianhaeyo.

### Variations

- **죄송해요 (Joesonghaeyo)** — "Sorry" (polite, formal)
- **미안해요 (Mianhaeyo)** — "Sorry" (polite, casual)
- **죄송합니다 (Joesonghamnida)** — "Sorry" (formal)
- **미안 (Mian)** — "Sorry" (casual)

### Cultural Notes

- **죄송 (Joesong)**: More formal/serious
- **미안 (Mian)**: More casual/lighter
- **Use cases**: 
  - 미안 = to friends, family, casual
  - 죄송 = to strangers, business, formal
- **Korean culture**: Sincere apology important
- **"진심으로 사과드립니다"**: Very formal corporate

## 아니에요 / 천만에요

**Part of Speech:** expression (response)
**Definition:** "No, it's nothing / You're welcome" — response to thanks.
**Form**: Anieyo / Cheonmaneyo.

### Variations

- **천만에요 (Cheonmaneyo)** — "You're welcome" (formal)
- **별말씀을요 (Byeolmalsseumeul-yo)** — "Don't mention it"
- **아니에요 (Anieyo)** — "No" (general)
- **네, 괜찮아요 (Ne, gwaenchanayo)** — "Yes, it's okay"

### Cultural Notes

- **천만에요**: Standard formal response
- **별말씀을요**: More humble/formal
- **아니에요**: Just "no" (often too casual)
- **"잘 했어요"**: "Well done" (response to good job)
- **Frequency**: Common but often just smile + nod

## ___ 해도 돼요?

**Part of Speech:** expression (permission)
**Definition:** "May I ___?" — polite permission request.
**Form**: ___ haedo dwaeyo?

### Variations

- **___ 해도 돼요? (___ haedo dwaeyo?)** — "May I ___?"
- **___ 해도 될까요? (___ haedo doelkkayo?)** — "May I ___?" (more polite)
- **___ 해도 괜찮아요? (___ haedo gwaenchanayo?)** — "Is ___ okay?"

### Cultural Notes

- **해도 돼요 (haedo dwaeyo)**: Standard "may I"
- **해야 해요 (haeya haeyo)**: "Have to" (obligation)
- **하지 마세요 (haji maseyo)**: "Please don't"
- **Levels**: casual (해도 돼?) → polite (해도 돼요) → formal (해도 됩니까)
- **Korean politeness**: Always ask before doing

## 잘 부탁드립니다

**Part of Speech:** expression (gratitude/humility)
**Definition:** "Please treat me well" — business greeting/closing.
**Form**: Jal butakdeumnida.

### Variations

- **잘 부탁드립니다 (Jal butakdeumnida)** — "Please treat me well" (formal)
- **잘 부탁해요 (Jal butakhaeyo)** — "Please treat me well" (polite)
- **잘 부탁해 (Jal butakhae)** — "Please treat me well" (casual)

### Cultural Notes

- **万能 greeting**: Meeting first time, project start, email closing
- **Business**: Almost every business interaction
- **함께 (Together)**: Implies working together
- **Hierarchy**: Always use with superiors/juniors
- **Closing**: Standard email closing

## 수고하셨습니다

**Part of Speech:** expression (acknowledgment)
**Definition:** "Thank you for your hard work" — closing.
**Form**: Sugo-hasyeotseumnida.

### Variations

- **수고하셨습니다 (Sugo-hasyeotseumnida)** — "Thank you for your hard work" (formal)
- **수고했어요 (Sugo-haesseoyo)** — "Thank you" (polite)
- **수고해 (Sugohae)** — "Good work" (casual)

### Cultural Notes

- **万能 closing**: After work, meetings, projects
- **Standard**: Every Korean interaction ends with this
- **職場 (Workplace)**: Universal use
- **Service industry**: Also used by staff to customers
- **Equivalent**: "お疲れ様" in Japanese (similar universal phrase)
