# 한국 커피와 카페 문화 (Korean Coffee and Cafe Culture)

**Overview:** South Korea is a top-3 global coffee consumer per capita — 1인 약 377잔 (cups/year, 2023). The country has approximately 100,000+ cafes (2024) for 51 million people, or roughly 1 cafe per 510 residents. Korea's coffee scene is distinct from Japan's kissaten (喫茶店) and Italy's espresso bar tradition — it blends specialty coffee culture (스페셜티 커피) with dessert café (디저트 카페), study cafés (스터디 카페), and franchise giants like 스타벅스 (Starbucks Korea) + 이디야 (Ediya) + 메가커피 (Mega Coffee).

## Key Points

- **커피 (coffee) 역사**: Coffee arrived in Korea in late 19th century via Japanese influence. 1970s + 1980s instant coffee (맥심) boom. 1990s 커피전문점 (specialty coffee shops) emerge. 2010s third-wave coffee (3rd wave specialty) boom. 2020s standalone roasters + hand-drip specialty.
- **스타벅스 (Starbucks Korea)**: Largest coffee chain in Korea. 1,800+ stores (2024). Pioneered the "third place" concept. Korea is Starbucks' 2nd-largest market after USA. Reshaped Korean cafe scene since 1999.
- **로컬 카페 (Local Cafes)**: Local coffee shop chains — 이디야 (Ediya, 3,500+ stores) + 메가커피 (Mega Coffee, 2,800+ stores) + 투썸플레이스 (A Twosome Place) + 빽다방 (Paik's Coffee) + 할리스 (Hollys). Most offer 아메리카노 (Americano) for ₩1,500–3,500.
- **메뉴 (Menu)**:
  - **아메리카노 (americano)** — most popular single drink. Plain espresso + water. Default order at most offices.
  - **카페라떼 (cafe latte)** — espresso + steamed milk. Sweetened variations.
  - **콜드브루 (cold brew)** — cold-extracted coffee, less bitter. Trendy since 2017.
  - **크루아상 (croissant) + 빵 (bread)** — Korean cafes pair coffee with French-style pastry. Salted butter bread (소금버터빵) is uniquely Korean.
- **디저트 카페 (Dessert Cafes)**: Combining coffee + dessert — 딸기라떼 (strawberry latte, seasonal) + 크로플 (croffle, croissant + waffle) + 인스타그램 (Instagram) presentation. Major social media + date spot.
- **스터디 카페 (Study Cafes)**: Paid study/work spaces — ₩5,000–10,000/hour. Free drinks + WiFi + cubicles. Popular with 고시생 (law exam prep students) + 직장인 (workers) + 대학생 (university students). Different from regular cafe.
- **KT&G + 보드게임 카페 (board game cafes) + 만화 카페 (manhwa cafes)**: Sub-categories of cafe culture.
- **모닝 커피 (morning coffee)**: Office workers arrive at cafe before work (7:30–9:00 AM) for an 아메리카노 at a discount (모닝 서비스). Common since 2010s.

## Cross-language Connections

- **English**: "Cafe culture" ≈ Korean 카페 문화 — but Western cafes are smaller, less ubiquitous. US has ~36,000 Starbucks stores for 330M (1 per 9,000); Korea has 1 per 510 residents. Korean cafe density is 18× higher than US.
- **Spanish**: Café con leche (coffee with milk) ≈ Korean 카페라떼. 차이: Spanish café culture is more bar/taberna (tavern) oriented; Korean cafes are more dessert/study/Instagram-oriented.
- **Japanese**: カフェ (kafe) ≈ 한국 카페 — both very dense urban cafe scenes. 차이: Japan's kissaten (喫茶店) = traditional atmospheric coffee house with jazz + wood interior; Korean modern cafe = minimalist white Instagram aesthetic.

## Ejemplos

1. **직장인 아침 루틴 (office worker morning routine)**: 8:00 AM arrive at 스타벡스 near 역 (station). Order 모닝 아메리카노 (morning Americano) + small breakfast set. 15 min cafe work session before subway commute to office.
2. **대학가 카페 (university district cafe)**: Near 서울대 (Seoul National Univ) or 연세대 (Yonsei Univ), cafes cluster densely. 학생 (students) gather for studying + group projects + casual dates. Discounted drinks for student ID.
3. **크루아상 + 커피 (croissant + coffee)**: French-Korean fusion pastry + specialty coffee is a classic weekend brunch combination. Cafes like "OURS" or "Le Pain Quotidien" charge ₩6,000–12,000 per set.
