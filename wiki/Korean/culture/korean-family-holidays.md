# Korean Family Holidays & Traditions

**Type:** culture, social-norms, family
**Region:** South Korea (with North Korea context)

## Overview

Korean family holidays center on ancestral rites, family reunion, and respect for elders. The two most important are Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok (Harvest Moon Festival), during which the entire country experiences mass migration as families reunite. Confucian values (*효, hyo* — filial piety) shape every tradition, from ritual bows to rice cake soup. This page complements `[[korean-dating-culture]]` and `[[korean-hallyu-wave]]`.

## Key Values Behind Holiday Culture

### 孝 (Hyo) — Filial Piety

- **Respect for parents/grandparents** — Core Confucian virtue
- **세배 (Sebae)** — Formal bow to elders (Seollal)
- **차례 (Charye)** — Ancestral memorial rites
- **Care for aging parents** — Adult children's responsibility
- **Intergenerational hierarchy** preserved

### 例 (Ye) — Tradition / Ritual

- **Strict adherence** to ritual steps
- **순서 (Sunseo)** — Correct order matters
- **Family recipes** passed down generations
- **Tea ceremonies** for ancestors
- **Ancestor photos** prominently displayed

### 정 (Jeong) — Deep Bond

- **Shared meals** strengthen bonds
- **Cooking together** (especially women)
- **Multi-generational gathering** for holidays
- **Tteok (rice cake)** sharing
- **Long meals** (multi-hour)

### 얼 (Eol) — Lineage / Kinship

- **Same clan** (성, seong) identity
- **Generational names** (돌, dol - first birthday)
- **Honoring deceased** ancestors
- **Dol-i (first birthday)** major celebration
- **Hundredth day (Baegil)** celebration

## Major Korean Holidays

### 설날 (Seollal) — Lunar New Year — 1st day of 1st lunar month

**The most important Korean holiday.** Korea essentially shuts down.

**Duration**: 3 days official; up to 5 days social

**Activities**:
- **세배 (Sebae)** — Formal bow to elders (most important ritual)
  - Younger people bow deeply to older relatives
  - **돈 (don)** received — pocket money for children
  - Often $10-100 per child depending on family wealth
- **차례 (Charye)** — Ancestor memorial rites
  - Food offerings on ancestral altar
  - 진설 (jinseol) — specific food arrangements
  - 차례상 (charyesang) — memorial table
  - Specific order of bowls/dishes
- **차례 음식 (Charye Eumsik)** — Ritual food (rice, soup, meat, fish, fruit)
- **제사 (Jesa)** — Memorial rites for deceased
- **성묘 (Seongmyo)** — Visit ancestral graves
- **놀이 (Nori)** — Traditional games (윷놀이, 제기차기, 투호)
- **한복 (Hanbok)** — Traditional dress
- **세뱃돈 (Sebaetdon)** — New Year's money for kids
- **전 (Jeon)** — Pancakes (special holiday food)
- **떡국 (Tteokguk)** — Rice cake soup (mandatory!)

**Tteokguk (떡국) - Rice Cake Soup - MANDATORY**:
- **Aging one year per bowl** — symbol of adding another year
- **White rice cake in clear broth**
- **Without it, you don't "age"** the new year
- **Served to elders first**

**Holiday Foods**:
- **떡국 (Tteokguk)** — Rice cake soup (mandatory)
- **갈비찜 (Galbi-jjim)** — Braised short ribs
- **잡채 (Japchae)** — Glass noodles with vegetables
- **전 (Jeon)** — Pancakes (kimchi jeon, pajeon, haemul jeon)
- **떡국국 (Tteokguk-guk)** — Rice cake soup variant
- **한과 (Hangwa)** — Traditional Korean sweets

**Greeting**: 새해 복 많이 받으세요 (Saehae bok mani badeuseyo) — "Receive lots of luck in the new year"

---

### 정월대보름 (Jeongwol Daeboreum) — 15th day of 1st lunar month

**First Full Moon Festival** (元宵节 equivalent).

**Activities**:
- **부럼 (Bureum)** — Cracking nuts with teeth (for dental health)
- **오곡밥 (Ogokbap)** — Five-grain rice (good luck)
- **귀밥 (Gwibap)** — Ear-shaped rice cake (hearing health)
- **먹밥 (Meokbap)** — Wine made with various ingredients
- **농악 (Nongak)** — Farm music performances
- **달집 (Daljip)** — Moon house bonfires

---

### 3.1절 (Samiljeol) — March 1

**Independence Movement Day** (1919 March 1st Movement against Japanese occupation).

**Activities**:
- **Memorial ceremonies** nationwide
- **Taegeukgi (태극기)** — Korean flag display
- **Government ceremonies**
- **Patriotic speeches**
- **Often weather-related** for military commemorations

---

### 식목일 (Singmokil) — April 5

**Tree-Planting Day** — Public holiday (national)

---

### 어버이날 (Eobeoinal) — May 8

**Parents' Day** — Note: Combines Mother's + Father's Day.

**Activities**:
- **Carnations** (카네이션, kaneyieon) — Flowers for parents
- **Red carnations** for living parents
- **White carnations** for deceased parents
- **Family gatherings**
- **Restaurant visits** with parents
- **Gifts** (flowers, red ginseng, health products)

---

### 부처님 오신 날 (Bucheonim Osin Nal) — May 15

**Buddha's Birthday** — Major Buddhist holiday.

**Activities**:
- **연등회 (Yeondeunghoe)** — Lantern Festival
- **Lanterns** in temples (전통등, jeontongdeung)
- **Temple visits**
- **부처님 (Buddha) bathing ceremony** (관음재)
- **Buddhist food** (수육, suyuk, temple food)
- **Parades** in cities (Buddha procession)

---

### 현충일 (Hyeonchungil) — June 6

**Memorial Day** (honoring war dead).

---

### 광복절 (Gwangbokjeol) — August 15

**Liberation Day** (1945 — End of Japanese occupation 1910-1945).

**Activities**:
- **Major national celebration**
- **Presidential address**
- **Flag displays** everywhere
- **Parades and ceremonies**
- **Free entry** to many national parks
- **Sales/promotions** at retail stores

---

### 추석 (Chuseok) — 15th day of 8th lunar month

**Harvest Moon Festival** — Second most important holiday after Seollal.

**Duration**: 3 days official (Aug 14-16 by lunar calendar); up to 5 days social

**Activities**:
- **한가위 (Hangawi)** — Original Korean name for Chuseok
- **차례 (Charye)** — Major ancestor memorial rites
  - Most elaborate ancestor ritual of the year
  - Special food offerings (14+ dishes typical)
  - Fresh fruit harvest offerings (배, pear; 감, persimmon; 밤, chestnut)
- **성묘 (Seongmyo)** — Visit ancestral graves + 벌초 (beolcho) — grave maintenance
- **송편 (Songpyeon)** — Half-moon rice cake (essential!)
  - Made together as family
  - Filled with sesame, honey, chestnuts, beans
  - Pine needle flavor
- **차례상 (Charyesang)** — Memorial table
- **경쟁 (Gyeongjaeng)** — Competitive ancestor rites between neighbors (subtle bragging)

**Traditional Foods**:
- **송편 (Songpyeon)** — Half-moon rice cake (mandatory)
- **송어 (Song-eo)** — Trout (seasonal)
- **배 (Bae)** — Korean pear (essential offering)
- **잣 (Jat)** — Pine nuts
- **대추 (Daechu)** — Dates
- **감 (Gam)** — Persimmon (autumn)
- **약과 (Yakgwa)** — Honey cookies
- **한과 (Hangwa)** — Traditional sweets
- **술 (Sul)** — Rice wine (makgeolli for older)
- **갈비찜 (Galbi-jjim)** — Braised short ribs (modern addition)

**Greeting**: 풍성한 한가위 보내세요 (Pungseonghan hangawi bonaeseyo) — "Have a bountiful Chuseok"

---

### 개천절 (Gaecheonjeol) — October 3

**National Foundation Day** (mythological founding of Korea by Dangun in 2333 BCE).

**Activities**:
- **Government ceremonies**
- **Dangun ritual** (단군제) at Samgak Mountain
- **Cultural performances**
- **Public holiday** (long weekend)

---

### 한글날 (Hangeulnal) — October 9

**Hangul Day** — Korean alphabet creation day (1446, King Sejong).

**Activities**:
- **Celebrations of Korean alphabet** (한글, Hangeul)
- **King Sejong** honored
- **Cultural exhibitions**
- **Public holiday** (started 2013, government holiday)
- **Special events** for children learning Korean

---

### 크리스마스 (Christmas) — December 25

**Christmas in Korea** = Romantic couple holiday + Christian celebration.

**Date**: December 25

**Activities**:
- **연인 (Yeonin)** = Couples celebrate together (date night)
- **교외 (Gyooe)** — Going out together (park, café, movie)
- **크리스마스 케이크** (Christmas cake)
- **크리스마스 트리** (Christmas tree)
- **크리스마스 카드** (Christmas card)
- **Hotel/event bookings** — extremely popular (peak season)

**Cultural Note**:
- Most Koreans are not Christian
- Christmas = date day for couples
- Peak hotel booking season (12/23-25)
- Increasing commercialization (less religious)

---

### 구정 (Gujeong) — Lunar New Year (alternate term)

Often used interchangeably with Seollal in North Korea and formal contexts.

## Korean Calendar System

### Lunar vs Solar Calendar

| Type | Korean | Use |
|------|---------|-----|
| **음력 (Eumryeok)** | Lunar calendar | Seollal, Chuseok, Jeongwol |
| **양력 (Yangryeok)** | Solar calendar | Most holidays, work life |
| **음력 24절기** | 24 Solar Terms | Traditional agricultural |

### Year Naming (Era Names)

- **대한민국 (Daehan Minguk)** = Republic of Korea
- **Famous eras**: 단기 (Dangi) 2333 BCE, 고려 (Goryeo) 918-1392, 조선 (Joseon) 1392-1910, 대한제국 (Korean Empire) 1897-1910
- **Dangi (단기) year**: Korea's founding year is 2333 BCE

## Gift-Giving Customs (Korean)

### Holiday Gifts

| Occasion | Direction | Common Gifts |
|----------|-----------|---------------|
| **Seollal** | Elder → Children | 세뱃돈 (money), clothes |
| **Seollal** | Adult → Parents | 건강식품 (health foods), money |
| **Chuseok** | Adult → Parents | Health foods, fruit, ginseng |
| **Parents' Day** | Children → Parents | 카네이션 (carnations), gift sets |
| **Teacher's Day** | Students → Teachers | 카네이션, fruit baskets |
| **Wedding** | Invited → Couple | 현금 (cash, 보통 50,000-300,000원) |

### Holiday Money (세뱃돈)

- **Pocket money** for children from elders
- **Amount depends on relationship**:
  - Grandparents: 50,000-100,000원 (~$40-80)
  - Parents: 30,000-50,000원 (~$25-40)
  - Aunts/Uncles: 10,000-30,000원 (~$8-25)
- **In bills**: Often 10,000원 bills
- **In envelope**: Traditional envelope (세뱃돈 봉투)
- **2 children**: Often 50,000원 each, 3+ = 30,000원

### Gift-Giving Etiquette

- **Two hands** when giving/receiving
- **Slight bow** when offering
- **Remove packaging** before giving (包装紙 벗기기)
- **Avoid certain gifts**:
  - **Sharp objects** (scissors, knives) — cutting ties
  - **Shoes** (shoes = leaving/走了)
  - **Green headwear** (cuckold)
  - **Handkerchiefs** (farewell/泪)
- **Fruit gift sets** popular (especially during holidays)
- **Red ginseng** (홍삼) = premium health gift

## Modern Korean Holidays (Added)

### 화이트데이 (White Day) — March 14

**Korean version of Valentine's** (responder day).

**Activities**:
- **Men give gifts to women** who gave them chocolate on Valentine's Day
- **Reciprocal gifts**: white chocolate, candies, flowers, jewelry
- **사탕 (satang)** — popular candies
- **Popular brands**: D'利斯, etc.

---

### 디데이 (D-Day) — Various Days

- **100일 (Baegil)** — 100 days of dating
- **200일 (Ibaegil)** — 200 days
- **300일 (Sambaegil)** — 300 days
- **1주년 (Iljunyeon)** — 1-year anniversary
- **주년 (Junyeon)** — Multiple years
- **Celebrated by couples** with gifts, photos, social media posts

---

### 스승의 날 (Seuseung-ui Nal) — May 15

**Teacher's Day** — Originally on this day; moved to September in some years.

**Activities**:
- **Students give gifts to teachers**
- **카네이션 (carnations)** still traditional
- **Money** (often given)
- **Schools often have events**
- **Controversy**: Commercialization concerns

---

### 제헌절 (Jeheonjeol) — October 3 (Alternate)

Sometimes used interchangeably with Gaecheonjeol.

## Cultural Sensitivity Notes

### What to Do at Seollal/Chuseok

✅ **Accept sebetdon graciously** (don't decline)
✅ **Bow to elders** (sebae is important)
✅ **Eat tteokguk** on New Year's morning (required!)
✅ **Try songpyeon** on Chuseok
✅ **Bring fruit/health gifts** when visiting
✅ **Take off shoes** in ancestral ritual rooms

### What to Avoid

❌ **Don't refuse charye food** (ancestral food)
❌ **Don't mention adoption** casually (한국 양자 문화 - common but sensitive)
❌ **Don't bring knives** as gifts
❌ **Don't write names in red** (death association)
❌ **Don't give green hats** to men
❌ **Don't pour soy sauce** on rice (감사, ancestral taboo)

## Cross-Cultural Insights

### For Foreign Visitors

✅ **Visit during Chuseok** (authentic) or **Seollal** (deepest)
✅ **Try tteokguk** on New Year's morning
✅ **Accept sebetdon** if offered
✅ **Bow to elders** (slight bow = respect)
✅ **Remove shoes** in ritual areas
✅ **Say "새해 복 많이 받으세요"** to anyone

❌ **Don't confuse Korea/Japan/China** (each unique)
❌ **Don't say "Gangnam Style"** as opening line
❌ **Don't ask about North Korea** at family dinners
❌ **Don't refuse sebaetdon** (it would insult)
