# 한국 한복 문화 (Korean Hanbok Culture)

**Overview:** 한복 (韓服, hanbok) is the traditional Korean dress worn for over 1,600 years, from the Three Kingdoms period (삼국시대, 57 BC – 668 AD) through the Joseon Dynasty (조선왕조, 1392–1897) to today. Although everyday 한복 wearing dropped below 5% after Western-style clothing became standard in the early 20th century, the garment remains central to festivals (설날, 추석, 돌잔치, 성년식), weddings (결혼식), and the modern Korean Wave (한류) revival of the 2010s.

## Key Points

- **한복 (韓服, hanbok)**: 韓 (한) = Korea + 服 (복) = clothing. The traditional two-piece garment: a jacket (저고리, jeogori) plus a skirt (치마, chima) for women, or pants (바지, baji) for men. Inner white undershirt (속옷) + outer colored top. Distinctive high-waisted skirt + short jacket silhouette.
- **저고리 (赤古里, jeogori)**: The short jacket. Originally covered the full torso; today exposes the waistline. Has 고름 (goreum) = ribbon ties on the chest, plus 동정 (dongjeong) = white collar.
- **치마 (裙子, chima)**: The high-waisted wrap skirt, secured by a long ribbon (고름). Creates the iconic voluminous silhouette. Modern revival shortened to ankle or mid-calf.
- **남성 한복 (men's hanbok)**: 바지 (baji) + 저고리 + 마고자 (magoja, vest) + 두루마기 (durumagi, overcoat) + 갓 (gat, hat). Worn by yangban (양반) aristocrats during Joseon.
- **오방색 (五方色, obangsaek)**: The five traditional colors — white (백, baek), black (흑, heuk), red (적, jeok), blue (청, cheong), yellow (황, hwang). 한복 color combinations follow obangsaek symbolism.
- **자수 (刺繡, jasu)**: Embroidery. Phoenixes (봉황), peonies (모란), bats (박쥐, lucky) are common motifs. Reserved for upper classes historically.
- **현대 revival (Modern Revival, 2010s–)**: King Sejong Institute, 한복 디자인 contests (한복살이 movement), hanbok rental shops (한복 대여) in Seoul (경복궁, 북촌, 인사동). Younger Koreans rent 한복 for tourism + photos + cultural pride.
- **돌잔치 + 성년식**: First-birthday party and coming-of-age ceremony both feature traditional 한복. Boys wear blue/black; girls wear pink/red.

## Cross-language Connections

- **Spanish**: Traje de flamenca (flamenco dress) ≈ 한복 — regional identity + festival wear + modern revival. Both garments were everyday wear in earlier centuries, became ceremonial, and now see youth-driven revival.
- **Japanese**: 着物 (kimono) ≈ 한복 — similar two-piece structure (上衣 + skirt), shared history of modernization-era decline. 차이: kimono = T-shape + obi (帯) belt, hanbok = high-waist + goreum ribbon. Kimono has higher contemporary everyday use; hanbok is more festival-focused.
- **Chinese**: 汉服 (Hanfu, hànfú) ≈ 한복 — revived traditional clothing movement, especially among Chinese youth in the 2010s–2020s. Hanfu movement parallels Korean hanbok revival but is more politically charged (Han Chinese identity vs. minority groups).

## Ejemplos

1. **경복궁 한복 체험 (Gyeongbokgung Palace Hanbok Experience)**: Tourists + locals rent 한복 (₩15,000–30,000 for 2 hours) near 경복궁 station. Walk freely in palace grounds while wearing 한복. Free palace entry for hanbok wearers. Popular activity since 2015.
2. **설날 가족 한복 (Lunar New Year Family Hanbok)**: Many Korean families wear matching 한복 for 세배 (sebae, formal bow) on Lunar New Year's morning. Children especially in colorful 한복 for photo + lucky money (세뱃돈, sebaetdon).
3. **현대 일상 한복 (Modern Everyday Hanbok)**: 2020s brands like 뮬리 (Muly), 더한복 (The Hanbok) blend traditional silhouette with modern fabrics — shorter jackets, machine-washable cotton, accessible price points (₩100,000–300,000).
