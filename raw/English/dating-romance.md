# Dating & Romance — Source Material (English)

**Type:** Curriculum + Film dialogues
**Theme:** Flirting, dating, romantic relationships (15+ appropriate)
**Target:** B1-B2 English learners
**Date Ingested:** 2026-06-19

## Overview

Curated reference for romantic/affectionate conversation in English. Sources include
established teaching materials and well-known romantic film/TV dialogue for natural
register cues. The content is **PG-13**: flirtation, dating, mild affection
(hand-holding, first kiss, confession), but no explicit content.

## Categories

### 1. Self-introduction (ice-breakers)

- name, nickname, work, hobby, interests, free time
- "Hi, I'm ___." / "What do you do?" / "What are you into?"

### 2. Compliments (sincere, respectful)

- **Appearance**: "You look great tonight." / "That's a beautiful smile."
- **Personality**: "You're really funny." / "I love how thoughtful you are."
- **Achievement**: "That's really impressive."

### 3. Showing interest

- "Tell me more about ___." / "I'd love to hear about it."
- "What do you usually do on weekends?" / "Any plans for the weekend?"
- "Do you come here often?"

### 4. Asking out

- "Would you like to grab a coffee sometime?"
- "Are you free this Saturday? There's a concert I'd love to check out."
- "I know a great little place — want to try it together?"
- "Could I see you again?"

### 5. During the date

- "I'm having a great time." / "This is really nice."
- "I feel like I've known you forever." / "You're easy to talk to."
- "Should we get the check?" / "Let me take care of it." (offering to pay)

### 6. Affection / progression

- "I really like you." / "I have feelings for you."
- "I've been thinking about you a lot." / "I missed you."
- "Can I hold your hand?" / "Would it be okay if I kissed you?"
- "Will you be my girlfriend / boyfriend?"
- "I love you." (English escalates: like → really like → love)

### 7. Reassurance / support

- "Is everything okay?" / "You seem a bit down."
- "I'm here for you." / "Let me know if you need anything."
- "Don't worry about it." / "We'll figure it out together."

### 8. Polite decline (preserving dignity)

- "I had a lovely time, but I don't think we're a match."
- "I really appreciate it, but I'm seeing someone."
- "Let's just stay friends?" (use carefully — can feel dismissive)

## Cultural Notes (English-speaking contexts)

- **Splitting the bill**: increasingly common in the US/UK. Offering to pay
  is polite, but "let's split it" is also fine. Don't insist.
- **Personal space**: ~45cm (18in) for friends; closer for romantic partners.
- **Eye contact**: 60-70% during conversation signals interest in US; less
  in UK.
- **Saying "I love you"**: typically 2-3 months into a relationship in the US;
  later in the UK. Earlier expressions like "I really like you" are more common.
- **Consent**: explicit verbal consent is now standard, especially for physical
  affection. "Is this okay?" / "Can I ___?" is expected, not awkward.
- **"Let's hang out"**: ambiguous — could be friends or date. Clarify early
  if intent is romantic.
- **Asking via text/DM**: now common for first contact, but a phone call or
  in-person follow-up is more sincere for serious intent.
